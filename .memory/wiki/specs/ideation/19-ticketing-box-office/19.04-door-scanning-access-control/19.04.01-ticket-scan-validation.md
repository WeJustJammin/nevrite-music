# Feature: Ticket Scan & Validation

> **Number**: 19.04.01
> **Parent**: [Door Scanning & Access Control](./19.04-door-scanning-access-control-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Point a device at a barcode; get an unambiguous accept or refuse in under a second, in the dark, with a queue behind you and someone's hand shaking. This is the platform's only physical interface — every other surface in 24 domains is a screen someone chose to look at. The door is the one place WeJammin is in the room, at the moment, whether it works or not.

Validation answers four questions: is this a real ticket, is it for **this** show, has it already been used, and is it still live (not refunded, voided or transferred away).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Sees the scanned count for their show after the fact — how many of the sold tickets actually walked in, and which of their guests came. Never touches a scanner; the door is not theirs. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Scans, sees the accept/refuse verdict and its reason, overrides a refusal when they judge it right, and looks up a ticket by name when the barcode is unreadable or the fan has no phone. |
| Fan | Read-only | Presents a barcode and is admitted or refused. Their entire experience is a green light and a door. When refused, they must be told *why* in words they can act on — "already scanned at 20:14, main door" is actionable; "invalid" starts an argument. |

## Behavior

A scan resolves a barcode to a ticket in the local replica (19.04.02), then applies checks in order: exists → belongs to this show → not voided/refunded (as of last sync) → not already scanned (on this device, or on any device that has synced). The verdict is accept, refuse-with-reason, or accept-with-warning.

Every verdict is recorded locally with a timestamp, device ID and gate, and queued for reconciliation.

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens the scanner for tonight's show; the manifest replica is already downloaded and the scanner states plainly that it is ready.
2. Fan presents a barcode (phone screen, wallet pass, or paper).
3. Scanner reads it and resolves it locally — no network round-trip.
4. Verdict renders in under a second: large, unambiguous, readable in the dark and at arm's length.
5. Accept → the scan is recorded, the scanned count increments, the fan walks in.
6. Verdict is queued for reconciliation; if there is signal it goes now, if not it goes when there is.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Already scanned | The core refusal | Reason with **specifics**: time, gate, device. Without those the Operator cannot tell fraud from a re-entry, and the fan cannot tell whether their friend used their screenshot |
| Ticket for a different show | Common — fans keep old wallet passes | Refuse naming the show the ticket is actually for |
| Ticket refunded after the replica's last sync | Replica says valid; server says refunded | **Admit** — see DT-02. Reconciliation flags it afterwards |
| Barcode unreadable — cracked screen, low brightness, paper in rain | Very common in practice | Name lookup fallback, always available, never buried. See DT-01 |
| Fan has no phone and no paper | Bought online, arrived with nothing | Name lookup against the manifest; identity check is the Operator's judgement |
| Screenshot passed to a friend; both present the same barcode | First scan admits, second refuses | Refusal reason names the first scan's time and gate; the argument is now about facts, not about the machine |
| Rotating/dynamic barcode with no signal on the fan's phone | The pass cannot rotate; a static fallback is required or the ticket is unusable in a basement | See 19.09.02 — this is where anti-fraud and reality collide |
| Comp ticket | Scans identically to a paid ticket | No difference at the door; only the counters differ (19.03.02 D-02) |
| Accessible ticket | Routes to the accessible entrance; the companion scans alongside | See 19.01.05 |
| Scan accepted, then ID check refuses (19.04.04) | The scan must be undone or the count lies | **Scanned-but-refused** must be a representable state; see `19.04-door-scanning-access-control-cx.md#CX-03` |
| Operator overrides a refusal | Permitted — sometimes the machine is wrong and the human is not | Override is recorded with who and why; it is attribution, not prevention (same posture as 19.03.03 D-01) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not ready | Replica not downloaded / stale beyond threshold | Blocking state before doors — "this scanner is not ready" is only useful *before* the queue forms |
| Ready | Replica current, doors not open | Scanner idle with the replica's age shown plainly |
| Scanning | Doors open | Camera live; last verdict persists on screen until the next scan |
| Accept | Valid ticket | Large green verdict; ticket holder name if present |
| Refuse | Any check failed | Large red verdict + specific reason + the fallback action available |
| Warning | Accepted but notable (comp no-show pattern, re-entry, stale replica) | Amber verdict with the reason |
| Offline | No network | Stated plainly and *unalarmingly* — scanning works; only reconciliation is deferred. See DT-03 |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | **Name lookup is a first-class path, not a fallback** | Operator persona works "on a phone at a loading dock"; Fan persona has "no professional stake" and will arrive with a cracked screen, 2% battery, a printed page in the rain, or nothing at all | ✅ CONFIRMED — a meaningful fraction of every real door is resolved by typing a name, and every ticketing system treats it as an escape hatch buried behind a menu. It is not an escape hatch; it is the second-most-used feature at the door. It must be one tap from the scanner, work offline against the same replica, and be as fast as scanning. Burying it does not reduce its use — it just makes the queue longer while the Operator hunts for it. |
| DT-02 | When the local replica and the server disagree, the correct default is **admit and reconcile**, not refuse | Reasoning about the cost asymmetry at the door | ✅ CONFIRMED — the two failure modes are not symmetric. Wrongly refusing a legitimate fan is a public humiliation, an argument, a queue, and a person who paid and cannot get in; wrongly admitting someone whose ticket was refunded eleven minutes ago costs one seat in a room that is probably not full. The door should be generous and the ledger should be honest — admit, record, flag at reconciliation, and let the Operator deal with a report instead of a scene. This inverts the instinct that a validation system exists to say no. |
| DT-03 | The scanner should warn prominently when it is offline | Instinct — offline is a degraded state | ❌ REJECTED — and this one matters for adoption. Offline is the *normal* state in the target venues, not the exception. An alarming offline indicator trains the Operator to distrust a system that is working perfectly, and on a night when everything is fine the scanner is screaming at them. What actually matters is **replica age** ("synced 20 minutes ago") and readiness before doors — a stale replica is a real risk; no signal is not. State it calmly, put the alarm on staleness, and let the door work. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Name lookup is a first-class path, one tap from the scanner, offline-capable | It is the second-most-used door feature and every system buries it (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Default posture on replica/server disagreement is admit-and-reconcile | The cost of wrongly refusing a real fan vastly exceeds the cost of wrongly admitting one (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Refusal reasons are specific — time, gate, device — never "invalid" | Specifics turn an argument into a fact; "invalid" starts one |
| D-04 | Offline is stated calmly; the alarm is on replica staleness, not on connectivity | Offline is the normal state in the target venues; alarming on it trains distrust (DT-03) |
| D-05 | Operator override of a refusal is permitted and recorded | Same attribution-not-prevention posture as 19.03.03 D-01 |
