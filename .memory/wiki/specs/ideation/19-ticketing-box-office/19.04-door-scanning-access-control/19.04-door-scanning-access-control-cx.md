## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Offline Sync ↔ Scan & Validation

**Relationship**: The scanner holds a replica of the manifest downloaded before doors. Every validation is a lookup against that replica plus a local record of scans made on this device. The replica ages: a ticket refunded at 19:50 (19.06.01) is still valid on a scanner that synced at 19:00. So validation is not "is this ticket valid" but "was this ticket valid as of my last sync, and has this device seen it before".

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: sees the replica's age on the scanner and must be able to trust the accept/refuse decision without understanding replication.
- **Fan**: experiences a scan that either works or does not; they cannot be asked to care about sync state.
- **Musician** / **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The manifest is the shared entity; the server holds the authority and each device holds a replica plus local writes. The merge strategy is the sub-domain's hardest problem — see 19.04.02.
2. **Trigger chain**: Sync → replica. Scan → local write → queued for reconciliation. If reconciliation reveals the ticket was refunded after sync, the person is already inside and the compensating action is commercial, not technical.
3. **Permission intersection**: No.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A stale-replica admission that reconciliation rejects must reach the Operator, not silently correct itself in a database.
5. **State transition conflict**: Yes, constantly. Refund, transfer and void all race the replica's age window. Default posture: **admit and reconcile** — see 19.04.01 DT-02.

### CX-02: Offline Sync ↔ Multi-Scanner & Re-Entry

**Relationship**: The double-scan problem. Two doors, two scanners, both offline. A ticket is scanned at door A and again at door B — a copied barcode, a screenshot passed to a friend, or an honest mistake. Neither device can know. Both admit. The conflict materialises only when both reconnect, by which point two people are inside on one ticket and nothing can be undone.

Re-entry policy compounds it: if repeat scans are legitimate (smokers, in-and-out), then a repeat scan is no longer evidence of fraud and the signal is gone.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: owns the consequence — an oversold room is a capacity/licensing problem, not just a revenue one.
- **Fan**: the second scanner's holder is admitted or humiliated depending on timing and luck.
- **Musician** / **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Scan records. Two devices write independently to the same logical ticket. Last-write-wins is meaningless — both writes are real, and the person is already in the room.
2. **Trigger chain**: Reconnect → reconciliation → conflict detection → nothing can be reversed. The output is a *report*, not a fix.
3. **Permission intersection**: No.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Detected double-scans must surface to the Operator during the show if possible, not in the morning.
5. **State transition conflict**: This IS the state transition conflict. See 19.04.02 DT-02 and DT-03.

### CX-03: Scan & Validation ↔ Age & ID Verification

**Relationship**: Two orthogonal admission gates. The scan asks *is this ticket good*; the ID check asks *may this human lawfully be in this room*. A valid ticket held by a 16-year-old at an 18+ show fails the second gate — and then the refund question fires, and the answer depends on whether the age restriction was disclosed at purchase.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: runs both gates; carries the licensing risk personally when the second one is skipped.
- **Fan**: the refused minor. Fan anti-persona explicitly names *"is a minor and hits an age-gated purchase or an adult venue"* as a worst-accidental case.
- **Musician** / **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None — separate decisions on the same admission event.
2. **Trigger chain**: Scan accept → ID check → refuse. A refusal after a successful scan must *undo* the scan, or the count says someone entered who did not. This is a real defect: scanned-but-refused is a state the naive model does not have.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — a ticket grants entry subject to lawful admission; it is not an entitlement that overrides a licence.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Refusal at the door on age grounds should create a refund case automatically (19.06.01), not require the fan to file one while standing outside.
5. **State transition conflict**: See trigger chain — scanned-but-refused must be representable.

### CX-04: Multi-Scanner & Re-Entry ↔ Age & ID Verification

**Relationship**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Confirmed at breadth: once re-entry is permitted, the wristband (not the ticket) becomes the admission token, and it also becomes the evidence that the ID check already passed. Re-checking ID on every re-entry generates a queue for no safety gain; not re-checking makes the wristband a transferable age credential, which is precisely how a 16-year-old gets in.

**Role scoping**: **Operator** carries the licensing risk; **Fan** experiences the friction.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 19.04.01 Scan & Validation | 19.04.02 Offline Sync — as separate concerns | Not a rejection of the pair (CX-01 confirms it) but a rejection of the *merge*: it is tempting to fold offline sync into validation as "validation, but local". Rejected because sync has a lifecycle validation does not — it runs before doors, during, and after reconnect, and it is the only thing in this sub-domain the Operator must actively manage (is my scanner ready?). Merging hides the one operation that determines whether the night works. |
| R-02 | 19.04.04 Age & ID Verification | 19.04.02 Offline Sync | Considered because everything else at the door has an offline story. Rejected: the ID check is a human looking at a document and a face. It requires no data, no replica and no reconnect — it is the one door capability that is perfectly reliable with no network at all. Its offline story is that it does not have one, and that is worth stating rather than assuming. |
