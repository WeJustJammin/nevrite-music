# Feature: RSVP & Free/Private Event Admission

> **Number**: 19.11
> **Parent**: [Ticketing & Box Office](./ticketing-box-office-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Admission with no money rail: a free show, an industry showcase, an album playback, a rehearsal-room open night, a private listening session for twenty people. The room still has a capacity, the door still needs a list, and the count still matters — but nobody pays, so every payment-shaped assumption in this domain is absent.

This is also **the only feature in domain 19 with a real Producer surface**. Everywhere else the session-owner persona is `None`, because producers do not run box offices. But a producer *does* run a playback session for a client's team, an invite-only listening party, a showcase for a mix they just finished — and that is exactly this feature.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | Runs their own free show, showcase or album playback. Creates the event, sets the cap, invites or opens it, and works the door from their phone. No venue required in the loop — a Musician can do this in a rehearsal room. |
| Producer | Full | **The only feature in this domain they can use.** Runs a private playback for a client's team, an invite-only listening session, a showcase for finished work. Small, private, invite-only, no money — the exact shape of everything else this persona does. Elsewhere in domain 19 they are `None`; here they are a primary user. |
| Operator | Full | Runs free nights, showcases and industry events in their room. Same capacity and licensing constraints as any ticketed show, minus the money. |
| Fan | Full | RSVPs to a free show and turns up. One tap; no checkout, no card, no fee — and no ceremony imported from a paid flow that does not apply. |

## Behavior

An RSVP event has a **capacity cap**, an **access mode**, and a **list** — and no money anywhere.

| Access mode | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Anyone can RSVP until the cap |
| **Invite-only** | Named invitees, or a shared link that acts as the key |
| **Private** | Not discoverable at all; reachable only by link |

RSVP issues a real admission record — the same object a ticket is, minus a price — so it scans at the door (19.04), counts in the live count (19.05.01), and appears in the close (19.05.05). Free does not mean uncounted; a fire officer does not care what anyone paid.

**No-show is the defining problem**: free events run 30–60% no-show, which makes the cap almost meaningless as a planning number.

### Happy Path

1. Producer creates a private playback: 20 people, invite-only, Thursday, a studio.
2. Sets the cap and the access mode; the event is not discoverable.
3. Shares the link with the client's team; each person RSVPs with one tap.
4. Admission records issue and are delivered exactly like tickets (19.12).
5. On the night, the Producer checks people in from their phone against the list — or, entirely reasonably, just looks at it (D-04).
6. Count is recorded; the event closes; nobody paid anything and nothing about the flow pretended otherwise.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| 40–60% no-show | The norm for free events, not an anomaly | The cap is nearly useless as a planning number. See DT-01 |
| Overbooked against expected no-show, everyone turns up | People are turned away from a free event they were told they had a place at | The risk of overbooking. Worse than an empty room? See DT-01 |
| Shared invite link leaks | The event fills with strangers | Same trade as 19.02.03's shared codes — often the point, sometimes fatal. For a *private* Producer session it is fatal |
| Free event at capacity, more want in | No money, so no exchange (19.09.03) and no refund path | Waitlist (19.02.05) still applies, and is arguably more useful here |
| RSVP with no admission record issued | A name on a list with no scannable artifact | Same failure as a comp without a barcode (19.03.02 DT-01) — the record must be real |
| Free event needs an age restriction | Free does not mean unlicensed | 19.04.04 applies unchanged; the licence does not care about the price |
| Someone RSVPs for 6 | **Refused — there is no party size.** Free events attract inflated party sizes, and the cap is denominated in heads (D-05): one tap can never claim six. The RSVP link is forwardable, and each head taps it for itself, so every admission record stays keyed to a real Fan identity (D-06, DT-03) | "You can't RSVP for six — send them the link and they'll each get their own pass." |
| Same Fan RSVPs twice (double-tap, two devices, re-opened link) | One human, one admission record — the second tap is idempotent, not a second head against the cap | "You're on the list" — the same record, not a duplicate. See DT-03 |
| Fan RSVPs, then can't come | With 40–60% no-show the norm, a Fan who *tells you* they're out is a gift: their slot should return to the pool | One-tap "can't make it" releases the seat; if a waitlist exists (19.02.05) the next person is offered it. See DT-03 |
| Private event: someone shows at the door not on the list | No admission record exists, so nothing scans and no name matches | Door decides by eye (D-04); for a Producer's 20-person client session this is a judgment call, not a system rule |
| Leaked invite link reaches a minor for an age-gated free event | The link is the key, but the age gate is not the link's job | 19.04.04 age assurance applies at the door regardless of how the RSVP was obtained — free and link-shared does not bypass the licence |
| Event has no venue (a Producer's studio) | Domains 16's licensing and capacity may not apply at all | Step 5 ran and narrowed rather than closed it: a **registered** studio *is* a domain 16 place record (`16` D-06), so an unregistered private room is the residual open case. A genuine gap — this feature can operate outside a venue. Tracked as Q-02 `[PENDING — /create-prd-architecture]` |
| Free show later becomes ticketed | The RSVPs are now competing with a paid manifest | D-02 makes an RSVP a real admission record, so the mechanics exist; whether holders are honoured free, converted, offered priority or voided is a commercial posture. Tracked as Q-04 `[PENDING — /create-prd]` |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Free events should support **deliberate overbooking** against an expected no-show rate, the way airlines do | The 40–60% no-show reality: a 100-cap free show with 100 RSVPs plays to 50 people, which is a bad night for the artist and an empty-looking room | ⏳ **DEFERRED — genuinely unresolved.** The arithmetic is compelling and the failure it fixes is real: a half-empty room at a free show is worse for everyone than a full one. But the downside is not symmetric with an airline's. An airline bumps you and pays you; a free event that overbooks and fills turns people away at a door, for free, after telling them they had a place — and they are standing outside a show their friends are inside. There is no compensation available because they paid nothing, which removes the mechanism that makes airline overbooking tolerable. The honest position is that this needs data (what *is* the no-show rate for this room, this artist, this night?) that WeJammin will not have until it has run events. Recorded as deferred rather than confirmed or rejected: the reasoning is sound, the consequence is unacceptable at the wrong ratio, and the right ratio is unknowable today. Revisit when 22 has real no-show data. |
| DT-03 | RSVP must be **idempotent per Fan and self-releasable** — one head per person, and a one-tap "can't make it" that returns the seat to the pool | The two facts that make free admission behave unlike a paid ticket: nobody is charged, so nothing stops a Fan tapping RSVP three times or handing the link around; and 40–60% no-show (DT-01) means a Fan who *volunteers* that they are out is the single most valuable signal the cap ever gets. A paid ticket has money as the natural de-dup and the natural friction; a free RSVP has neither, so the flow must supply both itself | ✅ CONFIRMED — the admission record is keyed to the Fan's identity (D-11: fans are real users, not CRM rows), so a repeat RSVP resolves to the same record rather than a new head against the cap. Self-release is the cheap half of the overbooking problem DT-01 cannot yet solve: it needs no no-show data and turns no one away — it just recovers seats the holder has freely given back, which is exactly where the waitlist (19.02.05) earns its keep on a free event. Confirmed as the correct default; the harder question of *deliberate* overbooking stays deferred at DT-01. |
| DT-02 | RSVP is a ticket with price = 0, and should reuse the whole ticketing flow | The obvious modelling economy, and it is nearly right | ❌ REJECTED — nearly right is the problem. The admission *record* should indeed be the same object (an RSVP must scan, count and close like a ticket, per D-02), and that half of the hypothesis is correct. But the **flow** must not be reused: a £0 ticket dragged through the paid path inherits a checkout, a fee structure (19.01.03), a refund policy (19.06.01), a price level, an all-in disclosure, and a transfer/resale posture (19.09) — every one of which is meaningless, and every one of which is a surface where something can break or confuse for zero benefit. 19.01.01 D-02 already routes zero-value tickets here for exactly this reason. Same record, different flow: reuse the noun, not the verb. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | No money anywhere — no checkout, no fees, no refunds, no resale | Free means the payment-shaped machinery is absent, not zeroed |
| D-02 | RSVP issues a real admission record that scans, counts and closes like a ticket | A name on a list with no scannable artifact is the same failure as a comp with no barcode (19.03.02 DT-01) |
| D-03 | The record is reused; the flow is not | A £0 ticket through the paid path inherits six meaningless surfaces for zero benefit (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Check-in by eye is a supported implementation | Twenty people in a studio do not need a scanner, and pretending otherwise is ceremony |
| D-05 | The cap counts heads, not RSVPs — and **an RSVP carries no party size**. One tap is one head; a fan bringing friends forwards the link and each taps it for themselves | Free events attract inflated party sizes. Owner-ratified 2026-07-30: a party-size attribute would leave the guests unidentified, which breaks the identity keying D-06 and DT-03 depend on — and the door, the live count and self-release all lean on every admission record resolving to a real Fan. The beachhead cases are named-invitee shaped anyway (a 20-person client playback, an invite-only listening party). If groups are ever wanted on Open free events, adopt `19.03.01` D-05's ratified admission-unit model verbatim — one body, one barcode, entry spend atomic — rather than inventing a party-size attribute; `19.03.01` DT-03 has already rejected the ride-free alternative |
| D-06 | RSVP is idempotent per Fan and self-releasable — one head per person, one-tap "can't make it" returns the seat | Free admission has neither money as a de-dup nor money as friction; the flow must supply both, and self-release is the only half of the overbooking problem (DT-01) solvable without no-show data (DT-03) |
