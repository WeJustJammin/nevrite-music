# Feature: Event Cancellation & Mass Refund Cascade

> **Number**: 19.06.02
> **Parent**: [Refunds, Cancellations & Rescheduling](./19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

The show is off. The singer lost their voice, the venue flooded, the tour collapsed, the promoter pulled it. Everyone who bought gets their money back — not as a policy decision, not as a request, but as an obligation the seller incurred the moment they cancelled.

This is one decision with N executions, and the distinction from N individual refunds is not pedantry: an individual refund may fail and be retried in isolation with nobody harmed, whereas 400 refunds of which 6 fail is **six people who are owed money and do not know that anyone knows**.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Usually the cause, always a party to the decision, and the one whose relationship with the fans takes the damage. Can initiate the cancellation conversation and must confirm it. Does not execute it and does not fund the refunds — the money owed to buyers is the seller's obligation. Sees the settlement consequence, which is where the deal's cancellation terms (domain 17) land on them. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Executes: cancels, voids every ticket, funds and drives the refund cascade to completion, and answers for it if it does not complete. |
| Fan | Read-only | Is told the show is cancelled and that their money is coming back, with a date. They apply for nothing. The message must lead with the refund, because that is the only question they have. |

## Behavior

Cancellation is irreversible and unconditional. It voids every ticket — paid, comp, VIP package, RSVP — and creates a **refund obligation** per paid ticket that must be discharged. There is no policy check: the ordinary refund policy is irrelevant when the seller is the one cancelling.

The cascade must be a **tracked obligation with a completion guarantee**, not a queue that drains. Its success condition is "every buyer has their money", not "every job was enqueued".

Downstream, cancellation reaches further than the fans: the settlement reverses (17), the waitlist closes (19.02.05), VIP packages with physical components may already have shipped (19.08.03), and the show's whole promotional apparatus (21) is pointing at something that no longer exists.

### Happy Path

1. Musician and Operator agree the show is off; Operator initiates cancellation with a reason.
2. System states the blast radius **before** confirming: 340 paid tickets, £5,100 to return, 22 comps, 4 VIP packages of which 2 have shipped, 180 on the waitlist.
3. Operator confirms. The show is cancelled — irreversibly.
4. Every ticket voids. The refund obligation is created, per ticket, tracked.
5. Every holder is notified: the show is cancelled, your money is coming back, by [date], you need do nothing.
6. Refunds execute against the rail; the obligation tracks each to completion.
7. Failures surface as incidents against a named person, not as retries in a log.
8. Settlement reverses (17); the waitlist is notified and closed; promo (21) is stood down.
9. Obligation reaches zero. Only then is the cancellation complete.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Partial failure** — 6 of 400 refunds fail at the rail | The defining failure — six people owed money in a system that thinks it succeeded | Six named incidents (name + amount + ticket) held open until discharged; the cancellation cannot reach Discharged while any remain. Never a drained queue. See DT-01 |
| **Dead original payment method** — expired card, closed account (rail returns a hard decline, e.g. `card_expired` / `account_closed`) | Common months after purchase; the money cannot go back the way it came, so the obligation does **not** clear. System retries the original method 3× over 72h (idempotent per ticket); on continued hard-decline it escalates to a named incident and opens an alternative-payout path. The obligation stays open. | The fan is proactively contacted for an alternative destination — never told "your card expired, therefore we keep your money", which is a forbidden terminal state (D-07). *Which* alternative rail is offered (store credit vs. bank transfer vs. manual payout) is a commercial decision → Q-01 |
| **Cancelled after the box office closed** — a show that already happened | Not coherent — a show cannot be cancelled after it happened; post-close corrections are settlement adjustments (19.05.05), not cancellations | Cancellation control absent post-close; a direct request returns `409 {error:"box_office_closed", code:"CANCEL_AFTER_CLOSE"}` |
| **Race** — an individual refund (19.06.01) is in flight when cancellation fires | Double-refund risk — real money, both directions | Each ticket carries one refund-obligation key; cancellation claims it atomically. If an individual refund already holds it, cancellation observes the discharged/in-progress state and does not re-issue; the loser of the race returns `409 {error:"refund_already_in_progress", code:"REFUND_INFLIGHT"}` and defers. No double refund (D-10). `#CX-03` Q5 |
| **Concurrency** — two Operators confirm cancellation of the same show at once | Only one cancellation may exist | First confirm wins and transitions Live→Cancelled atomically, creating exactly one obligation set; the second returns `409 {error:"already_cancelled", code:"EVENT_ALREADY_CANCELLED"}` and creates no second set |
| **Empty** — show cancelled with zero paid tickets sold | Nothing to discharge; the obligation set is empty | Blast-radius screen states "0 paid tickets, £0 to return"; comps/RSVPs and the waitlist are still voided and notified; cancellation reaches Discharged immediately (0 of 0) |
| **Permission** — a Musician-only identity attempts to execute the cancellation | Musician is Config, not Full (Role Matrix): they initiate/propose and confirm as a party, but execute-and-fund is Operator-only | The execute action is refused `403 {error:"operator_required", code:"CANCEL_REQUIRES_OPERATOR"}`; the artist keeps the initiate/confirm surface, not the disbursement one |
| **Permission** — a Producer or Fan reaches the cancel surface | Producer None, Fan Read-only | The control is absent, not merely disabled; a crafted direct request returns `403 {error:"forbidden", code:"CANCEL_FORBIDDEN"}` |
| **Multi-hyphenate in two roles** — the Operator is also the artist on the bill | One human holds both Musician (cause) and Operator (executor) | No second human is forced, but both acts still occur under that identity: the decision-as-cause and the execute-and-fund. The settlement consequence (17) still lands on the artist side of the same identity. No self-approval loophole exists because there is no approval gate to bypass — the only gate is the irreversible blast-radius confirm, which applies to every identity |
| **Notification fan-out partially fails** (network failure) | Some cancellation notices bounce or fail to send; a holder who is not told holds "a right nobody knows they have" — worse than a late refund | Failed notices are tracked per-recipient and retried; a holder whose refund discharged but whose notification never delivered is a **separate** open notification incident, distinct from the money obligation |
| **Rail unreachable for the whole cascade** (network failure) | The payments rail is down when the cascade begins | Cancellation still commits — it is irreversible and decoupled from disbursement (D-08). The obligation set is created and parked as undischarged; refunds begin/resume when the rail returns. Cancellation is never blocked on the rail, and is never marked Discharged until the money actually moves |
| **Malicious cancellation** (malicious input) — compromised Operator account, or an insider griefing a show / attempting mass outbound refund theft | Refunds only ever return to the **original** payment method (D-07), never an arbitrary destination, so a mass refund cannot be redirected to steal funds; the damage is denial-of-show, not theft | The irreversible act is logged against the acting identity and feeds 24; step-up auth on the blast-radius confirm is the mitigation → Q-05 |
| **Externally-ticketed show** (19.07) | WeJammin never held the money and cannot return it | Notification is possible; refund is not. Whether a notify-only cancellation is offered (and whether it over-promises) is Q-02 |
| **VIP package with shipped merchandise** (19.08.03) | The fan has the t-shirt and is getting a refund | The package price decomposes into a **ticket component** and a **physical-goods component**. The ticket component is refunded **unconditionally** — it is the cancellation obligation and is never blocked or delayed on the merch question (D-09). The physical-goods component's treatment (full refund with no recovery, refund minus shipped value, or ticket-component-only) is a commercial decision → Q-03 |
| **Comps on a cancelled show** | No money, but the guests still planned an evening | Notified; nothing to refund |
| **Waitlist members on a cancelled show** | Never bought; nothing owed; still need telling | Notified and closed (19.02.05) |
| **Cancelled during the on-sale, mid-cart** | Someone is holding a cart, or is mid-checkout, for a show that no longer exists | Active carts holding inventory are invalidated; an in-flight checkout that has **not** captured payment is rejected `409 {error:"event_cancelled", code:"EVENT_CANCELLED"}`; a checkout that captured within the cancellation window becomes a paid ticket and joins the refund obligation like any other (never a stranded charge) |
| **Deposit already paid by the venue to the artist** (17) | The refund obligation to fans and the deal's cancellation terms are separate money | Deal terms live in 17; who bears the cost is parent Q-04 |
| **Repeated cancellations by the same artist** | A trust signal, not just an operational event | Feeds domain 24; undischarged obligations from prior cancellations are also a dispute source |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Live | Show on sale or upcoming | Cancellation available to the Operator |
| Confirming | Cancellation initiated | **Blast radius stated before the point of no return** — tickets, money, comps, shipped packages, waitlist |
| Cancelled | Confirmed | Irreversible. Show is off; tickets void; storefront states it plainly |
| Cascading | Refund obligation executing | Operator sees progress against the obligation: N of M discharged |
| Incidents | Refunds failed | Named people, named amounts, until discharged |
| Discharged | Every refund complete | Only now is the cancellation finished |
| Reversed (settlement) | Settlement rolled back in 17 | Statement/settlement consequence recorded |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The cascade is a **tracked obligation with a completion guarantee**, not a job queue | Problem statement — the platform exists because facts about money evaporate and are reconstructed later; a silently-failed refund is exactly that failure, with the fan's money inside it | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is the difference between a feature and a liability. A queue's success condition is "the job ran"; an obligation's is "the person has their money". Those come apart precisely in the cases that matter: an expired card, a closed account, a rail error at 3am. In a queue model these become log lines that nobody reads, and the fan discovers months later — or does not, and the platform has quietly kept money for a show that did not happen. That is not a bug, it is the thing regulators and journalists write about. Modelling the cascade as N tracked obligations means the system cannot forget: an undischarged obligation is a standing incident with a name attached to it until someone resolves it. The cost is that you can never mark a cancellation "done" while a single person is unpaid — which is the correct definition of done. |
| DT-02 | Cancellation is a mass application of the individual refund path — loop over the tickets and call refund | The obvious implementation, and the outcome does look the same | ❌ REJECTED, and it inherits exactly the wrong semantics. The individual path (19.06.01) is built around *discretion*: policy checks, granted/excluded/discretionary shapes, an Operator decision, per-case failure that harms nobody else. Cancellation has none of that — there is no policy to check (the seller cancelled, so the policy is irrelevant), no decision to make, no case to open, and failure is not isolated because a partial mass refund is a single incident with N victims. Looping the individual path would run 400 policy evaluations that all resolve the same way, could produce 400 discretionary cases if the policy is restrictive, and would treat six failures as six unrelated retries rather than one incident. Same rail, different obligation. |
| DT-03 | The cancellation commit must be **decoupled** from refund disbursement — the show goes off the instant the Operator confirms, and the money moves on its own schedule | The rail is an external dependency that can be down, slow, or rate-limited exactly when a 400-ticket cascade hits it; and the two facts ("this show is cancelled" and "this buyer has been paid") have genuinely different truth conditions | ✅ CONFIRMED. If commit and disbursement were coupled, a rail outage would leave the Operator unable to cancel a show that is objectively off — the singer has lost their voice whether or not the payments rail is answering. Coupling would also make the irreversible act (cancellation) hostage to a flaky one (a card network at 3am), which inverts the risk: the safe, must-succeed action would depend on the unreliable one. Decoupling means cancellation always commits (irreversible, storefront states it plainly), the obligation set is created and can be *parked* undischarged through an outage, and the cascade drains against the rail whenever it is reachable. The cost is that "cancelled" and "everyone paid" are two distinct states the Operator must be able to see apart — which is exactly the Cascading→Discharged distinction the States table already carries, and is honest rather than a leak. |
| DT-04 | Refunds must only ever return to the buyer's **original** payment method — never an operator-supplied or request-supplied destination | The cascade moves real money outbound at scale, which is precisely the shape an attacker wants to hijack, and a compromised Operator account is a realistic threat | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is a security invariant, not a convenience. If a refund could be directed to an arbitrary destination, a single compromised Operator (or a malicious insider) could cancel a show and redirect 400 refunds to attacker-controlled methods — turning the safety mechanism into a theft primitive. Pinning every refund to the original method means the worst a hijacked cancellation can do is deny a show (recoverable, visible, feeds 24), never drain funds to a stranger. The trade-off is the dead-method case (expired card, closed account), which cannot go back the way it came — but the answer there is a *tracked, contacted, consent-based* alternative-payout incident (D-07, Q-01), not a silent redirect. The invariant holds; the exception is handled explicitly and never silently. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The cascade is a tracked obligation per ticket with a completion guarantee | A queue's success condition is "the job ran"; the fan's is "I have my money". They come apart exactly where it matters (DT-01) |
| D-02 | A cancellation is not complete while any refund is undischarged | The only honest definition of done |
| D-03 | Cancellation is its own operation, not a loop over the individual refund path | The individual path is built around discretion, policy and isolated failure — none of which apply (DT-02) |
| D-04 | The blast radius is stated before the point of no return | Tickets, money, comps, shipped packages and waitlist — cancellation is irreversible and the Operator should see what they are about to do |
| D-05 | The fan applies for nothing; the notification leads with the refund | It is the only question they have |
| D-06 | Refund obligation and the deal's cancellation terms are separate money | What fans are owed and who bears it are different questions; the second lives in domain 17 |
| D-07 | Refunds return only to the buyer's original payment method; a dead method escalates to a tracked, contacted alternative-payout incident, never a silent keep | A security invariant against redirect-theft (DT-04); "your card expired, therefore we keep your money" is a forbidden terminal state |
| D-08 | The cancellation commit is decoupled from refund disbursement — commit is irreversible and never blocked on the rail | A must-succeed action cannot be hostage to a flaky external dependency (DT-03); Cascading and Discharged are distinct observable states |
| D-09 | The ticket component of a VIP package is refunded unconditionally as part of the obligation; only the physical-goods component's treatment is open | The refund of what a buyer paid for a cancelled ticket is never blocked on the merch question (Q-03) |
| D-10 | Double-refund is prevented by a single per-ticket refund-obligation key claimed atomically | The individual-refund-in-flight race is real money in both directions; idempotency per ticket is the guarantee (`#CX-03`) |
