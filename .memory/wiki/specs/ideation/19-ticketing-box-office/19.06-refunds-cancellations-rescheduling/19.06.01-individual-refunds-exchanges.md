# Feature: Individual Refunds & Exchanges

> **Number**: 19.06.01
> **Parent**: [Refunds, Cancellations & Rescheduling](./19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

One buyer, one ticket, one change of circumstance: they cannot come, they bought the wrong night, they were refused at the door on age grounds (19.04.04), or they simply want their money back. This feature owns the request, the policy decision, the money movement, and — critically — what happens to the seat.

Live events are frequently **exempt** from the statutory right to cancel that covers ordinary distance selling, which means the refund policy here is a genuine commercial choice rather than a compliance readout. That makes the *values* of the policy (is there a window, are fees refundable, is there a right at all) a product decision the owner must make (parent Q-01, this file's Q-01/Q-02). The *behavior* of the engine that executes whatever policy is chosen is fully specified here.

The policy the engine executes is a per-show configuration the Operator sets, with these fields (values are the Operator's, the fields are fixed): a **refund cutoff** (e.g. "up to 48h before doors", or "none"); a **cooling-off period** (e.g. "24h after purchase, unless the show is sooner"); a **set of refundable ticket types** (GA yes, VIP no, comp N/A); and a **`feesRefundable` flag** (whether the booking fee returns with the face value). Every fan sees this policy stated plainly *before* they ask (D-06).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible as a box-office actor. A fan's individual refund is between the buyer and the seller, and the seller is the Operator; the artist sees only the aggregate in the count (19.05.01), never the individual case. **Multi-hyphenate note**: a Musician who *bought a ticket as a fan* to another artist's show acts through the **Fan** lens for that ticket — the persona is the buying context, not the account (D-19). They get no artist-side visibility into a show they merely bought into. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface, no refund role in any context. |
| Operator | Full | Sets the per-show refund policy; sees the discretionary queue (never the granted or excluded cases — those never reach a human, D-02); approves or refuses discretionary cases within the 72-hour answer-by deadline (D-07); funds every refund from the show's proceeds. Sees released inventory return to the manifest and route to the waitlist. **Multi-hyphenate note**: an Operator refunding *their own ticket to their own show* is the one self-serving case — see the edge-case table (self-refund is allowed but logged, because the Operator both grants and receives). |
| Fan | Full | Requests a refund and is told the answer, in the same view, with the reason and the exact money breakdown, before confirming. Where the policy grants it automatically, they should not have to *request* anything (D-01) — they tap "Refund" and it is done. Where the policy excludes it, they are offered the exchange path *before* the refusal (D-03). Where it is discretionary, they are told it is with the venue and by when they will hear (D-07). |

## Behavior

A refund request resolves against the show's policy the instant it is made. Three shapes exist and they must not be confused (D-02):

- **Policy-granted** — the case is inside the policy (cooling-off period active, a refundable ticket type before the cutoff, or an opt-out window opened by a reschedule per `#CX-01`). Automatic; no human, no queue, no case. The fan taps and it processes.
- **Policy-excluded** — outside the policy. Refused immediately, with the reason, and with the **exchange offered first** (D-03).
- **Discretionary** — outside the policy but the Operator may say yes anyway (an age refusal at the door, a bereavement, a genuine mistake). A real decision by a real person, carrying an **answer-by deadline of 72 hours from case open** (D-07), clamped to the show's doors when doors fall sooner than 72 hours so a case that could still admit the fan resolves before it is moot. The Operator may tighten the 72-hour default per show but never remove it.

**Exchange** — swapping to another date or tier — is a distinct all-or-nothing outcome (D-03), not refund-then-rebuy (DT-02): it either completes in full — old ticket voided, new ticket held, price difference settled — or nothing changes and the fan keeps the seat they had, with no inventory ever exposed to the pool in between. It is often better for everyone: the fan still comes, the venue keeps the money, the seat is not stranded. Price differences settle to the fan's payment method: up-tier the fan pays the delta, down-tier the delta is refunded under the same `feesRefundable` rule, same-price no money moves (D-08).

**Money movement** guarantees at most one refund can ever attach to a ticket (D-09), so a double-tap, a retry after a payment-provider timeout, or a cancellation (19.06.02) firing against a ticket that already has a refund in flight cannot pay twice — a duplicate attempt returns the first result, never a second payout. The money returns to the **original payment method** used at purchase; where that instrument is dead, the case is escalated, never silently dropped (see edge cases; parent 19.06.02 Q-01 owns the alternate-path decision).

Released inventory routes to the waitlist first (19.02.05 D-01), never straight back to public sale, so a bot cannot take a seat a waiting fan should have (D-04).

### Happy Path

1. Fan opens their ticket (19.12) and taps "Refund". The refund option was already visible with the policy stated plainly (D-06).
2. System resolves the policy in-view and states the answer with the reason and an exact breakdown: face value £30.00, booking fee £3.50, refund total £33.50 (or £30.00 if `feesRefundable` is false), arriving in 3–5 business days on the card ending 4242.
3. Policy-granted → fan confirms → refund processes against the original payment method; the ticket voids immediately; done — no queue, no case, no waiting.
4. Inventory returns to the manifest and is offered to the waitlist as a time-boxed private claim (19.02.05).
5. The live count (19.05.01) decrements; the movement appears in the next drop (19.05.02) as an explicit negative, not a silent adjustment.
6. Where the policy does **not** grant it, before the refusal the fan is offered the exchange path (another date or tier), because a fan who changes date is worth more than a fan who is refused (D-03).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Refund requested on a ticket already scanned (19.04.01) | They attended and want their money back | Refused. The scan record is the evidence — this is the one place the scan does commercial work (D-05). Fan sees "This ticket was used to enter on [date]; it can't be refunded." No discretionary route. |
| Refund processed after the offline door replica synced; fan turns up and is admitted (19.04.01 D-02) | They got in on a refunded ticket | Admitted at the door, then reconciliation flags it (19.04.02); the money conversation is the Operator's. Accepted cost of admit-and-reconcile — the door never blocks on a live refund lookup. |
| **Are fees refundable?** | Behavior: the refund amount is computed from the show's `feesRefundable` flag and the exact breakdown is shown before the fan confirms — no post-hoc surprise. | Fan sees face-value and fee as separate lines and the total that will actually arrive. The *default value* of the flag, and whether keeping fees is permissible at all, is the values decision — **Q-01**. |
| **Refund on a transferred ticket** (19.09.02) — payer ≠ holder | Only the **current holder** may request (they hold the valid ticket; the payer gave it away and cannot claw it back). If granted, the ticket voids and the money returns to the **original payer's** instrument, because a refund reverses the original charge. The holder receives no money from WeJammin. | Before confirming, the holder sees: "This ticket was transferred to you. A refund returns £33.50 to the original purchaser, not to you, and voids your ticket." They must acknowledge. Both parties are notified. Resolves prior Q-03 → **D-10**. |
| Holder paid the transferrer *off-platform* for a transferred ticket, then refunds | WeJammin only ever held the original purchase money; it cannot reverse a side-payment it never saw | Same as above — the payer is refunded, the holder is out of pocket to the transferrer and must settle privately. This is exactly why face-value resale (19.09.03) is the cleaner path than free transfer + refund. |
| Age refusal at the door (19.04.04 D-01) | A discretionary refund case opens **automatically**; the fan does not file anything | The case is system-opened and presumed toward grant; at the 72-hour deadline it **auto-grants** (D-07) — the fan is on a pavement and did nothing wrong. |
| Discretionary case, Operator never responds | Fan-initiated discretionary request left unactioned past the 72-hour deadline | **System-opened** cases (e.g. door refusal) auto-grant at the deadline; **fan-initiated** discretionary requests auto-refuse with the reason "no exception granted" and a one-tap route to open a dispute (24). Silence is never a silent yes on money the policy already excluded (D-07). |
| Two refund requests on the same ticket at once (double-tap, two devices) | Only one refund can attach to the ticket; the second collapses into a no-op that returns the first result | Second request returns "already refunded" with the same confirmation, not a second payout and not an error the fan must resolve (D-09). |
| Refund fires while a **cancellation** (19.06.02) is cascading against the same ticket | Double-refund risk — real money, both directions | The at-most-one-refund-per-ticket guarantee resolves it: whichever lands first wins; the other is a no-op. A money-safety race settled by the single-refund guarantee, not by locking the fan out (`#CX-03`, D-09). |
| Refund requested during a reschedule's opt-out window (`#CX-01`) | The opt-out right overrides ordinary policy for its duration | Granted as policy-granted even if the ordinary policy would refuse — the seller moved the date, so "no refunds" does not survive it. |
| Payment-provider timeout — request sent, no acknowledgement | Ambiguous: did the money move? | The refund is **not** retried blindly. The ticket stays in Processing; because at most one refund can attach, a single safe retry can confirm-or-complete without risking a double-send. The fan sees "Your refund is processing" — never a silent double-send and never a silent drop. |
| Refund fails hard at the payment provider (declined, closed account) | The fan is owed money and may not know | A **tracked incident** with a completion guarantee (not a silent retry log): the fan is proactively told, and the case escalates to the alternate-path question (parent 19.06.02 Q-01) rather than the platform quietly keeping the money. |
| Original payment method dead months later (expired card, closed account) | Common; the money cannot go back the way it came | Escalates to the alternate-path flow — "we can't refund to the card ending 4242; here's how to give us where to send it." The fan is never told "your card expired, so we keep your money." Alternate-path mechanics are parent 19.06.02 Q-01. |
| Partial refund on a multi-ticket order (bought 4, returns 1) | Per-ticket refund; the other 3 stay valid | Works; on reserved seating it may scatter the remaining group (seats 4/5/6/7 → 4/5/7). Fan is warned the group is no longer contiguous before confirming. |
| Exchange to a **more expensive** tier | The delta is charged as part of the all-or-nothing exchange | Fan sees "New ticket £45, you paid £30, £15 due now" and pays in the same flow; no seat is released in between (D-08). |
| Exchange to a **cheaper** tier | The delta is refunded under the same `feesRefundable` rule | Fan sees "New ticket £20, you paid £30, £10 back to your card"; fee treatment matches the show policy (D-08). |
| Exchange target is sold out by the time the fan confirms | The all-or-nothing exchange cannot complete against gone inventory | Swap fails cleanly with the original ticket untouched — the fan still holds the seat they had. They are offered the waitlist for the target (19.02.05) or a plain refund if eligible. No money moved, no seat lost. |
| Fan refunds, the show then sells out, then they want back in | Their own choice | Waitlist like anyone else (19.02.05); no special reinstatement, no held seat. |
| Malicious: fan claims non-attendance to get a discretionary refund but was scanned in | Fraudulent discretionary claim | The scan record refuses it automatically before it reaches the Operator (D-05); it never becomes a discretionary case. |
| Abuse: fan refunds-and-rebuys repeatedly, or refunds every show at the last legal minute ("abuses refunds" anti-persona) | Each individual refund is *within policy*, so it is granted (D-01) — we do not silently deny what the policy allows | The refunds are granted, but a per-fan refund-rate signal feeds Trust & Safety (24); the Operator may tighten the show's policy, but no single in-policy refund is blocked mid-flight (DT-06). |
| Chargeback filed while a refund is pending | Fan disputes with their bank and also has a refund in flight — double-recovery risk | The single-refund guarantee and the chargebacks cross-cut (parent D-02) reconcile so the fan is made whole exactly once; the scan record (if any) is this domain's evidence contribution to the bank process. |
| Operator self-refunds their own ticket to their own show (multi-hyphenate) | Allowed — an Operator can be a Fan of their own event | Processed as an ordinary policy-granted refund but **logged with both roles named**, because the same human grants and receives; feeds the same 24 signal so it cannot be a quiet leak. |
| Show has **no refund policy configured** | Operator never set one | The engine cannot invent a policy. The refund control shows "This organiser hasn't published a refund policy" and routes the fan to a discretionary request (the only safe default is a human decision, never a silent auto-refuse or auto-grant). Flagged to the Operator to configure. |
| Fan has no refundable ticket / the show already happened and was not attended | Empty case | The refund control is absent or disabled with the reason ("This show has passed"); there is nothing to request against. |
| Externally-ticketed show (19.07) | WeJammin never held the money | It cannot refund what it never took. The WeJammin refund control is absent; the fan sees the external ticketer's policy and a link out. Whether to *broker* the refund instead is the values decision — **Q-02** (parent Q-02). |
| Refund after box office close (19.05.05) | The certified statement is now historically true, currently false | Correct and expected. Post-close refunds are **settlement adjustments**, never edits to the certified statement (19.05.05 D-06). |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not requested | Fan holds a valid, unscanned ticket | Refund option visible with the policy and the exact refundable amount stated plainly *before* they ask (D-06) |
| Resolving | Fan taps "Refund" | In-view resolution of policy shape (granted / excluded / discretionary) in under 500ms — no separate page; a spinner appears only if resolution exceeds 500ms |
| Auto-granted | Request inside policy or inside an opt-out window | Immediate confirmation with the money breakdown; no queue, no case, no waiting |
| Refused | Request outside policy | Immediate answer with the reason **and the exchange alternative offered first** (D-03) |
| Pending decision | Discretionary case opened | Fan sees it is with the venue and the answer-by date (72h from open, sooner if doors are sooner); system-opened cases show they are presumed toward grant |
| Approved | Operator granted, or auto-grant at the 72h deadline on a system-opened case | Refund entering processing |
| Processing | Money moving to the payment method | Expected arrival window (3–5 business days) and the destination instrument |
| Refunded | Payment provider confirmed | Ticket void; inventory returned to manifest → waitlist; count decremented |
| Exchanged | All-or-nothing exchange complete | New ticket issued; delta charged/refunded/none per the price difference (D-08); no seat released in between |
| Failed | Hard payment-provider failure | Tracked incident; the fan is proactively told and routed to the alternate-path flow — never left to discover it |
| Escalated | Dead instrument, or fan-initiated discretionary auto-refused | Fan sees the next step: supply an alternate destination, or open a dispute (24) |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Where the policy grants a refund, there should be **no request** — the fan taps refund and gets it | Fan persona — *"the only persona with no professional stake… will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"*; the Fan's switching trigger is friction, and their anti-persona *"abuses refunds"*, which a policy engine handles better than a human queue | ✅ CONFIRMED — a "request" implies a decision, and where the policy already decided, manufacturing a decision is theatre with a queue attached. Every incumbent routes policy-granted refunds through a support form, which produces a fan who waits three days for an answer the system knew instantly, an Operator processing a decision that was not theirs to make, and a support cost on a case with no content. Splitting the three shapes at the front means the Operator only ever sees the third — the cases that genuinely need a human — and the fan gets an instant answer in the other two. The discretionary queue then stays small enough to actually be answered, which is what makes the good-faith cases (the refused minor, the bereavement) work. |
| DT-02 | Exchange is a variant of refund — refund the old, sell the new | Modelling economy; the outcome looks the same | ❌ REJECTED — and it is a commercially expensive conflation. Refund-then-rebuy releases the seat to the pool, takes the money out, and requires the fan to complete a second purchase against inventory that may be gone by the time the first refund clears. Every step is a chance to lose them, and on a sold-out show the exchange is simply impossible even though the swap was trivially available. An exchange is a *single all-or-nothing operation*: it completes in full or nothing changes, the fan holds a different ticket, no inventory is exposed to the pool in between, and only a price *delta* moves. It is also the outcome the Operator most wants, which means it should be offered *before* the refusal, not discovered afterwards. Different mechanics, different money, different result — not a variant. |
| DT-03 | A discretionary case can sit open indefinitely — the Operator gets to it when they get to it | The obvious "queue" model; discretion has no clock | ❌ REJECTED — an open-ended discretionary case is the same domain anti-pattern the reschedule feature already proved conclusive (19.06.03 DT-01): a party holds something that belongs by default to someone else, the incentive is to hold it, and the unbounded state is the failure. A fan refused at the door and waiting on a "maybe" is holding a non-answer while their money sits with the seller. So every discretionary case carries an **answer-by deadline of 72 hours from case open** (clamped to the show's doors when doors are sooner), and the deadline *resolves differently by who opened it*: a **system-opened** case (age refusal, 19.04.04) auto-**grants** at the deadline because the fan did nothing wrong and is owed; a **fan-initiated** case auto-**refuses** at the deadline (the policy already excluded it) but hands the fan a one-tap route to a dispute (24). Auto-refunding every ignored request would be an abuse magnet; auto-refusing the door-refused minor would be cruelty. The split by origin is what makes a single deadline fair in both directions → D-07. |
| DT-04 | Refund money-movement can be fire-and-forget: send it to the rail and trust it | Standard "call the payment API" thinking | ❌ REJECTED — this is precisely the failure the whole platform exists to prevent (problem statement: facts about money evaporate and are reconstructed later). Fire-and-forget breaks in the exact cases that matter: a double-tap pays twice; a payment-provider timeout leaves the fan not knowing if the money moved; a cancellation firing against an in-flight individual refund double-pays; a hard decline vanishes into a log nobody reads. The fix is an **at-most-one-refund-per-ticket guarantee** so exactly one refund can ever attach to a ticket, plus **tracked completion** so a failure is a standing incident with the fan's name on it rather than a silent drop. The cost — you can never call a refund "done" until the money is confirmed home — is the correct definition of done → D-09. |
| DT-05 | The seat released by a refund should go straight back to public sale to recoup fast | Revenue-recovery instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a seat dropped back to open sale is a seat a bot can grab in milliseconds, ahead of a human who has been waiting for it. Refunded inventory routes to the **waitlist first** as a time-boxed private claim (19.02.05 D-01) before public sale. This is the same fairness rule the whole domain applies to freed inventory, and breaking it here to recoup a few minutes faster would hand the seat to exactly the actor (the scalper bot) the platform is built against → D-04. |
| DT-06 | Because the Fan anti-persona "abuses refunds", the engine should silently deny suspicious refunds | Anti-persona defence instinct | ❌ REJECTED — silently denying a refund the *policy grants* breaks D-01 and turns an instant, honest interaction into the opaque support maze the feature exists to kill. A refund that is inside policy is granted, full stop, even to a serial refunder — the policy is the contract. Abuse is handled *out of band*: a per-fan refund-rate signal feeds Trust & Safety (24), and the Operator can tighten the *policy* for future sales, but no single in-policy refund is blocked mid-flight on suspicion. Defence belongs at the policy and trust layers, never as a silent exception in the money path → the abuse row and the 24 signal. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Policy-granted refunds are automatic — no request, no queue, no case | Manufacturing a decision the policy already made is theatre with a support cost (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Three shapes are distinguished at the front: granted, excluded, discretionary; only discretionary reaches a human | Keeps the human queue small enough that the good-faith cases actually get answered (DT-01) |
| D-03 | Exchange is an all-or-nothing operation (completes in full or nothing changes), not refund-then-rebuy, and is offered before a refusal | Refund-then-rebuy exposes the seat and loses the fan; on a sold-out show it makes a trivial swap impossible (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Refunded and exchange-released inventory routes to the waitlist before public sale | Consistent with 19.02.05 D-01 — otherwise a bot gets the seat a waiting fan should have (DT-05) |
| D-05 | A scanned ticket cannot be refunded, and a false non-attendance claim is defeated by the scan record | The scan is evidence of attendance; this is the one place the scan record does commercial work |
| D-06 | Refund policy and the exact refundable amount are stated before the fan asks, not after | A policy discovered at the point of refusal is a policy designed to be discovered too late |
| D-07 | Every discretionary case carries an answer-by deadline of 72 hours from case open (clamped to doors when sooner); **system-opened** cases auto-grant at the deadline, **fan-initiated** cases auto-refuse with a dispute route | An unbounded discretionary case is the domain's characteristic failure; resolving by origin makes one deadline fair both ways (DT-03) |
| D-08 | Exchange price differences settle to the payment method as part of one all-or-nothing exchange: up-tier charges the delta, down-tier refunds it under `feesRefundable`, same-price moves nothing | The swap must complete in full or not at all, with no seat released in between (DT-02) |
| D-09 | At most one refund can ever attach to a ticket, and each refund is a tracked obligation until the money is confirmed home | Prevents double-refund on double-tap, retry, or a cancellation racing an in-flight refund; a failure is a standing incident, not a silent drop (DT-04, `#CX-03`) |
| D-10 | On a transferred ticket only the current holder may request a refund; the money returns to the original payer's instrument, and the holder must acknowledge this before confirming | The valid ticket belongs to the holder, but a refund reverses the original charge, which was the payer's — resolves prior Q-03 |
