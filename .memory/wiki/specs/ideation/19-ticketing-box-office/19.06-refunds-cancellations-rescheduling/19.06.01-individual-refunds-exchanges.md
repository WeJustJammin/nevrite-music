# Feature: Individual Refunds & Exchanges

> **Number**: 19.06.01
> **Parent**: [Refunds, Cancellations & Rescheduling](./19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

One buyer, one ticket, one change of circumstance: they cannot come, they bought the wrong night, they were refused at the door on age grounds (19.04.04), or they simply want their money back. This feature owns the request, the policy decision, the money movement, and — critically — what happens to the seat.

Live events are frequently **exempt** from the statutory right to cancel that covers ordinary distance selling, which means the refund policy here is a genuine commercial choice rather than a compliance readout. That makes it a product decision the owner must make (parent Q-01).

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. A fan's individual refund is between the buyer and the seller, and the seller is the Operator. The artist sees the aggregate in the count (19.05.01), never the individual case. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sets the policy, sees requests, approves or refuses, and funds the refund. Sees the released inventory return and route to the waitlist. |
| Fan | Full | Requests a refund and is told the answer, in the same view, with the reason. Where the policy grants it automatically, they should not have to *request* anything — see D-01. |

## Behavior

A refund request resolves against the show's policy. Three shapes exist and they should not be confused:

- **Policy-granted** — the case is inside the policy (cooling-off period, refundable ticket type, a reschedule opt-out window per `#CX-01`). Automatic; no human, no queue, no case.
- **Policy-excluded** — outside the policy. Refused, with the reason, immediately.
- **Discretionary** — outside the policy but the Operator may say yes anyway (an age refusal at the door, a bereavement, a genuine mistake). A real decision by a real person.

**Exchange** — swapping to another date or tier — is a distinct outcome that is often better for everyone: the fan still comes, the venue keeps the money, the seat is not stranded.

Released inventory routes to the waitlist first (19.02.05 D-01), never straight back to public sale.

### Happy Path

1. Fan opens their ticket (19.12) and requests a refund.
2. System resolves the policy immediately and states the answer with the reason.
3. Policy-granted → refund processes on the original payment method; ticket voids; done, no queue.
4. Inventory returns to the manifest and is offered to the waitlist as a time-boxed private claim (19.02.05).
5. Count updates; the movement appears in the next drop (19.05.02) as an explicit negative.
6. Where the policy does not grant it, the fan is offered the exchange path before the refusal, because a fan who changes date is worth more than a fan who is refused.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Refund granted, ticket already scanned | They attended and want their money back | Refused — the scan is the evidence. This is the one place the scan record does commercial work |
| Refund processed after the replica synced; fan turns up and is admitted (19.04.01 D-02) | They got in on a refunded ticket | Reconciliation flags it (19.04.02); the money conversation is the Operator's. Accepted cost of admit-and-reconcile |
| Refund after box office close (19.05.05) | The certified statement is now historically true, currently false | Correct. Post-close refunds are settlement adjustments, never statement edits (19.05.05 D-06) |
| Are fees refundable? | The core policy question and where the industry earns its reputation | `[PENDING]` — parent Q-01. A refund that returns the face value and keeps the booking fee is legal in many places and is exactly the practice that made ticketing hated |
| Age refusal at the door | Refund case opens automatically (19.04.04 D-01) | The fan is on a pavement; they must not have to file anything |
| Externally-ticketed show (19.07) | WeJammin never held the money | Cannot refund what it never took. See parent Q-02 |
| Fan requests refund on a transferred ticket | The current holder is not the payer | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the money goes back to the payer, but the ticket belongs to the holder. A genuine tangle; see 19.09.02 |
| Partial refund on a multi-ticket order | Bought 4, needs to return 1 | Should work; on reserved seating it scatters the remaining group |
| Fan refunds, then the show sells out, then they want back in | Their own choice | Waitlist like anyone else. No special status |
| Refund fails at the rail | The fan is owed money and may not know | Must be a tracked incident with a completion guarantee, not a silent retry |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not requested | Fan holds a ticket | Refund option visible with the policy stated plainly *before* they ask |
| Auto-granted | Request inside policy | Immediate confirmation; no queue, no case, no waiting |
| Refused | Request outside policy | Immediate answer with the reason and the exchange alternative offered |
| Pending decision | Discretionary case | Fan sees it is with the venue and by when they will hear |
| Approved | Operator granted | Refund processing |
| Processing | Money moving | Fan sees the expected arrival window |
| Refunded | Complete | Ticket void; inventory returned |
| Exchanged | Swapped to another date/tier | New ticket issued; no money moved |
| Failed | Rail failure | Tracked incident; the fan is proactively told, not left to discover it |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Where the policy grants a refund, there should be **no request** — the fan clicks refund and gets it | Fan persona — *"the only persona with no professional stake… will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"*; the Fan's switching trigger is friction, and their anti-persona includes *"abuses refunds"*, which a policy engine handles better than a human queue does | ✅ CONFIRMED — a "request" implies a decision, and where the policy already decided, manufacturing a decision is theatre with a queue attached. Every ticketing incumbent routes policy-granted refunds through a support form, which produces a fan who waits three days for an answer the system knew instantly, an Operator processing a decision that was not theirs to make, and a support cost on a case with no content. Splitting the three shapes (granted / excluded / discretionary) at the front means the Operator only ever sees the third — the cases that genuinely need a human — and the fan gets an instant answer in the other two. The discretionary queue then stays small enough to actually be answered, which is what makes the good-faith cases (the refused minor, the bereavement) work. |
| DT-02 | Exchange is a variant of refund — refund the old, sell the new | Modelling economy; the outcome looks the same | ❌ REJECTED — and it is a commercially expensive conflation. Refund-then-rebuy releases the seat to the pool, takes the money out, and requires the fan to complete a second purchase against inventory that may be gone by the time the first refund clears. Every step is a chance to lose them, and on a sold-out show the exchange is simply impossible even though the swap was trivially available. An exchange is a *single atomic operation*: the fan holds a different ticket, no money moves, no inventory is exposed to the pool in between. It is also the outcome the Operator most wants (the fan still comes and the money stays), which means it should be offered *before* the refusal, not discovered afterwards. Different mechanics, different money, different result — not a variant. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Policy-granted refunds are automatic — no request, no queue, no case | Manufacturing a decision the policy already made is theatre with a support cost (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Three shapes are distinguished at the front: granted, excluded, discretionary | Keeps the human queue small enough that the good-faith cases actually get answered |
| D-03 | Exchange is an atomic operation, not refund-then-rebuy, and is offered before a refusal | Refund-then-rebuy exposes the seat and loses the fan; on a sold-out show it makes a trivial swap impossible (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Refunded inventory routes to the waitlist before public sale | Consistent with 19.02.05 D-01 — otherwise a bot gets the seat a waiting fan should have |
| D-05 | A scanned ticket cannot be refunded | The scan is evidence of attendance; this is the one place the scan record does commercial work |
| D-06 | Refund policy is stated before the fan asks, not after | A policy discovered at the point of refusal is a policy designed to be discovered too late |
