# Feature: Reschedule & Postponement Handling

> **Number**: 19.06.03
> **Parent**: [Refunds, Cancellations & Rescheduling](./19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The show is still happening — just not then. A reschedule moves the date and keeps the tickets valid; a postponement moves it to *nowhere yet*, which is a different and more dangerous thing.

Reschedule is neither a cancellation nor an edit, and treating it as either is where products go wrong. It is not a cancellation because the show survives and most holders will come. It is not an edit because moving the date hands **every existing buyer a right they did not have yesterday**: they bought a specific night, and the seller changed it.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Usually the cause (illness, routing, a conflicting offer) and always a party. Proposes the new date; must confirm. Sees the resulting attrition, which is frequently severe and which they should see *before* confirming — a reschedule into a bad week can cost more than a cancellation. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Executes the reschedule, opens the opt-out window, funds the resulting refunds, and owns the postponement deadline. |
| Fan | Full | Told the date moved, that their ticket still works, and that if the new date does not suit them they can have their money back by [date]. Their action is: do nothing, or opt out. Both must be one tap. |

## Behavior

**Reschedule** — a new date exists. Tickets remain valid by default. An **opt-out refund window** opens: time-boxed, no reason required, overriding the ordinary refund policy (`#CX-01`). Wallet passes update to the new date (19.12), or fans arrive on the wrong night holding a pass that says otherwise.

**Postponement** — no new date. Tickets remain valid for a show that does not exist yet. This state is commercially attractive to the seller (it defers the refund obligation while holding the buyer's money) and is therefore the one that must not be allowed to rest — several jurisdictions responded to the 2020 postponement wave by making an indefinite postponement convert into a refund right after a defined period (`#CX-02`).

### Happy Path

1. Musician cannot make the date; proposes a move.
2. Operator checks the room's availability (domain 16) and agrees a new date.
3. System states the consequence **before** confirming: 340 holders, all of whom gain an opt-out right; the estimated attrition; the new date's clash profile.
4. Operator confirms. Show moves; tickets remain valid; every holder is notified.
5. Wallet passes update automatically to the new date (19.12) — the single highest-value automation in this feature.
6. Opt-out window opens for a defined period. Fans do nothing (keep the ticket) or opt out (refund, one tap, no reason).
7. Window closes. Released inventory returns to the manifest and routes to the waitlist (19.02.05) before public sale.
8. The show sells on into its new date; pacing (19.05.03) re-baselines against the new trajectory.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Notification fails to reach a holder | They hold an opt-out right they never knew about, and may arrive on the original night | The gravest failure here — a right nobody knew they had is worse than no right. Wallet pass update (19.12) is a second channel that works even when email does not. See DT-02 |
| Indefinite postponement persists for months | The seller holds the money; the fan holds nothing | Postponement carries a mandatory deadline to become a date or a cancellation (D-02). See `#CX-02` |
| Second reschedule during the first opt-out window | Real and grim | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — the window presumably restarts, but attrition compounds |
| Reserved seating; the new date's configuration differs | Sold seats may not exist in the new configuration | Treated as a material change; those holders need more than an opt-out. See 19.01.04 D-03 |
| Room unavailable on any nearby date | The reschedule cannot happen; it becomes a cancellation | Routed to 19.06.02 |
| Fan opts out after the window closed | The right expired | Ordinary refund policy applies; the answer is probably no |
| Reschedule moves the show earlier | Unusual but real; some holders are now unavailable at shorter notice | Same opt-out right; the window must fit before the new date |
| Externally-ticketed show (19.07) | The incumbent owns the tickets and the refunds | WeJammin can reflect the change; the opt-out is theirs to honour |
| Support act cannot make the new date | Common; the bill changes | A material change for fans who bought for the support — `[PENDING]` |
| Age restriction changes with the new date/room | Some holders may become ineligible | See 19.04.04 Q-03 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Scheduled | Normal | No reschedule state |
| Confirming | Reschedule initiated | Consequence stated before the point of no return: holders affected, opt-out exposure, estimated attrition |
| Rescheduled | New date confirmed | Fans notified; tickets valid; wallet passes updated; opt-out window open |
| Opt-out open | Window running | Fan sees: your ticket works for [new date]; refund available until [date], one tap |
| Opt-out closed | Window expired | Tickets valid; released inventory to waitlist; ordinary policy resumes |
| Postponed | No new date | Fan sees: postponed, new date by [deadline], your options at that point. **Never an open-ended "TBC"** |
| Postponement deadline reached | No date found in time | Converts to cancellation (19.06.02) automatically |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | An indefinite postponement must carry a **mandatory deadline** at which it becomes a real date or an automatic cancellation | The 2020 postponement wave and the regulatory response to it; the structural incentive that the seller benefits from an unbounded postponement while the buyer's money sits with them | ✅ CONFIRMED — this is the third time in this domain the same pattern has appeared, which is now conclusive rather than coincidental: **19.01.02** (holds auto-release at a deadline), **19.03.01** (guest-list allocation auto-returns), and now postponement auto-converting. In every case, a party is holding something that belongs, by default, to someone else; in every case the incentive is to hold it indefinitely; and in every case an unbounded state is the failure. The deadline-with-automatic-resolution is the domain's characteristic mechanism. Here the stakes are highest, because what is being held indefinitely is a fan's money for a show that may never happen — and unlike a forgotten hold, this one is *rewarded* by inaction, which is why the law had to intervene. |
| DT-02 | The reschedule notification is the fan's channel — email or push tells them, and that discharges the duty | Standard notification thinking | ❌ REJECTED — notification is *necessary and insufficient*, and this is where the wallet pass (19.12) stops being a convenience and becomes the mechanism. A significant fraction of holders will not read the email: it lands in promotions, they bought six months ago, the address is dead, or the ticket was transferred and the notification goes to the payer rather than the holder (19.09.02). Those people turn up on the original night. But the **wallet pass on their phone updates itself**, and it is the artifact they will actually look at on the way to the show — it says the new date, in their pocket, without them reading anything. The pass is not a delivery channel for the ticket; it is the second, passive, near-unmissable channel for a date change. That reframes 19.12 from "how the fan gets the ticket" to "how the fan stays correct about the show", which materially raises its MoSCoW weight. |
| DT-03 | Reschedule is a state of cancellation with a re-book, or alternatively just an edit to the date field | Both simplifications are tempting from opposite directions | ❌ REJECTED both ways, which is why this is a feature rather than a note in one of its neighbours. Against *cancellation*: the show survives and most holders keep their tickets — cancelling and re-booking would void 340 valid tickets and require 340 people to buy again, losing most of them for no reason. Against *edit*: changing the date is not changing a field, because the buyer bought a specific night, and the seller unilaterally changing what was sold creates a right that did not previously exist. An edit model has no place to put that right. Reschedule is exactly the middle case both simplifications erase, and it is also the most common of the three flows in real touring. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | A reschedule opens a time-boxed opt-out refund window that overrides the ordinary refund policy | The seller changed what was sold; a "no refunds" policy cannot survive that |
| D-02 | Postponement carries a mandatory deadline to become a date or auto-convert to cancellation | The domain's characteristic mechanism, at its highest stakes — and the only one where inaction is *rewarded*, which is why it needs the hardest deadline (DT-01) |
| D-03 | Wallet passes update automatically to the new date | The passive second channel that catches the fans the notification misses (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Reschedule is neither a cancellation nor an edit | Cancelling voids valid tickets and loses the audience; editing has nowhere to put the right the fan just gained (DT-03) |
| D-05 | The attrition consequence is stated before confirmation | A reschedule into a bad week can cost more than a cancellation, and the Musician should see that before agreeing |
| D-06 | "Postponed, date TBC" is never an open-ended state shown to a fan | The deadline is always stated |
