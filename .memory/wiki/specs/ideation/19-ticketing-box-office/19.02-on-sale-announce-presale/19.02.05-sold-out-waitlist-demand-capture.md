# Feature: Sold-Out Waitlist & Demand Capture

> **Number**: 19.02.05
> **Parent**: [On-Sale, Announce & Presale Access](./19.02-on-sale-announce-presale-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Captures the demand that arrives after inventory is gone, and gives it somewhere to go. A fan who tried to buy and could not is the most valuable unrecorded fact in live music: they proved intent with an action, at a known price, for a known artist, in a known city — and every incumbent throws it away by showing them the word "SOLD OUT" and nothing else.

This feature turns that moment into two things: a **fair claim queue** for inventory that reappears (released holds, refunds, cancelled packages), and a **demand signal** that answers the only question the parties actually want answered — *should we add a second night?*

**Deep Think addition** — not in the sweep candidate list.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Sees waitlist depth for their show, and its geography — the answer to "is there a second night here?" and "which cities should the next tour hit?". This is the most directly career-useful number in the domain for this persona. Cannot see fan identities beyond what consent permits (19.10). |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Sees waitlist depth, configures how returned inventory is offered (claim window length, batch size), and triggers a release to the waitlist. Uses depth to decide whether to release holds early or add a date. |
| Fan | Full | Joins with one tap at the moment they were blocked, states how many tickets they want, and receives a time-boxed private claim when inventory appears. Can leave at any time. Never sees their position — see D-03. |

## Behavior

The waitlist is joinable from any exhaustion state: sold out, presale allocation exhausted (19.02.02), or reaching the front of a queue to find nothing (19.02.04). Joining records the show, the desired quantity, and the moment — nothing else beyond what the account already holds.

When inventory reappears — a hold auto-released (19.01.02), a refund processed (19.06.01), a VIP package cancelled (19.08) — it is offered to the waitlist in order, as a **time-boxed private claim** delivered via the code mechanism (19.02.03), in batches sized to the returned inventory.

Unclaimed inventory rolls to the next batch. Inventory that exhausts the waitlist returns to public sale.

### Happy Path

1. Fan hits an exhaustion state and is offered the waitlist in the same view, with no new page and no form.
2. Fan taps join and states quantity. System confirms with an honest statement of what it means: no guarantee, no position shown, notification if inventory appears.
3. Two weeks later, an artist hold auto-releases 12 tickets (19.01.02 D-01).
4. System offers them to the first batch of waitlist members as private, time-boxed claims (19.02.03).
5. Fan receives a notification, claims within the window, and buys at the show's current price.
6. Unclaimed claims expire; inventory rolls to the next batch.
7. Waitlist depth and geography flow to the Musician and Operator as a demand signal.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Inventory reappears and reopens to the public instead of the waitlist | The default failure — a bot wins a refunded ticket that a waiting fan should have had | Returned inventory goes to the waitlist first, by default. Public release is the fallback, not the path |
| Waitlist longer than the returned inventory by 100× | Normal for a genuinely hot show | Batch offers; the honest join message already set the expectation |
| Fan on the waitlist has since bought from a resale/other channel | Claims a ticket they no longer need, or ignores it | Claim expiry handles it; no penalty. `[PENDING]` — should claiming-and-not-buying affect future batch order? |
| Fan joins waitlists for 30 shows to scalp returned inventory | The waitlist is a scalper's supply line if unguarded | Identity binding + per-buyer limits from 19.09.01 apply to claims exactly as to on-sales |
| Show is cancelled while a waitlist exists | Waitlist members were never buyers; they need telling but not refunding | Notification; waitlist closes |
| Show reschedules and existing buyers refund out (19.06.03) | Returned inventory meets a waitlist that may still want it — a genuine recovery | Refunded inventory routes to the waitlist before public |
| Waitlist depth used to justify a price increase | Technically possible, directly hostile to the Fan persona | Not offered; see D-04 |
| Fan claims in the final second, network round-trip lands after window close | Claim raced the expiry boundary | The claim either wholly succeeds or wholly fails against a single server-side clock — never a half-committed hold. Losing the race shows the same "claim expired, still on the waitlist" message as any other expiry; no ghost reservation is created |
| Musician opens the demand report but consent (19.10) has not been granted for geography | Geography is fan data and is consent-bound | Depth (a count) is shown; geography breakdown is suppressed with a "not enough consented data to break down by city" note — the count is never blocked, only the identifying granularity |
| Fan wants 4 but only 2 return | Partial claim vs all-or-nothing | `[PENDING — Q-01, User decision]` — a genuine fairness trade-off (serve more fans vs serve the group who wanted to attend together); not an implementation detail the agent may decide |

### States

**Fan-facing flow states:**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Waitlist / claim status being fetched on view load | Skeleton on the join CTA or claim card; the blocked-purchase view never flashes a false "join" affordance before status resolves |
| Available | Any exhaustion state reached | One-tap join CTA in the same view as the blocked purchase |
| Joined | Fan joined | Plain confirmation, honest expectation, one-tap leave |
| Claim offered | Inventory returned, fan's batch reached | Time-boxed private claim with a countdown |
| Claim expired | Claim window passed unclaimed | Plain message; fan remains on the waitlist for the next batch |
| Converted | Claim used, ticket bought | Standard purchase confirmation |
| Closed | Show cancelled, or passed | Notification; list closes |
| Error | Join or claim request fails (network, contention) | Plain retry message; the join/claim is not silently dropped, and no partial hold is left behind |

**Operator / Musician demand-report states:**

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Depth/geography being aggregated | Skeleton on the demand panel |
| Empty | Show exists but no fan has waitlisted | Honest zero-state — "no unmet demand recorded yet", not a fabricated `0` that reads as a data error |
| Populated | Waitlist has depth | Depth + geography + requested quantity, with a release-holds CTA |
| Error | Aggregation query fails | Retry message; the last-known depth is never silently replaced with zero |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The waitlist's primary value is **not** ticket recovery — it is the demand signal that feeds domain 17's "add a second night" decision | Musician persona's success criteria (income visibility, winning the next job); problem statement's thesis that *"no system is present when the work happens, so the facts of the work are never recorded"* — applied to demand rather than credits | ✅ CONFIRMED — and this reframes the feature's priority. Recovering 12 refunded tickets is worth a few hundred pounds; knowing that 400 people in Manchester tried to buy and failed is what books the second night, sizes the next tour, and tells a Musician which cities to route through. That signal is generated at exactly one moment and currently evaporates. It is the same structural argument as the platform's thesis: be present when the fact becomes true, or reconstruct it never. This is a strong candidate for the domain's most under-rated feature. |
| DT-02 | Fans should see their waitlist position, as they do in a queue | Symmetry with 19.02.04; transparency instinct | ❌ REJECTED — a position implies an ordered promise the system cannot keep. Waitlist position is not predictive of anything: whether #47 ever gets a ticket depends on how many people refund, which is unknowable and usually zero. Showing "you are #47 of 400" invites the fan to compute odds from a number that does not mean what it appears to mean, and manufactures a grievance when #52 claims a batch that #47 was slow to open. Show the honest thing instead — no guarantee, we will tell you if something appears — and keep the ordering internal and fair. |
| DT-03 | Waitlist depth should automatically prompt the Operator to release held inventory early (19.01.02), closing a loop nobody currently closes (Q-03) | Q-03; the auto-released hold is the main source of returned inventory, so the signal that justifies releasing it and the mechanism that consumes it live in the same feature | ✅ CONFIRMED — but as a **prompt, not an automatic release**. When waitlist depth crosses a meaningful threshold (agent default: depth ≥ the size of the artist/promoter hold pool for that show), the Operator's demand panel surfaces a "release N held tickets to the waitlist?" CTA. It must not fire automatically: releasing a hold is the Operator's (and, via 19.01.02, the artist's) commercial decision — the platform surfaces the evidence and makes the action one tap away, exactly as the thesis argues (be present at the moment the fact is actionable), but never overrides the holder's control of their own allocation. |
| DT-04 | Waitlist demand data should flow to domain 17 (Live Booking) as a first-class, structured artifact, not a report a human eyeballs (Q-04) | Q-04; the platform thesis — "be present when the fact becomes true, or reconstruct it never" — applied to demand. A number a human reads and re-types into a booking conversation is a reconstruction | ✅ CONFIRMED — unmet demand is emitted as a structured, queryable **demand record** (show, city, requested quantity, timestamp, consent state) that 17 and 22 consume directly, not a rendered PDF. The Operator/Musician panel is a *view* over that record, not the record itself. This is the same structural move as capturing a split at source: the artifact is machine-consumable from the moment it exists, so "add a second night" and "route the next tour through Manchester" are queries against real data, not a memory of a screenshot. Fan-identifying fields on the record remain consent-bound (19.10). |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Returned inventory routes to the waitlist before public sale, by default | Reopening returned inventory publicly hands it to whoever is fastest — which is a bot, not a waiting fan |
| D-02 | Claims are time-boxed and private, delivered via the code mechanism | A public mini-on-sale on 12 refunded tickets recreates the problem the waitlist solves |
| D-03 | Fans never see their waitlist position | Position implies an ordered promise the system cannot keep and invites false odds-computation (DT-02) |
| D-04 | Waitlist depth is not exposed as a price-increase trigger | Directly hostile to the Fan persona and to the platform's anti-scalping posture; the signal exists to book more shows, not to charge more for this one |
| D-05 | Waitlist depth surfaces an early-hold-release *prompt* to the Operator, never an automatic release | Releasing a hold is the holder's commercial decision (19.01.02); the platform supplies the evidence and one-tap action but does not override allocation control (DT-03) |
| D-06 | Unmet demand is emitted as a structured, consent-bound demand record consumed directly by domains 17 and 22, not a human-read report | A number a human re-types into a booking conversation is a reconstruction; the thesis demands the artifact be machine-consumable at source (DT-04) |
