# Feature: Queue, Waiting Room & Cart Hold

> **Number**: 19.02.04
> **Parent**: [On-Sale, Announce & Presale Access](./19.02-on-sale-announce-presale-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

What happens when more people want to buy than there is inventory to sell, at the same second. A **waiting room** holds arrivals before the on-sale opens; a **queue** admits them in a defined order once it does; a **cart hold** reserves inventory for a buyer who has been admitted but has not yet paid.

The cart hold is the part that matters most and gets least attention: it is inventory that is unavailable to everyone else while one person finds their card, and every abandoned cart strands allocation for the hold's duration.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Reports | Sees queue depth and drop-off for their show after the fact — a demand signal that feeds the decision to add a second night (domain 17). Cannot configure it. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Config | Sets cart hold duration and per-buyer limits, decides whether a queue is enabled for a given on-sale, and watches queue depth live. Cannot reorder the queue — see D-02. |
| Fan | Full | Waits, sees their position, is admitted, holds a cart with a visible countdown, and completes or loses it. This is the most emotionally charged surface in the entire domain and the one where the platform's fairness claim is either proved or destroyed. |

## Behavior

Before on-sale, arrivals enter a **waiting room** — a holding state where no order is yet assigned, because assigning order on arrival rewards whoever refreshed earliest and is the mechanic that makes bots profitable.

At on-sale, the waiting room is randomised into a **queue** order and admission begins. Admitted buyers get a session in which they may select inventory; selection creates a **cart hold** with a countdown. On payment, the hold converts to a ticket. On expiry or abandonment, held inventory returns to the pool it came from (the window's allocation, or public inventory).

### Happy Path

1. Fan arrives before on-sale and enters the waiting room. System states plainly that arrival order does not affect position.
2. At on-sale, the waiting room is randomised; every waiting fan receives a queue position.
3. System admits buyers at a rate the inventory and checkout can sustain, showing each waiting fan their position and a moving estimate.
4. Fan is admitted, selects tickets within the per-buyer limit; a cart hold is created with a visible countdown.
5. Fan completes payment; hold converts to a ticket; ticket delivery runs (19.12).
6. Fans still queueing see position updates; those who reach the front after inventory is gone are routed to the waitlist (`#CX-04`).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan reaches the front, inventory gone | The defining queue experience, and the highest-intent waitlist moment there is | Honest statement + one-tap waitlist join (19.02.05), not a dead end. See `#CX-04` |
| Cart abandoned | Hold expires, inventory returns to its source pool | Other fans see inventory reappear; Operator sees stranded-cart count |
| Cart hold too long relative to a small allocation | Window appears sold out while nothing has been sold | See `#CX-03`; hold duration must be tuned against allocation size, not set globally |
| Fan loses connection mid-queue | Position must survive a reconnect within a grace period, or the queue punishes bad venues' wifi and mobile signal | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Fan opens multiple tabs/devices | Multiple positions = a queue that rewards the best-equipped, which is the fairness failure it exists to prevent | Identity-bound positions; see 19.09.01 |
| Queue enabled on a show with no demand | Fans face ceremony for a show with 200 unsold tickets — an actively harmful default | Queue is opt-in per on-sale, not on by default. See parent Q-02 |
| Hold expires during payment authorisation | Payment succeeds against inventory that was returned; an oversell | Hold must survive the auth window; `[PENDING]` — a genuine race with money on both sides |
| Operator wants to prioritise someone in the queue | Not offered | See D-02 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Waiting room | Fan arrives before on-sale | "You're in. Position is assigned at [on-sale time], not by arrival — you can put your phone down." |
| Randomising | On-sale moment | Brief transition; positions assigned |
| Queued | Position assigned | Position, moving estimate, and a plain statement of what happens if inventory runs out |
| Admitted | Buyer's turn | Inventory selection with a session timer |
| Holding | Tickets selected | Cart with a visible countdown and the price it is honouring (19.01.01) |
| Converted | Payment succeeded | Confirmation; ticket delivery begins |
| Expired | Hold countdown reached zero | Plain message; inventory released; re-queue or waitlist offered |
| Exhausted | Reached the front, nothing left | Honest message + waitlist CTA |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Queue position must be **randomised at on-sale**, not assigned by arrival order in the waiting room | Fan persona's success criterion — *"money reaches the artist rather than a reseller"*; Fan anti-persona — *"bulk-buys tickets to scalp"*; the anti-scalping posture of 19.09 | ✅ CONFIRMED — first-come ordering in the waiting room is precisely the mechanic that makes automated arrival profitable, and it converts the queue from a fairness device into a speed contest that a bot always wins. Randomisation makes early arrival worthless, which removes the bot's edge without a single bot-detection heuristic. It also lets the platform tell fans something true and kind: refreshing early does nothing. This is the cheapest anti-bot control in the domain and it is a *design* choice, not a detection one. |
| DT-02 | Operators should be able to prioritise or admit specific people in the queue | Operators will ask for it — a promoter's friend, a journalist, an artist's family | ❌ REJECTED — a queue with a back door is not a queue, and the moment one exists the platform's fairness claim becomes a marketing statement rather than a mechanism. The legitimate need is real, but it is already served correctly elsewhere: guest list (19.03) for people who should not be paying, and a presale window with its own allocation (19.02.02) for people who should get early access. Both are auditable and visible; queue-jumping is neither. Routing the need to the right mechanism is the answer, not building the back door. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Queue order is randomised at on-sale; waiting-room arrival order confers nothing | Arrival-order ranking is the mechanic that pays for bots; randomisation removes their edge structurally (DT-01) |
| D-02 | No queue prioritisation or back door for any role | A queue with a back door is not a queue; the legitimate needs are served by guest list (19.03) and presale windows (19.02.02), both auditable (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Queue is opt-in per on-sale, not on by default | Ceremony on a show with no demand problem is pure friction |
| D-04 | Cart holds return inventory to their source pool, not to public inventory | A hold taken from a window's allocation belongs to that window |
