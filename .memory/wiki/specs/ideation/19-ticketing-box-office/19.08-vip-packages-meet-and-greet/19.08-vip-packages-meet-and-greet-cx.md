## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Composition ↔ M&G Scheduling

**Relationship**: When a package includes a meet-and-greet, selling the package must reserve an M&G slot. The number of slots is not a business preference — it is set by how many humans the artist can meet in the window between soundcheck and doors while remaining able to perform. That makes M&G capacity a hard ceiling on package sales, and it is usually the *lowest* ceiling among the package's components.

**Role scoping**:
- **Musician**: sets the slot capacity, because it is their time and stamina.
- **Operator**: composes and sells the package, and must live inside the artist's ceiling.
- **Fan**: buys a package and gets a slot, or the package is sold out.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: M&G slot inventory. Composition reserves it; scheduling owns it. Single owner (the artist's capacity), so no merge — but a package sold without a slot reserved is a promise that cannot be kept.
2. **Trigger chain**: Package sale → reserve ticket (19.01.02) + reserve merch (20/13) + reserve M&G slot, **atomically**. Partial success is the failure mode: a fan holding a package with no slot is worse than a fan who could not buy.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes, and it is the interesting one — the Operator sells, but the Musician caps. The Operator cannot raise the ceiling to make a sale.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A package sale notifies the artist's slot roster; approaching capacity should warn before it is hit.
5. **State transition conflict**: Two concurrent package purchases for the last slot. Money on both sides.

### CX-02: Composition ↔ Redemption & Fulfilment

**Relationship**: A package is one purchase and several promises with unrelated clocks. The signed poster must be printed, signed and shipped — weeks ahead, before anyone knows if the show will happen. The laminate is handed over at the door. The early entry is a door behaviour. The M&G is at 18:40. Fulfilment is therefore not one process but several, and the package's state is the *conjunction* of them.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: fulfils the venue-side components.
- **Fan**: experiences whichever component fails.
- **Musician**: signs the posters, and is the reason the merch component exists.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The package record. Multiple fulfilment processes update it independently.
2. **Trigger chain**: Composition defines the promises; each fulfils on its own clock. **Cancellation is where this bites** — a show cancelled after the posters shipped means the fan has the poster and a refund claim (19.06.02 Q-02).
3. **Permission intersection**: No.
4. **Notification fan-out**: Each component's fulfilment notifies separately, which risks the fan receiving four messages about one purchase.
5. **State transition conflict**: Partially-fulfilled packages at cancellation. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`

### CX-03: M&G Scheduling ↔ Redemption & Fulfilment

| R-01 | 19.08.02 M&G Scheduling | 19.04.01 Ticket Scan & Validation | Considered because both are "check the person at a door". Rejected: the M&G's constrained resource is the artist's time and its check is a roster ("are you on the list for 18:40"), not a validation against a manifest. It also has no offline problem worth solving — it is 30 people on a printed list in a corridor, and the correct implementation may genuinely be a printed list. Modelling it as a scan would import the whole replica/reconciliation apparatus (19.04.02) to solve a problem that a sheet of A4 solves better. |
| R-02 | 19.08.01 Composition | 19.01.01 Ticket Types & Price Scaling | Tempting — a VIP package looks like a premium ticket tier. Rejected: a price level is a *place in the room at a price*. A package is a bundle of heterogeneous promises across three domains, only one of which is admission. Modelling it as a tier would put a t-shirt and a human's calendar inside the manifest, which is exactly the 40-nullable-columns failure that D-14 rejected at the marketplace level. The package *contains* a ticket at a price level; it is not one. |
