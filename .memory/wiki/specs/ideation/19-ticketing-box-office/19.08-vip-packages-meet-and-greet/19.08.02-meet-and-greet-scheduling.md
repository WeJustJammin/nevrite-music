# Feature: Meet-and-Greet Scheduling & Slots

> **Number**: 19.08.02
> **Parent**: [VIP Packages & Meet-and-Greet](./19.08-vip-packages-meet-and-greet-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Thirty people, forty minutes, one corridor, and an artist who has to be on stage at nine. The meet-and-greet is a capacity-constrained scheduling problem where the constrained resource is **a human being's time and stamina** — which is why this is the only feature in the entire domain where the Musician holds Full access and the Operator only configures.

It is also the only fulfilment in the domain that cannot be retried. A missed slot is gone.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Full | **Owns this.** Sets how many people they can meet and in what window, sees the roster of who is coming, and can cut the capacity when they are ill or the day has gone wrong. Their stamina is the inventory, so their word is final — including on the night. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Config | Supplies the room, the schedule slot in the day's running order, and the staff to run it. Cannot set the capacity — that is the artist's call — and cannot add a person to the roster the artist has not agreed to. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees their slot time and where to be, and gets reminded. Cannot choose or change their slot — see D-03. |

## Behavior

An M&G has a **window** (a start and end in the day's running order, between soundcheck and doors), a **capacity** (how many humans the artist will meet), and a **roster** (who they are, from package sales in 19.08.01).

Slots are assigned, not chosen. Each has a time, and the roster is what door staff work from — which in practice is very often a printed sheet of A4, and that is a legitimate implementation rather than a failure (`#R-01`).

The window sits inside the show's advance (domain 18), because an M&G that overruns eats the support act's soundcheck.

### Happy Path

1. Musician sets their M&G capacity for the run — "30 people, 40 minutes" — and it flows to every date.
2. Operator places the window in the day's running order (domain 18) and assigns a room and a staff member.
3. Packages sell (19.08.01); each sale reserves a slot and adds a name to the roster.
4. Approaching capacity, the artist is warned before it is hit.
5. On the day, the roster is available to the artist's team and to the door — on a screen or on paper, both fine.
6. Fans are reminded of their slot time and location.
7. It runs. It overruns. The window absorbs it because the window was set with slack (see DT-02).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fan misses their slot | Cannot be re-run — the artist is at soundcheck, then on stage, then in a van | The one non-retryable fulfilment in the domain (`#CX-03`). What is owed is a real question with no clean answer. `[PENDING]` |
| Artist is ill / voice is going | The whole M&G must go, on the day, on medical grounds | Musician can cancel the M&G without cancelling the show. Refund of the M&G component only — which requires 19.08.01's components to be separable (they are) |
| M&G overruns into doors | Common, and it delays the show | Slack must be in the window from the start, not discovered. See DT-02 |
| Roster contains someone the artist does not want to meet | Rare, real, and a safety matter for the artist | Musician can remove someone from their own roster. Refund follows. Touches domain 24 |
| Capacity cut after packages sold | The artist can no longer meet 30 | Some packages must be refunded; the artist's call stands. Order of refund is a fairness question — `[PENDING]` |
| No network in the corridor | The venue's connectivity problem again (19.04.02) | A printed roster works. This is a genuine offline story and it is paper |
| Fan brings three friends to a one-person slot | Universal | Roster is per named person; the door is a human decision |
| Transferred package (19.08.01 Q-03) | The roster names someone who did not buy | `[PENDING]` |
| M&G on a rescheduled show (19.06.03) | The window moves with the date | Roster follows; capacity may change with the artist's availability |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not offered | Show has no M&G | Nothing; most shows have none |
| Configured | Capacity and window set | Musician sees capacity; Operator sees the window in the running order |
| Selling | Packages on sale | Roster filling; capacity remaining |
| Near capacity | Threshold reached | Artist warned before it is hit |
| Full | Capacity reached | Packages containing an M&G are sold out (19.08.01's binding constraint) |
| Day-of | Show day | Roster available on screen and on paper |
| Running | Window open | Slot times; running order |
| Cancelled (M&G) | Artist pulled it | M&G component refunded; the show is unaffected |
| Complete | Window closed | No-shows recorded |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **artist owns the capacity**, not the venue — the inventory here is a person's stamina | `personas.md` on the Musician as a working professional with finite capacity; the Operator persona's inventory is defined as *"time and space, not goods or labour"* — and an M&G is precisely labour, which is outside what the Operator sells | ✅ CONFIRMED, and it is why the Role Matrix inverts here and nowhere else in the domain. Every other feature in Ticketing sells the Operator's inventory: seats in their room, time in their building. The M&G sells the artist's *labour and emotional energy*, forty minutes before they have to perform. If the venue can set that number, the venue can sell an artist into a state where the show suffers — and the commercial incentive points exactly that way, because M&G slots are high-margin and the venue does not pay the cost of an exhausted performer. The person who bears the cost must set the ceiling. This also explains why the M&G is usually 19.08.01's binding constraint: it is the only component whose supply is a human saying "that's enough". |
| DT-02 | Slots should be packed tight to maximise the number sold — 30 people in 40 minutes is 80 seconds each | Efficiency instinct, and it is what a scheduling algorithm optimises for by default | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection is about what the product is *for*. A meet-and-greet packed to its theoretical maximum produces the thing every fan complains about: a conveyor belt, eleven seconds of eye contact, a blurred photo, and a fan who paid £80 to feel processed. It also has no slack, so the first overrun (and there is always an overrun — someone cries, someone has a gift, someone's phone won't focus) cascades into doors and delays the show. The constraint is not "how many can we physically pass through" but "how many can the artist meet *properly* while staying able to play". Optimising throughput here optimises against both the fan's experience and the show. The window should carry deliberate slack, and the capacity should be the artist's honest number rather than the room's theoretical one. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The Musician sets M&G capacity; the Operator cannot | The inventory is the artist's stamina, and the party who bears the cost must set the ceiling (DT-01) |
| D-02 | The window carries deliberate slack; capacity is the artist's honest number, not the room's theoretical throughput | Packing to maximum produces a conveyor belt and cascades into doors (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Slots are assigned, not chosen | Fan choice over a 40-minute window creates a scheduling problem with no value to anyone |
| D-04 | The M&G can be cancelled without cancelling the show | An ill artist can still play; the components are separable (19.08.01 D-01) |
| D-05 | The Musician may remove someone from their own roster | It is their safety, and it is a decision nobody else can make for them |
| D-06 | A printed roster is a supported implementation, not a degraded one | The corridor has no signal and paper works |
