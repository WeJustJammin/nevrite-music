# Feature: VIP Redemption & Fulfilment

> **Number**: 19.08.03
> **Parent**: [VIP Packages & Meet-and-Greet](./19.08-vip-packages-meet-and-greet-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Delivering what the package promised. One purchase, several promises, and each one arrives by a different mechanism on a different clock: the poster ships in the post three weeks early, the laminate is handed over at a door at 18:30, the early entry is a door behaviour at 18:45, the meet-and-greet happens at 18:40 and never again.

The package's state is the **conjunction** of its components' states, which means a package is only fulfilled when the last of several unrelated processes completes — and any one of them failing is, to the fan, the package failing.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees fulfilment status on their run — particularly whether the signed items actually got signed and shipped, which is their obligation and the one most likely to slip. Does not operate the fulfilment. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Fulfils the venue-side components: laminate handover, early entry, M&G door. Sees the package roster for the night and each package's outstanding components. |
| Fan | Full | Receives the components. **Needs one view that says what they bought, what has arrived, and where to be at what time** — not four notifications about one purchase. See D-02. |

## Behavior

Each component has its own fulfilment mechanism:

| Component | Mechanism | Clock | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ticket | Delivery + scan (19.12, 19.04) | On purchase / at the door | Standard |
| Merch (physical) | Ships | Weeks ahead | Doesn't arrive; or arrives and the show cancels |
| Signed item | Artist signs, then ships | Weeks ahead, and the artist is touring | Never gets signed |
| Laminate | Handover | At the door | Ran out |
| Early entry | Door behaviour | 15–60 min before doors | Door staff don't know |
| M&G | Slot (19.08.02) | One specific minute | Missed — unrecoverable |

Physical components shipping **weeks before the show** is the structural problem: they are delivered before anyone knows whether the show will happen (19.06.02).

### Happy Path

1. Fan buys a package (19.08.01); components reserve atomically.
2. Fan sees **one** view: what they bought, what is coming, what to do on the night.
3. Physical components enter fulfilment — the artist signs a batch on a day off, items ship.
4. Fan receives the poster three weeks out; the package view updates.
5. On the night, door staff see the package roster: who gets early entry, who gets a laminate, who is at which M&G slot.
6. Fan arrives at 18:30 for early entry, collects the laminate, meets the artist at 18:40, and watches the show.
7. Every component fulfilled; the package closes.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show cancelled after physical components shipped | Fan has the poster and a refund claim | The structural problem. Ticket component refunded unconditionally (19.06.02 **D-09**); the physical-goods component's treatment is the open owner call — see Q-01 and 19.06.02 Q-03 |
| Signed items never got signed | The artist is on tour; there was no day off | Extremely common in reality. Visible to the artist as an obligation, not a surprise at the shipping deadline |
| Fan misses their M&G slot | Unrecoverable (`#CX-03`) | See 19.08.02 Q-01 |
| Door staff don't know about early entry | The fan is turned away from a door they paid to walk through early | Package roster must be on the door's device (19.04) and on paper. A package benefit nobody at the door knows about is not a benefit |
| Laminates run out | Someone miscounted | Operator's problem; the fan still got everything else. Partial fulfilment is real |
| Merch doesn't arrive before the show | The poster is in the post | Fan expected it at the show, or expected it at home — the package must have said which |
| Fan bought a package and cannot attend | The merch is theirs; the M&G and entry are not | Partial refund shape. `[PENDING]` |
| Reschedule (19.06.03) | The poster already arrived; the show moved | Physical components stay fulfilled; the date-bound ones move |
| Package transferred | Who gets the shipped poster? | `[PENDING]` — ties to 19.08.01 Q-03 |
| Fan turns up with no idea what they bought | Bought it six months ago in a bundle | The single view (D-02) is the answer |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Purchased | Package bought | One view: contents, expected arrivals, night-of instructions |
| Partially fulfilled | Some components delivered | Per-component status in the one view |
| Shipped | Physical components dispatched | Tracking, where the merch domain provides it |
| Awaiting signature | Signed items not yet signed | Artist sees this as an outstanding obligation with a deadline |
| Night-of ready | Show day | Times and locations: be here at 18:30, M&G at 18:40 |
| Redeemed (venue-side) | Laminate, entry, M&G done | Door marks them off the roster |
| Fulfilled | Every component complete | Package closes |
| Failed (component) | Something didn't happen | Named specifically — which component, and what is being done |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Physical components shipping **weeks before the show** is a structural exposure, not an edge case | Following 19.06.02's cancellation cascade into its consequence here; the reality that signed merch must be signed on a day off and posted, which means it goes out long before the date | ✅ CONFIRMED — and it is genuinely under-appreciated. Every other product in this domain is delivered *at or after* the moment the show is confirmed to be happening; the VIP poster is delivered before anyone knows. That inverts the normal refund logic: on a cancelled show, the fan is owed their money and already has the goods, and the goods are frequently the expensive part. It also means a package sale is partly an **e-commerce** transaction with distance-selling rights attached (`meta/constraints.md` flags consumer protection as `[PENDING]`), sitting inside a live-event product that is usually exempt from them. One purchase, two regulatory regimes, and the line runs through the middle of the bundle. This is a genuine reason to consider the ticket-only VIP shape (19.08.01 Q-01) at the beachhead. |
| DT-02 | Each component should notify the fan on its own clock — the poster shipping notification, the M&G reminder, the door instructions | Follows naturally from components fulfilling independently, and it is what a component-oriented implementation produces by default | ❌ REJECTED — the fan bought **one thing**, and four notifications about one purchase is how a premium product feels like admin. The Fan persona is explicitly *"the only persona with no professional stake"* who *"will not tolerate professional-tool complexity"*; they paid £80 for an experience and receiving a fulfilment status feed is the opposite of one. The correct model is a **single package view** that answers the only three questions they have — what did I buy, has it arrived, where do I need to be and when — with at most one timely nudge before the night. Internally the components fulfil independently; externally that is invisible. Letting the internal decomposition surface as the fan's experience is the classic failure of bundle products, and it is especially costly here because the whole thing being sold is *feeling looked after*. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Package state is the conjunction of its components' states | A package is fulfilled only when the last unrelated process completes |
| D-02 | The fan sees one package view, not a per-component notification feed | They bought one thing; four notifications turn a premium experience into admin (DT-02) |
| D-03 | The package roster is on the door's device and on paper | A package benefit nobody at the door knows about is not a benefit |
| D-04 | The package states plainly whether physical components arrive by post or at the show | It is the difference between a fan turning up expecting a poster and not |
| D-05 | Unsigned signed-items are an outstanding obligation visible to the artist with a deadline | Not a surprise discovered at the shipping cut-off |
