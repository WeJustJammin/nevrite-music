# Feature: Package Composition & Cross-Domain Inventory

> **Number**: 19.08.01
> **Parent**: [VIP Packages & Meet-and-Greet](./19.08-vip-packages-meet-and-greet-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Building the bundle: a ticket, plus early entry, plus a signed poster, plus a laminate, plus a meet-and-greet slot, sold as one thing at one price. The composition decides what the package promises; everything downstream is the consequence.

The hard part is that the components live in **different domains with different inventory models** — the ticket is a unit of the manifest (19.01.02), the poster is merch stock (20/13), the M&G slot is a minute of a human's finite evening (19.08.02). Selling the package must reserve all of them at once or it sells a promise it cannot keep.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Contributes the components that are theirs — the merch, the signed items, their M&G capacity — and approves the package that carries their name and face. Cannot compose or price it unilaterally where the venue is the seller of record. Where the Musician self-promotes, they hold Full. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Composes packages, prices them, and sells them. Sees the binding constraint — which component's inventory caps the package. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees what the package contains, in plain language, and buys it. Sees the price as an all-in figure like everything else (19.01.03). |

## Behavior

A package is a **bundle of component references**, never copies. Each component names its source and its inventory:

| Component type | Source domain | Inventory model |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket | 19.01.02 manifest | A unit at a price level |
| M&G slot | 19.08.02 | A minute of the artist's time |
| Merch item | 20 / 13 | Physical stock, by size |
| Early entry | 19.04 door | Behavioural, uncapped-ish |
| Laminate | — | Effectively unlimited |

Package availability is the **minimum** across its components, and the binding constraint is usually the M&G (`#CX-01`) or a t-shirt size.

Purchase reserves every component **atomically**. Partial reservation is the failure that must not exist.

### Happy Path

1. Operator composes a package: 1 GA ticket, 1 M&G slot, 1 signed poster, 1 laminate, early entry.
2. System resolves each component to its source and shows the available inventory of each.
3. System shows the **binding constraint**: "M&G capacity is 30 — you can sell 30 of these, regardless of the 200 tickets and 60 posters".
4. Operator prices the package; the all-in price displays like any other product (19.01.03).
5. Musician approves — it is their face, their merch, their evening.
6. Fan buys. All components reserve atomically: ticket from the manifest, poster from merch stock, slot from the roster.
7. Fulfilment (19.08.03) begins on each component's own clock.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Merch stock decremented by the merch store and by packages | Both must draw from the same stock or the platform oversells its own inventory | Components are references, never copies (parent D-03). This is the reason for that decision |
| Atomic reservation partially fails | Fan holds a package with no M&G slot, or a ticket with no poster | Must not be possible. A partial package is worse than a failed sale — the fan does not know what they hold |
| T-shirt size runs out | The package is available in M and L but not S | Package availability is per-variant, not per-package. A package with a sized component is really N packages |
| M&G capacity is the cap | The usual case | Stated at composition, not discovered at sell-out |
| Package sold; show cancelled after the poster shipped | Fan has the poster and a refund claim | See 19.06.02 Q-02; `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Merch domain (20/13) does not exist yet | Packages with physical components cannot be composed | Ticket-only VIP (early entry + M&G) has no such dependency. See parent Q-02 |
| Package refunded | Every component must return to its own source | Reverse of the atomic reserve; the poster may have shipped |
| Package resold/transferred (19.09.02) | The M&G slot names a person the artist will meet | `[PENDING]` — a transferred M&G is a stranger in the roster |
| Two packages, one last slot | Concurrent purchase, money both sides | `#CX-01` Q5 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Draft | Operator composing | Components with their sources and available inventory |
| Constrained | Composition resolved | The binding constraint named explicitly |
| Pending approval | Awaiting the Musician | Artist sees what is being sold in their name |
| Live | Approved and on sale | Fan sees the package in plain language with an all-in price |
| Sold out | Binding component exhausted | Stated with *which* component ran out where useful |
| Partially available | Some variants gone (sizes) | Per-variant availability |
| Withdrawn | Pulled from sale | Existing packages honoured |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **binding constraint must be named at composition**, not discovered at sell-out | Reasoning about what an Operator needs to know to make a commercial decision; parallel to 19.07.01 DT-01 (state the capability ceiling before connecting) | ✅ CONFIRMED — a package composed from 200 tickets, 60 posters and 30 M&G slots is a **30-unit product**, and if that is not stated at composition the Operator will price and promote it as though it were something bigger. Worse, the constraint is usually the component that feels least like inventory: nobody thinks of the artist's evening as stock, so the M&G is exactly the one that gets forgotten. Naming it converts an invisible ceiling into a commercial input — the Operator can then decide to ask the artist for more slots, drop the M&G from the package, or accept a 30-unit line. Same principle as 19.07.01's capability profile, arriving independently: state the ceiling before someone builds a plan on the wrong number. |
| DT-02 | The package's inventory can be tracked as its own number — allocate 30 packages and decrement as they sell | The obvious simplification, and every bundling feature is tempted by it | ❌ REJECTED — it double-counts, and it double-counts the components that hurt. A package's t-shirt is *the same t-shirt* the merch store (20/13) is selling. Giving the package its own stock number means the merch store and the package sell the same physical garment to two people, and nobody finds out until a box is short. The same applies to the ticket: a package's ticket is a unit of the manifest that must be invisible to public sale and countable at the door and present in the settlement. Components must be **references into their owning domain's inventory**, so that there is exactly one number per real-world thing. The cost is that package availability becomes a computed minimum across domains rather than a stored integer — which is more work and is correct. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Components are references into their owning domain's inventory, never copies with their own count | One real-world thing, one number — otherwise the merch store and the package sell the same t-shirt twice (DT-02) |
| D-02 | Package availability is a computed minimum across components, not a stored integer | Follows from D-01; more work, and correct |
| D-03 | The binding constraint is named at composition | An invisible ceiling gets priced and promoted as something bigger; the forgotten one is always the artist's time (DT-01) |
| D-04 | Purchase reserves every component atomically | A partial package is worse than a failed sale — the fan does not know what they hold |
| D-05 | Packages with sized components are per-variant products | A package available only in M is not "available" |
| D-06 | The Musician approves packages sold in their name | It is their face, their merch and their evening |
