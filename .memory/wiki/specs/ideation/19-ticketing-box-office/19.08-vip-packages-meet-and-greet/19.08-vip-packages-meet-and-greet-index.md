## Overview

Selling more than admission: a ticket bundled with early entry, a signed poster, a t-shirt, a soundcheck watch, and twenty seconds of eye contact with the artist. VIP packages are a substantial and growing revenue line for touring acts, and the meet-and-greet is the part fans remember and the part artists dread.

**Why this is a sub-domain, not a feature**: three capabilities that interact through a bundle whose components live in different domains. Package composition reserves inventory across a ticket (19.01) *and* merch (20/13) atomically; M&G scheduling is a capacity-constrained slot system with a human's finite time as the resource; and redemption/fulfilment splits between a physical thing that ships weeks ahead and a laminate handed over at a door. Break any one and the package sells something it cannot deliver.

**The honest framing**: this is the lowest-priority sub-domain in the domain. It matters to touring acts above club level, and WeJammin's beachhead is the working musician playing a 200-cap room where the VIP package does not exist. It is real, it is well-understood, and it can wait.

> **Note on 19.08.02 Musician = Full, Operator = Config**: the inversion is deliberate and is the only one in the domain. The M&G's constrained resource is the artist's own time and stamina — they are the inventory. The venue supplies a room and a schedule slot; the artist decides how many humans they can meet and stay functional for the show. Every other feature in this domain is the Operator's; this one is not.
