# Feature: External Ticketing Connectors & Onboarding

> **Number**: 19.07.01
> **Parent**: [External Ticketing Integration & Count Reconciliation](./19.07-external-ticketing-integration-index.md)
> **Status**: [PARTIAL]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

Connecting a venue's existing ticketing platform so its numbers flow into WeJammin. The venue already sells on DICE or Skiddle or Eventbrite or a WordPress plugin, they are not going to stop, and WeJammin needs the count anyway.

The uncomfortable truth this feature must hold: **the platforms on the other end have no incentive to help**. WeJammin ingesting a venue's box-office data is a competitor building a settlement product on their rail. Some have real APIs, some have partner programmes with terms, some have nothing, and some have terms that arguably prohibit exactly this.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | None | Not visible. Connecting a platform is venue plumbing. The artist sees the *consequence* — where their number came from (19.07.02) — not the connection. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Connects their platform with their own credentials, sees what the connection can and cannot retrieve, and sees when it breaks. WeJammin acts *as the venue* against the foreign platform, never as itself. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this. From the fan's side, they buy on DICE as they always did. |

## Behavior

Each supported platform has a **connector** with a declared **capability profile** — what it can actually retrieve, at what granularity, at what latency:

| Capability tier | What it yields | Downstream consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Full manifest** | Per-tier sold, comps, fees, buyer records | Everything downstream works |
| **Aggregate count** | Tickets sold, maybe gross | No per-tier mapping; drops and statements are coarse |
| **Scheduled export** | Nightly CSV | The count is a day stale, permanently |
| **None** | — | Manual entry (19.07.04) |

The capability profile must be visible to the Operator **before** they connect, because it determines what they will get out of the rest of the domain — a venue whose platform only yields an aggregate should know their statement will be coarse before they build a workflow on it.

### Show Matching (Q-03 resolved)

A connection is not a count. A connection yields a *foreign event* — a DICE event ID, an Eventbrite listing — and someone must decide which WeJammin show it belongs to before a single number flows into settlement. This is the load-bearing correctness step of the whole feature, because a wrong match is not a visible error: it silently pipes another show's money into this show's statement, and both shows look fine.

The behavior is **propose, never auto-bind**:

1. On connection and on each new foreign event, WeJammin proposes candidate matches, ranked, using the signals it has: **date/time** (the strongest), **venue** (the connection is already bound to one venue per D-04, so cross-venue candidates are excluded), and **title similarity** (the weakest — foreign titles differ from WeJammin's).
2. The Operator **confirms** the match explicitly. A count never flows into 19.07.02 from an unconfirmed match. There is no confidence threshold above which the system binds silently — the same principle as D-02: a confident wrong answer that reaches money is worse than a step the Operator has to take.
3. A confirmed mapping is **stored and reused** — the Operator confirms a given foreign event once, not on every fetch.
4. Ambiguity is surfaced, not guessed. If two WeJammin shows share the date (a venue running two rooms; a matinee and an evening), the Operator disambiguates; the system presents both rather than picking.

Match confidence tiers and the long-tail heuristics (fuzzy title scoring weights, multi-day festival events that map to many shows, recurring/residency events) are `[PENDING — Step 5 Must-depth or /write-be-spec]`. The Level-1 guarantee is the shape: **no count flows on an unconfirmed match, and the match is the Operator's to confirm.**

### Happy Path

1. Operator selects their ticketing platform from the supported list.
2. System states the capability profile plainly: what will be retrieved, at what granularity, how often, and what will not work as a result.
3. Operator authorises with their own credentials on the foreign platform.
4. System verifies the connection by fetching a real show and showing the Operator exactly what came back.
5. Connection binds to the venue; shows are matched to their foreign counterparts (see edge cases — this is harder than it sounds).
6. Counts flow on the connector's cadence into ingestion (19.07.02).
7. Connector health is monitored; a break reaches the Operator rather than silently freezing the count.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Platform has no API | The majority case at the target scale | Manual entry (19.07.04). This is a supported path, not a failure — see `#CX-04` |
| Platform's terms prohibit this use | The connector may be legally unavailable regardless of technical feasibility | Parent Q-02. A real risk to the whole feature |
| Connector breaks; count freezes at a stale value | **The dangerous failure** — a stale count looks exactly like a current one and feeds a drop a Musician reads as true | Freshness is mandatory on every number (parent D-04). A gap must be visible as a gap; see DT-02 |
| Show matching — which WeJammin show is which DICE event? | Not trivial: names differ, dates match many shows, a venue may run two rooms. Resolved at Level 1: WeJammin proposes ranked candidates; the Operator confirms; no count flows on an unconfirmed match (see Show Matching, DT-03, D-06) | Ranked candidate list with the confirm action; ambiguous dates present both shows for the Operator to disambiguate rather than being silently picked |
| Two WeJammin shows share the foreign event's date | A venue running two rooms, or a matinee + evening | The system presents both candidates; the Operator disambiguates. It never guesses which room the count belongs to |
| Credentials expire or are revoked | Silent stop | Health check must catch it; the Operator is told, not the count |
| Platform rate-limits or throttles | Cadence degrades | Freshness reflects reality rather than aspiration |
| Venue changes platform mid-campaign | Real; contracts end | Both sources exist for one show. See parent Q-04 (hybrid) |
| Foreign platform restates history (a refund posted late) | Our count moves backwards for a past date | Correct, and it must be visible; drops already sent stay immutable (19.05.02 D-01) |
| Platform returns data for a show that was cancelled | Their model and ours disagree on the show's existence | The mismatch surfaces to the Operator as a conflict rather than silently ingesting; the count is not bound to a cancelled WeJammin show. Refund handling for tickets already sold on the foreign platform is `[PENDING — /write-be-spec]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Not connected | Venue has no external platform linked | Platform picker with capability profiles shown *before* connecting |
| Unsupported | Platform has no connector | Manual entry offered as a first-class path, not an apology |
| Connecting | Auth in flight | Standard auth handoff |
| Verifying | Connected, fetching a test show | The actual returned data shown to the Operator — proof, not a green tick |
| Connected | Verified | Capability profile, cadence, last successful fetch, freshness |
| Degraded | Fetches failing / throttled | Named clearly; count freshness reflects it |
| Broken | Auth revoked / API gone | Operator alerted; the count shows a visible gap rather than a stale number |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The **capability profile must be shown before connecting**, not discovered afterwards | Reasoning about what determines the Operator's experience of the entire domain; the Operator persona's switching trigger is a costly surprise | ✅ CONFIRMED — the connector is not a binary "connected/not". A venue on a platform that yields only an aggregate sold count will get a coarse statement (19.05.05), a vague drop (19.05.02), and pacing (19.05.03) with no tier detail — and they will discover this weeks later, having built a workflow on an expectation nobody set. Stating the ceiling up front costs one screen and prevents the class of disappointment that makes an Operator conclude the platform "doesn't really work". It also makes the honest case for manual entry (19.07.04) visible: a human typing per-tier numbers may genuinely yield a *richer* count than a thin API, which is counter-intuitive enough that nobody will believe it unless the profile is on screen. |
| DT-02 | A broken connector should retry quietly and keep serving the last known count until it recovers | Standard resilience thinking — availability over correctness | ❌ REJECTED, and it is the most dangerous instinct in this feature. A stale count is not a degraded count, it is a **wrong count that looks right**. It flows into a drop that a Musician reads as current (19.05.02), into pacing that concludes the show has stopped selling (19.05.03), and potentially into a statement that gets certified (19.05.05) — and at no point does anything look broken. The failure is silent, confident and lands on money. The correct behaviour is the opposite of graceful degradation: make the gap **loud and visible**, mark the count's freshness honestly, block certification on a stale source, and let the Operator see a hole rather than a lie. This is the same principle as 19.05.05 D-02 (an honest gap beats a confident fabrication) arriving independently in a different sub-domain. |
| DT-03 | Show matching should auto-bind above a confidence threshold — if the date and venue match and the title is close enough, bind it silently so the Operator has less to do | Convenience / friction-reduction reasoning; the connection already knows the venue, so a single strong candidate feels safe to auto-accept | ❌ REJECTED — same failure family as DT-02. A wrong match is invisible: it pipes another show's numbers into this show's settlement and **both shows still look correct**, so nothing ever surfaces the error until the money is wrong. The signals that feel decisive are not: a date collides across two rooms and a matinee, and foreign titles rarely match WeJammin's. The one place friction is worth paying for is the point where a mistake becomes unattributable money. So the system *proposes* (ranked candidates, venue already scoped by D-04) but the Operator *confirms once per foreign event*, and no count flows on an unconfirmed match. This is DT-02's principle — honest step over confident silence — applied to identity rather than freshness. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | The capability profile is shown before connecting | It determines the Operator's experience of the whole domain; discovering it late is the surprise that loses them (DT-01) |
| D-02 | A broken connector produces a visible gap, never a stale number presented as current | Silent staleness is a confident lie that reaches a drop, pacing and a statement without ever looking broken (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Verification shows the actual returned data, not a green tick | Proof that the connection yields what was promised, on a real show |
| D-04 | WeJammin acts as the venue against the foreign platform, using the venue's own credentials | The venue's data is the venue's to pipe; WeJammin has no independent standing on the incumbent's rail |
| D-05 | Manual entry is offered as a first-class path when a platform is unsupported, not as an apology | It is the majority case (parent D-02) |
| D-06 | Foreign events are matched to WeJammin shows by **operator-confirmed proposal**, never silent auto-bind; no count flows on an unconfirmed match, and a confirmed mapping is stored and reused | A wrong match is an invisible error that lands on settlement money; the matching signals (date, venue, title) are not decisive enough to auto-accept safely (DT-03, Q-03) |
