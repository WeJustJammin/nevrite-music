## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Connectors ↔ Ingestion & Mapping

**Relationship**: Each connector has a capability ceiling — what the foreign platform will actually give up. Some expose a full manifest with per-tier breakdowns, fee splits and buyer records. Some expose "tickets sold: 260". Some expose a CSV emailed nightly. The mapping can never be richer than its source, so the connector's ceiling is the mapping's ceiling, and every downstream consumer (the drop, the statement, pacing) inherits it.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: connects the platform and lives with what it gives.
- **Musician**: read-only, and affected — a thin connector means a thin drop and a vague statement about their own money.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: None internally — the foreign platform is authoritative for its own numbers by definition.
2. **Trigger chain**: Connector fetches → mapping transforms → count updates (19.05.01). A fetch failure must not silently freeze the count at a stale value that looks current — that is worse than an obvious gap.
3. **Permission intersection**: The venue's credentials on the foreign platform gate everything. WeJammin acts as the venue, not as itself.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A broken connector must reach the Operator, because a stale count silently feeds a drop that a Musician then reads as current.
5. **State transition conflict**: A fetch landing during a box office close (19.05.05) mutates a count that is being frozen.

### CX-02: Ingestion & Mapping ↔ Discrepancy & Reconciliation

**Relationship**: The nasty one. Mapping a foreign model onto ours loses fidelity — their tier structure is not ours, their fee model is not ours, their comps may not be distinguished from their sales at all. When the resulting number disagrees with WeJammin's own observation (the scanned count from the door, if we have one), the disagreement has two possible causes that look identical: **their number is wrong**, or **our mapping is wrong**. Reconciliation cannot tell them apart without knowing what the mapping did.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: adjudicates a disagreement whose cause may be our own code.
- **Musician**: sees a disputed number that determines their money.
- **Producer** / **Fan**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The canonical count (19.05.01). Ingestion writes to it with a source; reconciliation adjudicates when the sources disagree.
2. **Trigger chain**: Ingest → count → compare against scanned → discrepancy → adjudication → possibly a mapping correction that retroactively changes history.
3. **Permission intersection**: Adjudication authority is settlement-adjacent, not box-office-adjacent — deciding which count is true decides what is paid.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A discrepancy above a threshold must reach both parties before the statement is certified, not after.
5. **State transition conflict**: A mapping fix changes previously-ingested numbers. If a drop (19.05.02) already went out with the old mapping, the drop is immutable (19.05.02 D-01) and now disagrees with the count — correctly, because it is a record of what was believed on Tuesday.

### CX-03: Manual Entry ↔ Discrepancy & Reconciliation

**Relationship**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Confirmed at breadth: reconciliation on an API-sourced count is arithmetic — fetch again and diff. Reconciliation on a manually-entered count is impossible in the same sense, because there is no source to re-fetch; the human is the source. The check degrades from "do the numbers agree" to "who said this, when, and does the counterparty accept it" — which is attestation, and which is the platform's actual competence (D-18).

**Role scoping**: **Operator** attests; **Musician** accepts or disputes.

### CX-04: Connectors ↔ Manual Entry

**Relationship**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Confirmed at breadth: these are two ways of filling the same slot, and manual entry is the majority path rather than the exception (parent D-02). A show's count comes from a connector or from a human, and the system's job is to be equally honest about both — including being clear that the human-entered number is not less trustworthy for being typed, only differently trustworthy.

## Rejected Pairs

| # | Source | Target | Reason for Rejection |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------|
| R-01 | 19.07.01 Connectors | 19.07.03 Discrepancy & Reconciliation | Considered — surely the connector is implicated when numbers disagree? Rejected: the connector's job ends at delivering what the foreign platform said. Whether that number is *right* is not a connector concern, and whether it matches ours is a mapping (CX-02) and reconciliation question. A connector that faithfully delivers a wrong number is working correctly. Conflating fetch-fidelity with count-correctness would put retry logic and adjudication in the same feature. |
| R-02 | 19.07.02 Ingestion & Mapping | 19.07.04 Manual Entry | Tempting, because a manual entry also needs mapping onto our model. Rejected: a manually-entered count is entered *in our model already* — the human reads their dashboard and types our fields. There is no foreign schema to transform, which is precisely what makes manual entry lossless where a thin connector is lossy, and is a genuinely counter-intuitive advantage worth stating (see 19.07.04 DT-02). |
