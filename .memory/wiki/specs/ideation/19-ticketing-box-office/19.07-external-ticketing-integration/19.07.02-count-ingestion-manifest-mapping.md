# Feature: Count Ingestion & Manifest Mapping

> **Number**: 19.07.02
> **Parent**: [External Ticketing Integration & Count Reconciliation](./19.07-external-ticketing-integration-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Taking what a foreign platform said and turning it into numbers in WeJammin's model. Their "General Admission" is our price level; their "booking fee" may bundle three of our fee types; their sold count may or may not include comps; their gross may or may not include tax.

This is where **fidelity is lost**, and lost fidelity does not announce itself — it presents downstream as a count discrepancy (19.07.03), indistinguishable from the foreign platform being wrong. Every mapping decision is therefore a small, permanent, invisible assumption about someone's money.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees where their number came from, when, and at what fidelity. Not because they will operate anything, but because a settlement computed from an ingested count is only trustworthy if its provenance is visible. Without this the platform has replaced "trust the venue" with "trust the venue's integration", which is not progress. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Defines the mapping from their platform's model to their show's manifest, reviews what was ingested, and corrects a mapping when it turns out wrong. |
| Fan | None | Never sees this. |

## Behavior

Ingestion takes a connector's payload (19.07.01) and writes into the canonical count (19.05.01) with a **source** and a **freshness** on every number (parent D-04).

Mapping is the transform, and it has three shapes depending on the connector's capability tier:

- **Full manifest** → tier-to-price-level mapping, fee decomposition, comp separation. Rich, and rich in ways to be subtly wrong.
- **Aggregate** → one number into paid, no tier detail. Poor, and honestly poor.
- **Scheduled export** → the same as either, permanently a day stale.

The mapping's assumptions must be **explicit and inspectable**, because they will need to be questioned when a discrepancy appears.

### Happy Path

1. Connector delivers a payload for a matched show (19.07.01).
2. System presents the foreign model's structure alongside WeJammin's manifest and asks the Operator to map: their tiers to our price levels, their fee lines to ours.
3. System states what it *cannot* map and what that costs — "their export does not distinguish comps, so your comp count will be zero and your paid count includes them".
4. Operator confirms the mapping; it binds to the connection, not to the show, so it is done once.
5. Counts flow: paid, comp and gross land in 19.05.01 with source `DICE` and a fetch timestamp.
6. Every consumer downstream (drop, statement, pacing) can say where the number came from and how fresh it is.
7. A mapping error discovered later is corrected; the count re-derives; previously-sent drops stay immutable and now legitimately disagree.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Their sold count includes comps; ours does not | Our paid count is inflated; the door split (17) overpays or underpays | **Must be stated at mapping time, not discovered at settlement.** The single most consequential mapping ambiguity |
| Their fee model does not decompose | We know gross and net but not who the fee belongs to | Fee-recipient lines on the statement (19.05.05) are unavailable; state it up front |
| Their tier does not map onto ours 1:1 | Their "GA" covers two of our price levels, or vice versa | Per-tier numbers become approximations, and approximations look like discrepancies (`#CX-02`) |
| Mapping is wrong and nobody notices | The count is confidently wrong for the whole campaign | The reason mapping assumptions must be inspectable rather than buried |
| Mapping corrected mid-campaign | Historical numbers re-derive | Drops already sent are immutable and now disagree with the count — correct, and it must be explicable rather than alarming |
| Foreign platform restates a past number | Their refund posted late | Count moves; if it moves after a certified statement, it is a settlement adjustment (19.05.05 D-06) |
| Currency differs | Foreign platform reports in a different currency | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Hybrid show — some ours, some theirs | Every counter is a blend of two sources | See parent Q-04 and 19.05.01 Q-03. The blend must remain visible, never averaged |
| Their numbers arrive during a box office close | Mutates a count being frozen | Close must quiesce ingestion too (19.05.05) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Unmapped | Connection made, mapping not defined | Mapping screen with both models side by side |
| Mapping | Operator defining it | What maps, what does not, and **what each gap costs downstream** |
| Mapped | Confirmed | Mapping bound to the connection; assumptions listed and inspectable |
| Ingesting | Payload arriving | Count updating with source and freshness |
| Degraded fidelity | Mapping cannot express something | Named explicitly on the count and on every consumer of it |
| Stale | Connector broken (19.07.01) | Visible gap, never a stale number dressed as current |
| Remapped | Mapping corrected | Count re-derives; prior drops stay immutable and legitimately differ |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | The mapping must state **what each gap costs downstream**, in the Operator's terms, at mapping time | Reasoning about who can evaluate a mapping decision and when; the Operator persona is a venue owner, not a data engineer | ✅ CONFIRMED — "their export does not distinguish comps" is a sentence with no meaning to a venue owner. "Your comp count will show zero and your paid count will include your 20 guest-list tickets, which means your door split will be computed on 20 tickets that nobody paid for" is the same fact, and it is a decision they can actually make. Mapping is presented everywhere in software as a technical exercise; here it is a **commercial** one — every unmapped field is money that will be computed wrongly, silently, for the entire campaign, and the only moment anyone will ever look at it is now. If the cost is not stated in money and in their language at this screen, it will not be understood at any later point either. |
| DT-02 | Fidelity loss and genuine discrepancy are different problems and can be handled separately | Clean-separation instinct | ❌ REJECTED — they are **indistinguishable downstream**, which is the whole difficulty (`#CX-02`). When our count says 260 and the door scanned 251, the cause is either their number being wrong, or our mapping quietly folding comps into paid. Both present identically: a number that does not add up. Reconciliation (19.07.03) therefore cannot be built as pure arithmetic over two counts — it must be able to interrogate the mapping's assumptions, which means the mapping cannot be an opaque transform buried in a connector. This is why mapping assumptions must be first-class, inspectable data rather than code: the reconciliation feature is a *consumer* of them. Separating the two concerns would produce a reconciliation feature that can detect a disagreement but never explain it, and an unexplained disagreement about money is just an argument with extra steps. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Mapping states what each gap costs downstream, in money and in the Operator's language | A venue owner cannot evaluate "comps are not distinguished"; they can evaluate "your split will be computed on 20 tickets nobody paid for" (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Mapping assumptions are first-class inspectable data, not code | Reconciliation is a consumer of them; an opaque transform produces disagreements that can be detected but never explained (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Every ingested number carries source and freshness | Otherwise a blended count averages away the disagreement it should be surfacing (parent D-04) |
| D-04 | Mapping binds to the connection, not the show | A venue maps once, not per show |
| D-05 | Correcting a mapping re-derives the count; already-sent drops stay immutable | The drop is a record of what was believed, not of what is true (19.05.02 D-01) |
