# Feature: Capacity Manifest, Holds & Allocations

> **Number**: 19.01.02
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

The manifest is the authoritative statement of what exists to be admitted: total licensed capacity, divided into priced blocks, minus everything held back from public sale. **Holds** are inventory reserved for a named party — artist hold, production hold (seats killed for a mixing desk or a camera platform), venue hold, promoter hold. This feature owns the lifecycle of every unit: held → released → sold → scanned, or killed and never sold at all.

It exists because the single most expensive routine mistake in live music is inventory that nobody released — a hold left on 30 seats for a show that "sold out" at 90% is money that evaporated silently, and the Operator persona's own anti-pattern names it: *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold."*

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Sees and manages their **own artist hold** — the block reserved for their guests, their team and their own comps. Can request release of it back to sale, and must respond to a release deadline. Cannot see the venue's or production's holds, or total remaining inventory unless the deal grants it. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface for the session-owner lens. |
| Operator | Full | Creates the manifest, sets licensed capacity, creates and releases every hold class, kills inventory, and is the only role that can raise capacity. Sees every block and every hold's owner and deadline. |
| Fan | None | Never sees the manifest. Sees only what is currently on sale — a held block and a sold-out block are indistinguishable to them, which is deliberate and is also the mechanism by which a forgotten hold hides. |

## Behavior

Licensed capacity is a hard ceiling supplied by the room (domain 16) and is a legal/safety limit, not a business preference. The manifest allocates that capacity across price levels (19.01.01), then reserves subsets as holds. Each hold has an **owner** (a party), a **quantity or seat set**, a **reason**, and a **release deadline** — the date by which unclaimed held inventory automatically returns to public sale.

Killed inventory is capacity removed from sale permanently for a physical reason (production sightlines, sound desk, accessibility circulation) and never returns.

### Happy Path

1. Operator confirms the show's licensed capacity, defaulted from the room's record in domain 16.
2. System derives sellable inventory: capacity minus kills.
3. Operator creates holds — e.g. artist hold 20, production kill 12, venue hold 10 — each with an owner and a release deadline.
4. System shows public-sale inventory as the remainder, per price level, and flags any hold with no deadline set.
5. On-sale runs; the held blocks are invisible to Fans.
6. At each hold's release deadline, the system prompts the hold owner: claim, partially claim, or release. Unclaimed inventory returns to public sale automatically.
7. Manifest reaches show day with holds resolved; the remaining count is what the door and the settlement work from.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Hold never released, show sells out with inventory still held | The failure this feature exists to prevent. Automatic deadline release is the mitigation; a hold with no deadline is the defect | Held-inventory warning surfaced on the live count (19.05.01) once public inventory drops below a threshold — the alert must fire while there is still time to sell |
| Operator raises licensed capacity above the room's licensed figure | Rejected — capacity is a safety limit from domain 16, not an input here | Blocking error citing the room's licensed capacity and its source |
| Hold released while a timed tier has advanced | Released inventory needs a price decision; see `#CX-01` | Prompt naming both candidate prices before release completes |
| Hold owner is unreachable at the deadline | Auto-release fires; the hold owner is notified after the fact, not asked | Musician sees "your hold was released on [date] — you did not respond by the deadline" with the released quantity |
| Two Operator staff release the same hold concurrently | Race on a money-bearing count | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Show is reserved-seating and a hold names specific seats | Releasing scatters singles across the map, damaging adjacency for remaining buyers | `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Capacity reduced after tickets sold (licence change, production growth) | Oversold state — some sold tickets must be bought back or upgraded | `[PENDING]` — this is a genuine incident flow, not an edge case; needs Step 5 |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Show created, no manifest | Empty state with the room's capacity and template offered |
| Draft | Manifest built, not on sale | Editable block/hold view with a live "public inventory" figure |
| Live | On sale | Read-mostly view; hold edits permitted with warnings; every change is audited |
| Deadline due | A hold's release deadline arrives | Hold owner sees a claim/release prompt; Operator sees pending-release status |
| Constrained | Public inventory near zero while holds remain | Prominent held-inventory alert to the Operator naming each unreleased hold and its owner |
| Closed | Box office closed (19.05.05) | Manifest frozen; becomes an input to settlement |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Every hold must carry a **mandatory release deadline** with automatic release on expiry | Operator anti-persona: *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold"*; the Producer anti-persona principle that *"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — a hold without a deadline is an unbounded liability, and the persona doc explicitly names forgetting as the failure. Deadline-by-default with auto-release makes forgetting harmless. This is the highest-value design call in the sub-domain. |
| DT-02 | The held-inventory alert must fire on **remaining public inventory**, not on a date | Reasoning about when the Operator can still act | ✅ CONFIRMED — a date-based reminder fires when the Operator is not thinking about the show. An alert at "public inventory below 10%, 30 tickets still held" fires exactly when release converts to revenue. A domain expert would expect this and the sweep did not name it. |
| DT-03 | Kills and holds are the same mechanism with different flags | Modelling instinct — both remove inventory from sale | ❌ REJECTED — they have opposite lifecycles. A hold *wants* to be released and its default end-state is public sale; a kill must never return and its release would seat someone behind a sound desk or in a fire lane. Collapsing them makes auto-release (DT-01) dangerous: the mechanism designed to recover forgotten holds would cheerfully sell the mixing-desk kill. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Every hold requires a release deadline; auto-release on expiry is the default | Directly targets the Operator persona's named accidental failure (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Kills and holds are distinct entities, not one entity with a flag | Opposite default end-states; conflating them makes auto-release unsafe (DT-03) |
| D-03 | Licensed capacity is read-only here, sourced from domain 16 | It is a safety/licensing limit, not a box-office preference |
