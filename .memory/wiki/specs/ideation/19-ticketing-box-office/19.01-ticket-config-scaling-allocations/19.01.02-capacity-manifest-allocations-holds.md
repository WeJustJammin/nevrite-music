# Feature: Capacity Manifest, Holds & Allocations

> **Number**: 19.01.02
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-23

## Overview

The manifest is the authoritative statement of what exists to be admitted: total licensed capacity, divided into priced blocks, minus everything held back from public sale. **Holds** are inventory reserved for a named party — artist hold, production hold (seats killed for a mixing desk or a camera platform), venue hold, promoter hold. This feature owns the lifecycle of every unit: held → released → sold → **admitted**, or killed and never sold at all. The terminal step is *admitted*, not *scanned*: `scanned` is the gate-observed counter only, and a walk-up unit sold at the window is admitted at birth without ever being scanned (19.05.01 D-09, 19.05.04 D-01). Both channels land in the derived all-bodies total `admissions_total`.

It exists because the single most expensive routine mistake in live music is inventory that nobody released — a hold left on 30 seats for a show that "sold out" at 90% is money that evaporated silently, and the Operator persona's own anti-pattern names it: *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold."*

The second thing it exists for is less obvious and matters more to the platform thesis. A hold is **capacity withheld from sale**, and on a door-split deal every withheld unit is money the artist did not make. Today the artist cannot see holds at all, so a room that held 40 and sold 260 is indistinguishable from a room that sold out at 300. That is the settlement asymmetry of `17.03.02 DT-01` — *"the promoter knows the room's economics and the artist does not"* — expressed as inventory rather than as arithmetic. This feature is where it is either removed or preserved.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Config | Owns and manages their **artist hold** — the block reserved for their guests, their team and their own comps, and the block 19.03 draws the guest list from. Responds to its release deadline (claim / partially claim / release). Sees, on their own show only, the **held aggregate broken down by class** — "40 held: 20 artist, 12 production kill, 8 venue" — because withheld capacity is settlement-relevant to them (D-07). Does **not** see hold owners' identities, guest names, or the reason text on another party's hold. Cannot re-price released inventory (`#CX-01`) and cannot release a hold they do not own. Where the Musician self-promotes, they hold Operator-Full as well — and the role collapse suspends nothing (D-16). |
| Producer | None | Not visible, and not a gap. The Producer's value in `meta/personas.md` sits at the **session**, and a manifest presupposes a box office, which a session does not have. A Producer running a private playback or invite-only listening session reaches admission through **19.11 RSVP**, which has no manifest, no price levels and no holds — its capacity control is an invite list, not an inventory ledger. |
| Operator | Full | Confirms licensed capacity from the room configuration (domain 16), instantiates the manifest, creates and releases every hold class, sets and edits deadlines, kills inventory and reverses kills (D-08), resolves oversold incidents (D-12), and is the only role that can act on another party's hold. Sees every block, every hold's owner, reason, deadline and full audit trail. Cannot raise capacity above the configuration's licensed figure (D-03). |
| Fan | None | Never sees the manifest, the held count, or the block structure. A held block and a sold-out block are indistinguishable to them — deliberate, and consistent with `19.05.01 D-04` (the count is never exposed to Fans; *"12 left!"* is scarcity marketing hostile to the persona and usually untrue once holds are counted). Their only contact with this feature is indirect: when a hold releases at or after sell-out, they receive a **waitlist** notification (19.02.05) they will never attribute to a hold (D-09). |

## Behavior

### The invariant

Licensed capacity is a hard ceiling supplied by the **room configuration** in domain 16 (per-configuration, not per-room — `19.01.04 DT-02`). It is a legal and safety limit, not a business preference, and this feature can only read it (D-03).

Every unit of that capacity is in exactly one state at every instant. The manifest's core invariant, checked at the moment of every mutation:

```
licensed capacity  =  killed + held + comp + paid + carted + remaining
```

| Bucket | Meaning | Who moves it |
|---|---|---|
| `killed` | Removed from sale for a physical reason; does not return automatically (D-02, D-08) | Operator, or a production advance from domain 18 |
| `held` | Reserved for a named party, unsold, with a deadline | Operator creates; owner or Operator releases; the deadline releases (D-01) |
| `comp` | Issued free, inventory spent, paid unchanged (`19.03.02 D-02`) | 19.03 guest list / comp issuance |
| `paid` | Sold, money received. The settlement number | 19.02 on-sale, 19.05.04 walk-up |
| `carted` | Reserved to a buyer mid-checkout (`19.02.04`) | Cart hold create/expire |
| `remaining` | Sellable right now | Derived — never written directly |

**Any operation that would break the identity is rejected at the moment of the attempt, not reconciled afterwards.** The counters themselves are owned and reported by `19.05.01`; the manifest is the state, the live count is its reporting face.

### Holds

A hold has five mandatory properties. There is no valid hold missing any of them:

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | A party — a Musician, a band **entity** (`personas.md`: bands are first-class entities, not personas), the venue, production, or a promoter. Never blank. |
| **Class** | `artist` · `venue` · `production` · `promoter` · `platform`. Class is what the Musician sees (D-07); owner identity is not. |
| **Quantity or seat set** | GA holds a count (fungible). Reserved holds named seat identities (`#CX-03`). Mixed holds one or the other per section, never a blended figure. |
| **Price level** | Exactly one (D-11). A hold spanning two price levels is two holds. |
| **Release deadline** | Mandatory (D-01). Stored relative to the show's doors time, in the venue's local timezone (D-05). |

**Default deadlines** are proposed at creation and are Operator-editable:

| Hold shape | Default deadline | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Artist hold bound to a guest-list allocation | The guest-list close (owned by `19.03.01`) | The hold *is* the guest-list budget; releasing it earlier strands the artist's guests |
| Any other hold | **Doors − 14 days** | Released inventory needs a promotional cycle to convert. A release with no marketing window is a release in name only |
| Floor | **Doors − 48h**, warned not blocked (D-14, DT-08) | Inventory that cannot be marketed cannot be sold; a deadline inside 48h is functionally a kill, and the system says so rather than pretending the release has value |

**Auto-release** fires at the deadline whether or not anyone is present. It is not consent-gated (DT-13) — the deadline's entire purpose is to work when the humans do not. The owner is prompted **7 days and 24 hours before** the deadline, and notified *after* the release, not asked.

**Auto-release is structurally excluded** from two blocks, for opposite reasons:

- **Accessible inventory** (19.01.05) — auto-releasing accessible positions to general sale on a date is the precise unlawful act (`19.01.05 DT-01`). The block is exempt by construction, not by a flag someone can clear.
- **Externally-ticketed shows** (19.07) — WeJammin does not control the incumbent's inventory, so a release it cannot effect would produce a manifest that lies (D-10, DT-06).

### Kills

Killed inventory is capacity removed from sale for a **physical** reason — production sightlines, sound desk, camera platform, accessibility circulation, barricade. It has the opposite lifecycle to a hold: a hold's default end-state is public sale; a kill's default end-state is nothing at all.

A kill **never auto-returns** (D-02). It may be **manually reversed by the Operator before doors, with a stated reason and full attribution** (D-08) — because production changes and the camera that justified 12 dead seats sometimes does not come. Reversal is an act with a name on it, never a sweep.

### The held-inventory alert

The alert that makes DT-01's deadline safe fires on **remaining inventory, not on a date** (DT-02) — a date-based reminder arrives when the Operator is not thinking about the show; a remaining-based alert arrives exactly when release converts to revenue.

| Trigger | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `remaining ≤ max(10% of sellable, 20 units)` **and** `held > 0` | Alert fires to the Operator, naming each unreleased hold, its class, its owner and its deadline |
| Still unresolved | Re-fires daily until `held = 0` or doors |
| `remaining = 0` **and** `held > 0` | Re-fires at highest prominence — this is a sell-out with money left on the table, the exact failure the feature exists for |

The `max(…, 20)` floor exists because 10% of a 60-capacity room is 6 tickets, which is far too late to act on. Surfaced by `19.05.01`; specified here (`ticketing-box-office-cx.md#CX-03`).

### Happy Path

1. Operator selects the show's room configuration (19.01.04); system reads the configuration's **licensed capacity** from domain 16 and presents it read-only with its source and licence reference.
2. System instantiates the manifest and derives sellable inventory as `capacity − killed`, with `killed` pre-populated from the room configuration's standing kills (permanent obstructions) and the accessible block carved out per 19.01.05.
3. **Where an executed booking contract exists** (17.04), system pre-populates hold classes from its structured terms — "artist gets 20 comps" arrives as a 20-unit artist hold bound to the guest-list allocation — and marks each as contract-derived, citing the contract clause (D-04). Where no contract exists, the Operator keys holds directly.
4. Operator reviews or creates each hold: owner, class, quantity or seat set, price level, reason, deadline. System proposes the default deadline per the table above and blocks save on any hold missing owner, class, price level or deadline.
5. System validates the invariant. Public inventory is shown as the remainder **per price level**, alongside gross potential (19.01.01) recomputed for the held-back state — the number the deal in domain 17 is measured against.
6. Manifest locks; on-sale runs (19.02). Held blocks are invisible to Fans. Counters initialise in 19.05.01.
7. At `doors − 7 days` and `doors − 24h` relative to each hold's deadline, system prompts the hold owner: claim all, claim part, or release. The Musician's prompt states what the unclaimed portion is worth to them at their split (`19.03.02 D-03` pattern — the information is only useful while the decision is still open).
8. At the deadline, unclaimed inventory auto-releases to public sale at the price resolved by `#CX-01`. `held` falls, `remaining` rises, and the owner is notified of what was released and when.
9. If `remaining` crosses the alert threshold with holds outstanding, the Operator is alerted with each hold named — while there is still time to sell.
10. Manifest reaches show day with `held = 0`. The remaining count is what the door (19.04) and the certified statement (19.05.05) work from.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Hold never released; show sells out with inventory still held** | The failure this feature exists to prevent. Mandatory deadline + auto-release (D-01) is the mitigation; a hold with no deadline is structurally impossible, not merely discouraged | Held-inventory alert on the live count (19.05.01) at `remaining ≤ max(10%, 20)`, re-firing daily and again at `remaining = 0`, naming every unreleased hold, its class, owner and deadline |
| **Operator raises capacity above the configuration's licensed figure** | Rejected. Capacity is a safety limit from domain 16 (D-03), and overselling a fire capacity is a criminal matter, not yield management (DT-14) | Blocking error citing the configuration's licensed capacity, the attempted figure, the overage, and the licence reference it came from |
| **Two Operator staff release the same hold concurrently** | Release is idempotent against the hold's observed state. The first commits; the second is rejected on the stale state and **never applies the quantity twice** — double-crediting inventory would break the invariant and oversell the room. Fails closed | Second actor sees a conflict naming who released it, when, and the quantity released, with the current state rendered — not a generic "try again" |
| **Reserved hold released partially, scattering singles** | Partial release of a contiguous reserved block releases **from the block's edges inward** (D-13), preserving contiguity of both the released seats and the retained hold. The system does not re-seat sold buyers — it cannot | Operator sees which seats release and the retained block highlighted; the orphan-seat rule applies at *purchase* selection (19.01.04), not at release |
| **Released seats leave an orphan single between sold blocks** | Not preventable at release — the seats are physically real and withholding a saleable seat to protect a hypothetical pair risks selling neither. Handled at selection by the seat-picker's best-available-contiguous rule | Fan is offered contiguous sets first; single seats remain purchasable |
| **Hold released while a timed tier has advanced** | Released inventory needs a price decision (`#CX-01`). The release resolves price against the tier state **at the instant of release**. If a tier advance is scheduled inside the release confirmation window, the release re-resolves or aborts — it never completes at an undefined price | Prompt naming both candidate prices and which will apply, before release completes. Auto-release resolves to the *current public tier* with the resolution recorded |
| **Price resolution is ambiguous, so auto-release cannot complete** | Hold enters **Release-blocked** — it does **not** silently remain held. Failure is loud, because a silently-stuck hold is the original bug wearing the fix's clothes | Operator alerted immediately with the hold, the ambiguity, and the two candidate resolutions; hold shows `Release-blocked`, not `Held` |
| **Hold owner is unreachable at the deadline** | Auto-release fires. The owner is notified after the fact, not asked (DT-13) — an unreachable owner at the deadline is the *common* case, not the exception | Musician sees "Your hold was released on [date] — you did not respond by [deadline]", with the released quantity, its price level, and what it is now worth to them |
| **Hold owner is a band entity with no authorised responder** | The prompt addresses the entity; auto-release fires regardless. Notification fans out to **every** member with visibility, not only to a nominated contact — an entity with a stale nominee is exactly how the deadline gets missed | Every band member with show visibility sees the pre-deadline prompt and the post-release notice |
| **Musician self-promotes: same human is Operator and hold owner** | Role collapse suspends nothing (D-16). Auto-release still fires, the alert still surfaces, and both are addressed to the same human in both lenses. A safeguard that only works when two people are present is not a safeguard in a market where one person routinely is both | The self-promoting Musician sees the artist-hold prompt in the Musician lens and the held-inventory alert in the Operator lens — deliberately not de-duplicated |
| **Hold owner claims more than their hold** (artist wants 25 against a 20 hold) | The excess draws from **public** inventory and is an explicit Operator decision with attribution, never an automatic top-up. Not blocked — the answer is genuinely sometimes yes (domain mechanism: *attribution, not prevention*) | Operator sees the overdraw, its cost at face value, and must name a reason; the excess is recorded against the Operator, not the artist hold. Door-time over-allocation is 19.03.03's flow |
| **Hold created for more than remaining public inventory** | Rejected. A hold cannot be created from inventory that is already sold, comped or carted | Blocking error naming remaining, requested, and the shortfall |
| **Hold created during a live on-sale draws on carted inventory** | A hold draws only from **unreserved** `remaining` — never from `carted`. A buyer mid-checkout is not inventory to be taken back | Operator sees a lower available figure than `remaining` during on-sale, with carted quantity stated |
| **Very large hold** (e.g. 60% of the room for a corporate buyout or a private hire) | **Not blocked** (D-14, DT-09). Legitimate reasons exist and a product that blocks gets switched off. The controls are structural: the deadline is mandatory (D-01) and the class and quantity are visible to the artist (D-07) | Operator states a reason; the artist's class-level view shows `venue: 180`. No approval gate |
| **Operator hides inventory in a large venue hold, then sells it as door cash off the settlement** | The Operator anti-persona's *worst intentional*, expressed as inventory. Made structurally uneconomic rather than detected: the class-level held aggregate is visible to the artist (D-07), the alert names each hold, and walk-up sales at the door move `paid`, which the certified statement (19.05.05) reconciles against the manifest | Musician sees `venue: 180 held` before doors, not a sold-out show. Any subsequent movement of that inventory lands in `paid` and appears on the statement |
| **Capacity reduced after tickets sold** (licence varied, production grew, licence lapsed) | Incident flow, not an edit (D-12). Resolution ladder, in order, absorbing as much as possible at each rung before descending: **(1)** reduce `remaining`; **(2)** kill unreleased `held` — the venue's and artist's own inventory is taken before any fan's; **(3)** solicit **volunteers** among paid holders (refund or upgrade + incentive), window of 72h or until doors, whichever is sooner; **(4)** involuntary unwind, **reverse-chronological** (last bought, first unwound) — non-arbitrary, explainable, and the only rule that does not require someone to choose between named fans. Routes to 19.06.01 for the money; notifies domain 17 because gross potential moved and a guarantee may no longer be viable | Operator sees the ladder with the delta absorbed at each rung and how many sold tickets remain to unwind. Affected Fans see a plain statement of what happened and their options — never a silent void. Musician is notified that capacity and gross potential changed, with the new figures |
| **Capacity increased after on-sale** (licence upgraded, seats out for standing) | New inventory is real and sellable, but it enters at or near sell-out — so it routes to the **waitlist** (19.02.05), never to silent open sale (D-09, DT-07) | Waitlisted Fans are offered in waitlist order; Operator sees the added capacity and its source licence |
| **Kill reversed and inventory returns at sell-out** | Same rule — un-killed inventory routes to the waitlist, not to open sale. A silent release at sell-out is a stealth micro-on-sale that rewards refreshers and bots, contradicting `19.02.04 D-01`'s structural-fairness posture | Operator states the reversal reason; waitlist is offered in order; nothing appears on the public storefront unannounced |
| **Late production kill lands on already-sold seats** | Real and routine (DT-11) — FOH decides the desk position at load-in, weeks after on-sale. Sold inventory cannot be killed. Reserved: system offers **relocation** to equivalent-or-better seats at no charge, and the buyer is told before they travel. GA: the kill reduces sellable capacity and, if that pushes the room oversold, enters the D-12 ladder | Operator sees the collision listed seat-by-seat with the sold buyers named and a relocation proposal per seat. Fan receives a relocation notice stating the new seat and why, not a cancellation |
| **Production kill lands on the accessible block or its circulation** | Blocked without an equivalent replacement position (`19.01.05`) — a capacity reduction that lands entirely on disabled fans is not a production decision | Blocking error; escalates to the Operator with the replacement requirement stated |
| **Auto-release sweep reaches an accessible position** | Structurally impossible. Accessible inventory is exempt by construction, not by a clearable flag (`#CX-04`, `19.01.05 DT-01`) — the mechanism that recovers a forgotten hold must never perform the unlawful act | Operator sees accessible inventory in a distinct block with its own conditional-release flow and statutory test stated |
| **Show is externally ticketed** (19.07) | The manifest is a **shadow** record: holds are records, not controls, and auto-release is disabled (D-10, DT-06). Deadlines still prompt the owner and the alert still fires — WeJammin can tell the Operator to release, it just cannot do it for them | Manifest banners `Shadow — [incumbent] controls this inventory`. Prompts read "release this hold in [incumbent]" and ask for confirmation that it was done; the confirmation is attributable, not provable (`19.07.04 D-01`) |
| **Show is rescheduled** (19.06.03) | Deadlines are relative to doors in venue-local time (D-05), so a reschedule **re-derives** them against the new doors and fires nothing. Absolute timestamps would put every deadline in the past and auto-release the entire manifest at the instant of reschedule | Operator sees each hold's recomputed deadline before confirming the reschedule; hold owners are notified of the new deadline, not of a release |
| **Show is cancelled** (19.06.02) | Manifest freezes at its state at cancellation. Holds are moot and do not release — releasing inventory for a show that will not happen is nonsense. The frozen state is an input to the mass-refund reconciliation | Manifest read-only, marked `Frozen — cancelled [date]`, with the held/killed/paid/comp breakdown preserved as evidence |
| **Deadline falls after doors, or a hold survives to doors** | Auto-release evaluation stops at doors. A release after the show has started sells nothing and corrupts the count the door is working from | Hold shown as `Unresolved at doors` on the certified statement (19.05.05) as a distinct line — the money nobody decided to lose, recorded rather than hidden |
| **Deadline computed in UTC fires at the wrong local hour** | Prevented by D-05 — deadlines evaluate in the **venue's** local timezone. `Doors − 14 days` on a show that crosses a DST boundary re-derives against local wall-clock doors, not against a fixed offset | Deadline is displayed in venue-local time with the timezone named, and in the viewer's local time alongside it |
| **Price level deleted while it holds inventory** (19.01.01) | Deletion is blocked while any hold, cart or sale references the level. An orphaned hold has no price to release at, which is `#CX-01`'s money incident by another route | Blocking error naming the holds bound to the level, with a re-home flow — reassign the holds to another level, then delete |
| **Ticket refunded after being released from a hold** (19.06.01) | Refunded inventory returns to **public** inventory, never to the hold it originated from (D-15). Otherwise every refund re-holds inventory and the manifest ratchets shut over the life of the on-sale | Operator sees `remaining +1`; the original hold's state is unchanged and remains released |
| **Room configuration has no licensed capacity in domain 16** | Manifest cannot be instantiated. The capacity is not guessable and a guessed capacity is a licensing breach | Blocking empty state naming the missing datum and linking to the room configuration in domain 16 (parent index Q-12) |
| **Room has a capacity figure but not per-configuration** (`19.01.04 DT-02`) | GA manifest can be built against the single figure with an explicit warning; reserved/mixed cannot, because the seated figure and the standing figure are different licences and being wrong is a safety breach | Partial state: capacity shown with `Source: room-level, not configuration-level — confirm before on-sale` and configuration-level entry offered |
| **Licence lapses or is varied between manifest build and doors** | The most common real cause of the D-12 capacity-reduction incident. The manifest is built on a licence with a validity window; a lapse invalidates the ceiling the whole manifest rests on | Operator alerted at the lapse with the affected capacity and the D-12 ladder pre-computed. Requires domain 16 to expose licence validity, not just a number (Q-05) |
| **Contract-derived hold edited by the Operator** | Permitted, but it is a **divergence from an executed contract** (D-04). Editing a 20-comp artist hold down to 10 is a term change, not a box-office edit — the divergence is flagged and routed to 17.04's amendment flow | Operator sees the contract clause, the contracted value, the attempted value, and a required reason. Musician is notified that a contract-derived term diverged, with both values |
| **No booking contract exists at all** (handshake club show) | The majority path in the target market, not an error. Holds are keyed directly and marked `no contract of record` — honest rather than absent | Operator keys holds normally; the manifest states the terms have no contract behind them, which is a fact the settlement (17.09) will need |
| **Hold quantity manipulated to break the invariant** (malformed or hostile input) | Rejected at the attempt. The invariant is checked on every mutation, not reconciled on a schedule — a manifest that is briefly wrong is a room that is briefly oversold | Blocking error stating the identity, the attempted values, and which term failed |
| **Release requested by a party who owns neither the hold nor the box office** | Rejected. Release authority is the hold's owner or the Operator. Holding inventory confers no rights over anyone else's (`#CX-01` permission intersection) | Not offered in the UI; a direct attempt is refused and recorded in the show's audit trail |
| **Hold owner's account is deleted or leaves the platform** | The hold is owned by a **party**, not a login. The hold survives; the deadline survives; auto-release survives. An orphaned hold with no responder simply auto-releases, which is the correct outcome | Operator sees `Owner unreachable — will auto-release [date]` on the hold |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Manifest opened; capacity and configuration being read from domain 16 | Skeleton of the block/hold table with the capacity figure and its source resolving last — the figure is never rendered until its source and licence reference are known, because an unattributed capacity number is the thing being fixed |
| **Empty** | Show created, no manifest | Empty state showing the room configuration's licensed capacity, its standing kills, its accessible block, and a "build from configuration" CTA. If capacity is missing, this is Error, not Empty |
| **Draft** | Manifest built, not on sale | Editable block/hold view with live `remaining` per price level, gross potential, the invariant rendered as a visible identity, and a blocking flag on any hold missing owner, class, price level or deadline |
| **Live** | On sale | Read-mostly. Hold create/release permitted with warnings; capacity and configuration locked; every change audited with actor, time and reason. Available-to-hold shown separately from `remaining` because `carted` is untouchable |
| **Deadline due** | A hold's deadline arrives (or `−7d` / `−24h` prompts fire) | Hold owner sees claim / claim-part / release with the value of the unclaimed portion at their split. Operator sees pending-release status per hold |
| **Release-blocked** | Auto-release could not resolve a price (`#CX-01`), or the target price level was deleted | Loud alert to the Operator naming the hold, the ambiguity and the candidate resolutions. Shown as `Release-blocked`, never as `Held` — a stuck release must not look like a normal hold |
| **Constrained** | `remaining ≤ max(10% of sellable, 20)` while `held > 0` | Prominent held-inventory alert naming every unreleased hold, its class, owner, deadline and face value. Re-fires daily; escalates at `remaining = 0` |
| **Oversold** | `paid + comp > capacity − killed` after a capacity reduction or a late kill | Incident view, not an edit view. Shows the delta, the D-12 ladder with the amount absorbable at each rung, and the count of sold tickets that would need unwinding. Blocks further sale immediately |
| **Shadow** | Show is externally ticketed (19.07) | Manifest rendered read-only with `Shadow — [incumbent] controls this inventory`. Holds are records; auto-release is off; prompts ask the Operator to act in the incumbent and confirm |
| **Partial** | Capacity present but not per-configuration; or ingested counts (19.07) are stale; or the accessible block is undefined | Populated view with an explicit incompleteness banner naming the missing datum and what it blocks. Never a silently-degraded figure — `19.05.01 D-02` requires every counter to carry source and freshness |
| **Frozen** | Show cancelled (19.06.02) | Read-only manifest at its cancellation state, held/killed/paid/comp preserved as evidence for the mass refund |
| **Closed** | Box office closed (19.05.05) | Manifest frozen and becomes an input to settlement. Any hold unresolved at doors appears as its own line on the certified statement |
| **Error** | Invariant violation attempted, capacity missing, or domain 16 unreachable | Blocking error naming the failing term or the missing source. No partial write — a half-applied manifest mutation is an oversold room |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Every hold must carry a **mandatory release deadline** with automatic release on expiry | Operator anti-persona: *"forgets to release a hold, blocking a slot they'd happily have sold"*; the Producer anti-persona principle that *"the design must make the lazy path the correct path"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — a hold without a deadline is an unbounded liability, and the persona doc explicitly names forgetting as the failure. Deadline-by-default with auto-release makes forgetting harmless. This is the highest-value design call in the sub-domain, and the domain's first characteristic mechanism (`ticketing-box-office-cx.md` — deadline-with-automatic-resolution). |
| DT-02 | The held-inventory alert must fire on **remaining public inventory**, not on a date | Reasoning about when the Operator can still act | ✅ CONFIRMED — a date-based reminder fires when the Operator is not thinking about the show. An alert at "public inventory below 10%, 30 tickets still held" fires exactly when release converts to revenue. A domain expert would expect this and the sweep did not name it. Deepened with a `max(10%, 20)` floor: 10% of a 60-cap room is 6 tickets, which is too late to sell. |
| DT-03 | Kills and holds are the same mechanism with different flags | Modelling instinct — both remove inventory from sale | ❌ REJECTED — they have opposite lifecycles. A hold *wants* to be released and its default end-state is public sale; a kill must never return and its release would seat someone behind a sound desk or in a fire lane. Collapsing them makes auto-release (DT-01) dangerous: the mechanism designed to recover forgotten holds would cheerfully sell the mixing-desk kill. |
| DT-04 | **Held inventory must be visible to the Musician on their own show, at class level** | The thesis (D-18) is that the platform removes reconstruction and asymmetry; `17.03.02 DT-01` states the asymmetry outright — *"the promoter knows the room's economics and the artist does not"*; the domain index says settlement asymmetry *"runs one direction"* | ✅ CONFIRMED, with scoping. A hold is capacity withheld from sale, and on a door split every withheld unit is money the artist did not make — so a room that held 40 and sold 260 is, to an artist who cannot see holds, identical to a sell-out at 300. **That indistinguishability is not a UI limitation; it is the asymmetry.** But full transparency over-corrects: a venue's hold for a sponsor or a corporate negotiation is legitimately confidential at the *identity* level. Resolution: **class-level aggregate visible, identity-level detail not.** "40 held: 20 artist, 12 production, 8 venue" is settlement-relevant; "8 held for a named buyer" is not. This is a constraint discovered here that lands on `19.05.01`, whose Musician lens currently reads *"scoped to what their deal grants"*. |
| DT-05 | Hold deadlines must be stored **relative to the show's doors time**, in venue-local time — never as absolute timestamps | Reasoning about the intersection with 19.06.03 reschedule | ✅ CONFIRMED — **and this is the failure mode nobody designs for.** Every deadline is naturally expressed as "two weeks before the show". Stored absolutely, a reschedule to a later date leaves every deadline in the past, and the next auto-release sweep releases the entire manifest at the instant of the reschedule — including the artist's guest-list budget for a show that has not happened. The reschedule flow would fire the safety mechanism as a weapon. Relative storage makes reschedule a re-derivation that fires nothing. Venue-local (not UTC) because doors is a wall-clock fact and DST is real. |
| DT-06 | On externally-ticketed shows (19.07), auto-release must be **structurally disabled**, not merely inert | Reasoning about the intersection with 19.07; parent index Q-05 (*"the gate-observed `scanned` count is the only count WeJammin independently observes"*) | ✅ CONFIRMED — WeJammin does not control the incumbent's inventory. An auto-release that fires against a shadow manifest moves `held → remaining` in a ledger that has no authority over the actual box office: the platform then reports inventory as sellable that the incumbent still has held. **A manifest that lies is worse than a manifest that is honestly incomplete.** The deadline and the alert still have value — WeJammin can tell the Operator to go and release it — but the release itself becomes attributable, not provable (`19.07.04 D-01`), which is the domain's second characteristic mechanism applied to inventory. |
| DT-07 | Inventory entering public availability at or after sell-out must route to the **waitlist**, not to open sale | `19.02.04 D-01` (queue randomisation removes the bot's edge structurally); `19.02.05` waitlist; 19.09 anti-scalping posture | ✅ CONFIRMED — a hold releasing 20 tickets into a sold-out storefront with no announcement is a stealth micro-on-sale. The waitlist already exists for precisely this moment and already has ordered, identified demand. Routing releases through it is free fairness and turns the domain's worst-timed event into its highest-intent one. Same rule for un-killed inventory (D-08) and for post-on-sale capacity increases. |
| DT-08 | A hold deadline inside **48h of doors** is functionally a kill | Reasoning about the marketing window required to convert released inventory | ✅ CONFIRMED — but as a warning, not a block (DT-09's logic). Twenty tickets released 36 hours out, with no announce, no email and no promo cycle, will not sell; the release is ceremony. Naming the loss at the point of the decision is the whole product. |
| DT-09 | The system should **block** holds above some percentage of capacity | Instinct — a 60% venue hold looks like abuse | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection is load-bearing. The domain's second characteristic mechanism is explicit that *"a system that blocks gets switched off by 8pm on night one, because the answer genuinely is sometimes yes."* The correct controls here are structural, not detective: the deadline is mandatory (DT-01) so the hold cannot be permanent, and the class-level aggregate is visible to the artist (DT-04) so the hold cannot be secret. The camera platform gets cancelled, the desk moves, the barricade line changes at load-in — production reality moves after the manifest is built, and 12 dead seats on a sold-out show is the same evaporated money DT-01 exists to prevent, arriving by a different door. The distinction that matters is **sweep versus act**: auto-release is a sweep and must never touch kills; a manual un-kill before doors, with a stated reason and a name on it, is an act and is correct. D-08 records the refinement; un-killed inventory routes to the waitlist per DT-07. |
| DT-11 | **Production kills arrive late and land on sold seats** | Music-industry domain knowledge: FOH decides the desk position at load-in, and touring productions grow between announce and doors. `19.01.04` mentions production kills only in a *pre-on-sale* happy path; 18 owns the technical advance | ✅ CONFIRMED — **this is the thing a domain expert expects that nobody in this sub-domain has said.** Every model here assumes kills are configured before on-sale. They are not: the desk position is a decision made by a human standing in the room on the day, and a touring production's footprint routinely grows after the tickets are gone. So the manifest must accept a kill that collides with sold inventory, and "you cannot kill sold inventory" is not an answer — the desk is going there regardless of what the ledger says. | DT-12 | A hold names exactly **one price level**; a multi-level allocation is N holds | `#CX-01` — *"a hold is a hold of specific priced inventory, not of an abstract count"* | ✅ CONFIRMED — and the reason is sharper than modelling tidiness. | DT-13 | The auto-release should ask the hold owner to **confirm** before firing | Politeness instinct — releasing someone's inventory without asking feels hostile | ❌ REJECTED — consent-gating the release reintroduces the exact failure it exists to remove. ## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([path](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) — *shared entity + upstream dependency*. Licensed capacity, standing kills, the accessible block and seat maps originate there and are authoritative here; this domain cannot raise capacity (D-03). Two requirements discovered here land on 16: capacity must be **per room configuration** (already raised by `19.01.04 DT-02`), and the capacity figure must carry a **licence reference and validity window** — a lapsed or varied licence is the most common real cause of the D-12 capacity-reduction incident, and a bare integer cannot express it (Q-05).
- Touches **17 Live Booking & Settlement** ([path](../../17-live-booking-settlement/)) — *trigger dependency + shared entity*. Hold classes are frequently **deal terms** ("artist gets 20 comps"), so they flow from the executed contract (17.04) as structured data rather than being re-keyed (D-04). `17.04`'s own principle is decisive here: *"a contract retyped from an offer is a second source of truth that will disagree with the first."* Two consequences run back: an Operator edit to a contract-derived hold is an **amendment**, routed to 17.04's amendment flow; and a capacity reduction (D-12) changes gross potential, which may make a negotiated guarantee unviable — 17 must be notified, not merely reconciled at settlement. This is the parent index's **Q-11**, answered for this feature.
- Touches **18 Show Production & Touring** ([path](../../18-show-production-touring/)) — *trigger dependency*. Production kills (desk, camera platform, barricade, delay towers) originate from the technical advance and, per DT-11, **arrive after on-sale**. 18 must be able to push a late kill into a live manifest and receive back the collision set — which sold seats it lands on — because the advance is where that answer is needed. A one-way "kills are configured up front" contract between 18 and 19 is the shape that makes DT-11 unrepresentable.
- Touches **19.01.01 Ticket Types & Price Scaling** ([path](./19.01.01-ticket-types-price-scaling.md)) — *shared entity + state race*. A hold names exactly one price level (DT-12); release must resolve price against the tier state at the instant of release, and must not complete at an undefined price (`#CX-01`). Deleting a price level with holds bound to it is blocked pending a re-home. This feature's answer to `#CX-01`'s open synthesis question 1 (concurrent Operator edits during a live on-sale): release is idempotent against observed state and fails closed on conflict.
- Touches **19.01.04 Seating Model** ([path](./19.01.04-seating-model-ga-reserved-seat-maps.md)) — *shared entity*. Hold semantics differ entirely between GA (fungible count) and reserved (named seats); mixed carries both per section, never a blended figure. Partial release of a contiguous reserved block releases edges-inward (D-13); adjacency is protected at *purchase* selection, not at release. See `#CX-03` — this feature supplies the answer that CX-03's synthesis section is missing.
- Touches **19.01.05 Accessible Seating & Companion Tickets** ([path](./19.01.05-accessible-seating-companion-tickets.md)) — *permission / structural exclusion*. Accessible inventory sits inside the manifest and counts against capacity but is **exempt by construction** from the hold lifecycle — auto-release must be structurally unable to reach it, not merely flagged off (`#CX-04`, `19.01.05 DT-01`). A production kill on the accessible block or its circulation is blocked without an equivalent replacement.
- Touches **19.02.04 Queue, Waiting Room & Cart Hold** ([path](../19.02-on-sale-announce-presale/19.02.04-queue-waiting-room-cart-hold.md)) — *shared entity + state race*. `carted` is a distinct bucket in the invariant: a hold created during a live on-sale draws only from unreserved `remaining`, never from inventory a buyer holds mid-checkout. Symmetrically, `19.02.04 D-04` (cart holds return to their **source pool**) means a cart taken from released inventory returns to public inventory, not to the hold it descends from.
- Touches **19.02.05 Sold-Out Waitlist & Demand Capture** ([path](../19.02-on-sale-announce-presale/19.02.05-sold-out-waitlist-demand-capture.md)) — *notification fan-out*. The consumer of DT-07: every route by which inventory becomes publicly available at or after sell-out — hold release, kill reversal, capacity increase — is offered through the waitlist in waitlist order, never posted silently to the storefront.
- Touches **19.03 Guest List & Comps** ([path](../19.03-guest-list-comps/)) — *shared entity + trigger dependency*. The artist hold **is** the inventory the guest list draws from, and an artist hold bound to an allocation takes its deadline from the guest-list close (`19.03.01`) rather than the generic doors − 14d default. Comps deplete the manifest without depleting `paid` (`19.03.02 D-02`). An over-claim beyond the hold draws from public inventory as an attributed Operator decision, not an automatic top-up — door-time over-allocation is `19.03.03`'s flow, not this one's.
- Touches **19.05.01 Live Count & Manifest State** ([path](../19.05-box-office-counts-drops/19.05.01-live-count-manifest-state.md)) — *shared entity + notification*. The manifest is the state; the live count is its reporting face. Two things specified here surface there: the held-inventory alert with its `max(10%, 20)` threshold (`ticketing-box-office-cx.md#CX-03`), and DT-04's class-level held breakdown for the Musician — which is a **change** to `19.05.01`'s current Musician lens (*"scoped to what their deal grants"*), not merely a read of it. Flagged for Step 6 synthesis.
- Touches **19.05.02 Drop Reports** ([path](../19.05-box-office-counts-drops/19.05.02-drop-reports-counterparty-distribution.md)) — *notification fan-out*. The drop is how the Musician actually receives counts (they are not watching a dashboard). If DT-04 holds, the drop is where the class-level held figure must appear — a drop showing "260 sold of 300" while 40 sit held is the asymmetry surviving the fix.
- Touches **19.05.05 Box Office Close & Certified Statement** ([path](../19.05-box-office-counts-drops/19.05.05-box-office-close-certified-statement.md)) — *trigger dependency*. Close freezes the manifest. Any hold unresolved at doors appears as its **own line** on the certified statement — the money nobody decided to lose, recorded rather than hidden. Killed inventory appears as a distinct line from held, for the same reason DT-03 keeps them apart.
- Touches **19.06.01 Individual Refunds & Exchanges** ([path](../19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling/19.06.01-individual-refunds-exchanges.md)) — *trigger dependency*. Refunded inventory returns to public inventory, never to its originating hold (D-15). Rungs 3 and 4 of the D-12 ladder route their money through this feature.
- Touches **19.06.02 Event Cancellation & Mass Refund** ([path](../19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling/19.06.02-event-cancellation-mass-refund.md)) — *state*. Cancellation freezes the manifest rather than releasing holds; the frozen breakdown is evidence for the mass refund.
- Touches **19.06.03 Reschedule & Postponement** ([path](../19.06-refunds-cancellations-rescheduling/19.06.03-reschedule-postponement-handling.md)) — *trigger dependency + state race*, and the most dangerous edge in this file. A reschedule must **re-derive** every hold deadline against the new doors and fire none of them (D-05, DT-05). With absolute deadlines, rescheduling a show would auto-release the entire manifest at the instant of the reschedule. `19.01.04 D-03` separately makes post-on-sale reconfiguration a reschedule-class event, which re-instantiates the manifest at a new granularity — holds must survive that transition or be explicitly re-created, never silently dropped.
- Touches **19.07 External Ticketing Integration** ([path](../19.07-external-ticketing-integration/)) — *permission / control boundary*. On externally-ticketed shows the manifest is a shadow and auto-release is structurally disabled (D-10, DT-06). Deadline prompts become instructions to act in the incumbent, confirmed attributably (`19.07.04 D-01`). Whether any incumbent exposes hold write-back at all decides whether the shadow is transitional or permanent (Q-06).
- Touches **19.09 Ticketing Fraud, Bot & Resale Controls** ([path](../19.09-ticketing-fraud-bot-resale-controls/)) — *structural control*. DT-07's waitlist routing is an anti-bot control wearing an inventory costume: an unannounced release into a sold-out storefront is the single most bot-favourable event a box office can produce, and routing it through ordered, identified waitlist demand removes the advantage structurally rather than detecting it — the domain's third characteristic mechanism.
- **Domain mechanism instances** (`ticketing-box-office-cx.md`): this feature carries all three. *Deadline-with-automatic-resolution* — D-01, already registered. *Attribution, not prevention* — D-14 (oversized holds), D-08 (kill reversal), the over-claim draw, and the shadow-manifest confirmation. *Structural over detective* — D-09 (waitlist routing) and the accessible block's exemption-by-construction. Each needs one shared implementation or it will drift.
