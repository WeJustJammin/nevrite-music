# Feature: Fee Structure & All-In Price Display

> **Number**: 19.01.03
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Defines every charge added to a ticket's face value — booking fee, service charge, facility fee, order-level handling, delivery — who each one splits to, and how the total is displayed. Ticket fees are now a regulated display surface: US FTC junk-fee rules for live-event ticketing and comparable UK/EU duties require the **total price to be the first price shown**, not a checkout-time reveal.

This feature exists here rather than in the payments cross-cut because the money *rail* is shared infrastructure, but *what the fee is, who it belongs to, and what the buyer is legally shown* is a ticketing product decision with a settlement consequence.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees the full fee structure on their own show, because fees determine the difference between the face value a fan pays and the money that reaches settlement. Cannot change it — the fee split is a term of the booking deal (domain 17). Sees the fee breakdown in their drop report (19.05.02). |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Configures fee types, amounts (fixed or percentage), the level they apply at (per-ticket vs per-order), and the split recipients. Sees the resulting all-in price for every tier before on-sale. |
| Fan | Read-only | Sees the all-in total as the headline price, with an itemised breakdown available on demand. Never sees the split — who receives the booking fee is not their business, though the total is legally required to be. |

## Behavior

Each fee has a type, a basis (fixed amount or percentage of face value), a scope (per-ticket or per-order), and one or more split recipients (venue, platform, promoter, act). The displayed price for any tier is `face value + Σ per-ticket fees + tax`, with per-order fees disclosed before the buyer commits.

The all-in figure is computed once and is the same value shown at browse, at cart, and at charge. A discrepancy between the displayed and charged total is a compliance incident, not a rounding bug.

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens fee configuration for the show and sees the venue's saved default fee structure.
2. Operator adjusts fee types and amounts as needed, and sets the split recipients for each.
3. System shows a live preview: for each price level, face value → all-in price, with the itemised breakdown as the Fan will see it.
4. System shows the settlement preview: for a hypothetical sold-out show, what each split recipient receives.
5. Operator saves. System validates that splits sum to 100% of each fee and that no fee lacks a recipient.
6. On-sale displays all-in prices from the first impression.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fee splits do not sum to 100% | Save rejected — an unallocated fee remainder is money with no owner, which surfaces at settlement as a dispute | Blocking error naming the fee and the shortfall/overage |
| Fee changed after on-sale | Two buyer cohorts at different totals for identical inventory; an increase is likely unlawful mid-sale | Warning naming affected buyer count; increases blocked pending `[PENDING — legal review]` |
| Displayed total ≠ charged total | Compliance incident | Must be structurally impossible — one computation, not two. Flagged to `/create-prd` as an invariant, not a test case |
| Percentage fee on a £0 ticket | Yields £0; free events route via 19.11 anyway | N/A |
| Tax rate differs by venue jurisdiction (amusement tax, VAT) | Tax is a cross-cut mechanism; this feature consumes the computed rate rather than owning it | Breakdown shows tax as a distinct line |
| Companion/accessible ticket carries a duplicate order fee | Creates a de-facto accessibility surcharge — a parity breach | See `#CX-05`; `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Fan disputes the fee at the door on a walk-up sale | Walk-up fee structure may differ from online; must be disclosed at the window | `[PENDING]` |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | New show | Venue default fee structure offered as a starting point |
| Draft | Fees configured, not on sale | Live all-in preview + settlement preview |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | All-in display must be the **default and only** display mode, with no "show face value first" configuration option | FTC junk-fee rule for live-event ticketing; UK/EU equivalents; the domain index names *"fee-disclosure and junk-fee rules"* as part of this domain's own regulatory regime | ✅ CONFIRMED — making it configurable creates a setting whose wrong value is unlawful. A domain expert would expect the toggle (every incumbent had one) and would be wrong: the regulation removed the choice. Not offering the toggle is the correct design. |
| DT-02 | Fee configuration is a box-office permission | Sweep framing — fees look like a pricing setting | ❌ REJECTED — the fee *split* determines who gets paid, so configuring fees is effectively editing a settlement term. It requires settlement-side rights (domain 17), not box-office rights. Treating it as a box-office setting would let door staff silently re-cut the deal. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | All-in pricing is unconditional — no face-value-first display mode exists | A configurable option whose wrong setting is unlawful should not exist (DT-01) |
| D-02 | The all-in total is computed once and reused at browse/cart/charge | Two computations is two chances to diverge, and divergence is a compliance incident |
| D-03 | Fee configuration requires settlement-side rights, not box-office rights | Editing a fee split is editing who gets paid (DT-02) |
