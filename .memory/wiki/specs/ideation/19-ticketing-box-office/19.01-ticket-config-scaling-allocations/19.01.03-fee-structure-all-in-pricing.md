# Feature: Fee Structure & All-In Price Display

> **Number**: 19.01.03
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Defines every charge added to a ticket's face value — booking fee, service charge, facility fee, order-level handling, delivery — who each one splits to, and how the total is displayed. Ticket fees are a regulated display surface: US FTC junk-fee rules for live-event ticketing and comparable UK/EU duties require the **total price to be the first price shown**, not a checkout-time reveal.

This feature exists here rather than in the payments cross-cut because the money *rail* is shared infrastructure, but *what the fee is, who it belongs to, and what the buyer is legally shown* is a ticketing product decision with a settlement consequence (D-03, parent index). It owns the fee model and the display invariant; it consumes the tax rate and the money rail rather than owning either.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Musician** | Read-only | Sees the full fee structure on their own show — every fee type, its basis, its scope, and the split recipients — because fees determine the gap between the face value a fan pays and the money that reaches settlement. Sees the settlement preview (per-recipient payout on a sold-out show) and the itemised breakdown in their drop report (19.05.02). Cannot change any of it — the fee split is a term of the booking deal (domain 17); a Musician editing it would be silently re-cutting the settlement. Where the *act* is itself a split recipient (a booking-fee rebate to the artist), the Musician sees their own recipient line but still cannot alter the percentages. |
| **Producer** | None | Not visible. No box-office surface — the Producer's provenance/session role never touches ticket fee configuration. Listed explicitly so downstream spec writers do not accidentally expose a fee panel to this persona. |
| **Operator** | Full | Configures fee types, basis (fixed minor-unit amount or percentage of face value), scope (per-ticket vs per-order), rounding, and one or more split recipients per fee. Sees the live all-in preview per price level and the settlement preview per recipient before on-sale. After on-sale, edits are constrained (decreases allowed and audited; increases blocked — see Edge Cases). |
| **Fan** | Read-only | Sees the all-in total as the headline price from the first impression, with an itemised breakdown available on demand (face value + each named fee + tax). Never sees the split recipients — who receives the booking fee is not the buyer's concern; the *total* is legally required to be. Sees an unchanged number from browse → cart → charge; any divergence is a compliance incident, not a UI variance the Fan should ever experience. |

> **Multi-hyphenate note (Operator ∩ Musician)**: a musician who owns the venue and also plays the show holds both lenses. Access resolves to the **union** (Full, via Operator), but the settlement preview must render their two stakes as distinct lines — venue-side fee recipient *and* act-side booking settlement — never merged into one figure, so the certified statement (19.05.05) reconciles cleanly. See Edge Cases.

## Behavior

Each fee has: a **type** (booking / service / facility / handling / delivery / custom-labelled), a **basis** (fixed amount in minor units, or a percentage of face value), a **scope** (per-ticket or per-order), a **rounding rule** (default: round half-up to the nearest minor unit at the individual fee line), and **one or more split recipients** (venue, platform, promoter, act), each holding an integer basis-point share summing to exactly 10 000 bp (100.00%) of that fee.

All money is computed and stored in **integer minor units** (pence / cents) — never floating point — to make the display/charge invariant exact rather than approximately equal.

The **all-in headline price** for a price level is:

```
all_in(tier) = face_value
             + Σ(per-ticket mandatory fees, each rounded at its own line)
             + amortised per-order mandatory fees        [see per-order handling below]
             + tax(on the taxable base for the jurisdiction)   [rate consumed, not owned]
```

**Per-order fee handling.** Per-order fees (a single handling charge, delivery) apply once per order, not per ticket. Mandatory per-order fees are disclosed in the headline where a jurisdiction requires all mandatory charges to be shown upfront; the buyer sees the per-order component itemised at cart before commit. Genuinely optional ancillary charges (e.g. a premium physical-delivery upgrade the buyer may decline) are excluded from the headline but disclosed and confirmed before charge. Which mandatory-fee and tax components must appear *in* the headline versus *disclosed alongside* it is jurisdiction-specific (Q-01); the invariant below holds regardless of that choice.

**The display/charge invariant.** The all-in total shown to a Fan at first impression is the **exact total charged** — the same number at browse, cart and charge, never re-derived downstream at the payment step. A discrepancy between displayed and charged total is therefore not a rounding bug to be tested for — it is a guarantee the design must make structurally unbreakable, and any observed divergence is a P1 compliance incident routed to 24 (Trust & Safety), not a support ticket. (*How* the total is captured once and carried unchanged is a computation-model decision for the architecture/BE layer; the product requirement is simply that the first price shown is the price paid.)

### Happy Path

1. Operator opens fee configuration for the show; the system pre-loads the venue's saved default fee structure (from the room/venue record, domain 16) as the starting point.
2. Operator adjusts fee types, basis, scope and rounding as needed, and sets the integer basis-point split recipients for each fee.
3. System renders a **live preview**: for each price level, `face value → all-in price`, with the itemised breakdown exactly as the Fan will see it (each fee named, tax as a distinct line).
4. System renders a **settlement preview**: for a hypothetical sold-out show, the gross, the net-of-fees, and the exact minor-unit payout to each split recipient — reconciling to the manifest capacity (19.01.02).
5. Operator saves. System validates, in order: (a) every fee's splits sum to exactly 10 000 bp; (b) no fee lacks at least one recipient; (c) no per-ticket percentage fee exceeds any jurisdiction fee cap on record; (d) the save does not silently overwrite a change another operator made since this edit began. On success it writes the fee structure.
6. On-sale draws from the saved structure and displays all-in prices from the first impression — no configuration exists that could show face value first (D-01).

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Fee splits do not sum to 100% (≠ 10 000 bp) | Save rejected with `FEE_SPLIT_UNBALANCED`. An unallocated remainder is money with no owner and surfaces at settlement as a dispute. | Blocking inline error naming the fee and the exact bp shortfall/overage (e.g. "Booking fee splits total 9 800 bp — 200 bp unallocated"). Save button disabled until resolved. |
| A fee has zero split recipients | Save rejected with `FEE_RECIPIENT_MISSING`. | Inline error naming the recipient-less fee; save blocked. |
| Two Operator staff edit the fee structure concurrently during on-sale prep | Last writer's save is rejected with `FEE_EDIT_CONFLICT` because it would silently overwrite the change the first saver made since this edit began — a concurrent edit must not clobber another operator's work. The *detection mechanism* is an architecture decision; the product requirement is no silent overwrite. | Second saver sees "This fee structure was changed by someone else — review the current version before saving"; their diff is preserved for re-application. |
| Operator attempts a fee **increase** after on-sale | Rejected with `FEE_INCREASE_AFTER_ONSALE`. An increase creates two buyer cohorts at different totals for identical inventory and is presumptively unlawful mid-sale. The *whether-ever-lawful* question is Q-03 (legal review); the *default behaviour* is a hard block, so the unlawful path is absent by default rather than merely warned. | "Fee increases are not permitted while tickets are on sale." No override in the base flow. |
| Operator applies a fee **decrease** after on-sale | Allowed and audited. Already-sold orders are not retro-credited unless a price-guarantee policy exists (that policy is a product decision — CX-02 SQ4, deferred). New buyers pay the lower total. | Confirmation dialog stating the change is logged and applies to future sales only; entry written to the fee audit trail. |
| Displayed total ≠ charged total | Must not occur — the total shown is the total charged, carried unchanged rather than independently re-derived downstream (D-02). If ever observed (e.g. a rail re-derives the number), it is raised as a P1 compliance incident to 24, the charge is voided, and the order is failed rather than settled. | Fan is never charged a figure they did not see; a failed order shows a retryable error, never a silently different amount. |
| Percentage fee applied to a £0 / free ticket | Yields £0; free events route through 19.11 (free/RSVP) anyway, so the percentage basis is a no-op here rather than an error. | Breakdown shows the fee line at £0.00. |
| Rounding of a percentage fee lands on a half-minor-unit (e.g. 12.5% of £7.00 = 87.5p) | Deterministic **round half-up** at the individual fee line before summation, so the total is reproducible and the same on every surface. Rounding is never applied to the grand total after the fact. | Breakdown shows the rounded fee (88p); the sum of shown lines equals the shown total exactly. |
| Tax rate differs by venue jurisdiction (amusement tax, VAT) | Tax is a cross-cut mechanism; this feature consumes the computed rate for the venue's jurisdiction and renders it, but does not own the rate table. If the rate service is unavailable at configuration time, preview shows tax as `unavailable` and save of a taxable show is blocked rather than guessing. | Breakdown shows tax as a distinct labelled line; on rate-service failure, a non-dismissable notice that pricing cannot be finalised until tax is resolved. |
| **Companion / accessible ticket carries a duplicate per-order fee** | Resolved (previously an open CX-05 gap), authored at `19.01.05` **D-05**: **per-order fees are charged once per order, not once per ticket** — an accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are **one order carrying exactly one** per-order handling/delivery fee. The companion's **per-ticket fees equal those on a comparable general ticket at the same face tier** — never a premium, and never zero-rated either, because a companion line carrying no booking fee breaks the same parity test from the cheap side. The pair therefore has identical total structure to a comparable two-ticket general order at the same tier. Any configuration that would compute a second per-order fee for the companion line, or a per-ticket fee above the comparable general tier, is rejected at save as a parity breach (`FEE_PARITY_BREACH`, surfaced to the Operator as `422 ACCESSIBLE_FEE_PARITY_BREACH`). | Operator sees a parity check in the preview: accessible+companion all-in equals comparable general all-in, or save is blocked naming the offending fee. Fan sees no surcharge. See `#CX-05`. |
| **Walk-up / box-office (door) sale has a different fee structure than online** | Resolved (previously an open gap): a show may carry a distinct **walk-up fee profile** (commonly lower or zero online-service fees, sometimes a cash-handling fee). If defined, it must be disclosed at the window before payment; if undefined, the walk-up sale falls back to the online all-in structure so the buyer is never charged an undisclosed door premium. Walk-up all-in is computed by the same one-computation invariant and printed on the receipt. The door-sale transaction surface itself lives in 19.03/19.05; this feature owns only the *fee profile* it consumes. | Door staff see the applicable walk-up all-in; the buyer sees an itemised receipt with the same breakdown shape as online. |
| Malicious input: negative fee, >100% percentage, or a split recipient not party to the deal | Rejected at the schema boundary before any preview computation — negative amounts and percentages outside `[0, 100]` fail validation (`FEE_INVALID_BASIS`); a recipient not present in the booking agreement (domain 17) fails referential validation (`FEE_RECIPIENT_UNKNOWN`). No fee configuration reaches settlement without every recipient resolving to a real settlement party. | Inline field-level error identifying the offending value/recipient; save blocked. |
| Partial data: booking deal (domain 17) not yet finalised, so split recipients are unknown | Fee *amounts* can be drafted, but splits cannot be validated against parties that don't exist yet. Save of a splittable fee is blocked with a boundary state pointing at the missing deal term rather than allowing an unallocatable fee. | "Split recipients depend on the booking agreement, which isn't finalised — complete the deal terms in [17] before locking fees." |
| Race: a fee decrease and a timed price-tier advance (19.01.01) commit in the same instant | Deterministic ordering — the tier's face value resolves first, then fees apply to the resolved face value, so the all-in is single-valued regardless of arrival order. Never a partially-applied blend. | The tier change and the fee change are both reflected atomically in the next computed all-in; no intermediate mixed price is ever displayed or charged. |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Loading** | Fee config panel opening; preview recomputing after an edit; tax rate being fetched | Skeleton on the preview/settlement panels; edit controls disabled until the current structure and tax rate have loaded. |
| **Empty** | New show, no fee structure yet | Venue default fee structure offered as a pre-filled starting point; if the venue has no default either, an explicit "no fees configured" prompt (a show can legitimately have zero added fees). |
| **Populated (Draft)** | Fees configured, show not yet on sale | Live all-in preview per tier + settlement preview per recipient; all edits permitted. |
| **Populated (Locked)** | Show on sale | Read-mostly; decreases permitted-and-audited, increases blocked; every edit warns and writes an audit entry. |
| **Error** | Splits invalid, recipient missing/unknown, invalid basis, parity breach, or a concurrent-edit conflict | Inline field-level error(s) naming the specific failure (`FEE_SPLIT_UNBALANCED`, `FEE_RECIPIENT_MISSING`, etc.) and the offending value; save blocked until cleared. |
| **Partial** | Tax rate unavailable, or booking-deal split recipients not yet finalised | All-in shown with the missing component flagged (`tax: unavailable` / `splits: pending deal`); finalising/locking blocked while a required component is missing — never a silently-guessed number. |
| **Displayed (Fan-facing)** | On-sale, buyer browsing | All-in headline price with an expandable itemised breakdown (face + each named fee + tax); the same total at browse, cart and charge. |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | All-in display must be the **default and only** display mode, with no "show face value first" configuration option | FTC junk-fee rule for live-event ticketing; UK/EU equivalents; the domain index names *"fee-disclosure and junk-fee rules"* as this domain's own regulatory regime | ✅ CONFIRMED — a configurable toggle whose wrong value is unlawful should not exist. A domain expert expects the toggle (every incumbent had one) and is wrong: the regulation removed the choice. Not offering it is the correct design (D-01). |
| DT-02 | Fee configuration is a box-office permission | Sweep framing — fees look like a pricing setting | ❌ REJECTED — the fee *split* determines who gets paid, so configuring fees is editing a settlement term. It requires settlement-side rights (domain 17), not box-office rights (D-03). Treating it as a box-office setting would let door staff silently re-cut the deal. |
| DT-03 | Money can be computed in decimal currency (floating point) and rounded for display | Naive implementation instinct | ❌ REJECTED — floating-point currency makes the display/charge invariant *approximately* true, and "approximately equal totals" is precisely the compliance failure the regulation targets. Storing and computing in **integer minor units** with a fixed round-half-up rule at each fee line makes the invariant exact and reproducible across surfaces. This is why rounding is specified at the line, never on the grand total. |
| DT-04 | The all-in should be recomputed at each surface (browse, cart, charge) from the same formula, which is safe because the formula is identical | Plausible "stateless is simpler" argument | ❌ REJECTED — identical formulas can still diverge if any input drifts between calls (a tax rate refresh, a tier advance, a rounding library version). The product requirement is that the fan is charged exactly the first price shown, so the total must be **captured once and carried unchanged** rather than independently re-derived at each surface (D-02). Same formula ≠ same result; recomputing independently at three surfaces is not a safe way to meet the requirement. (The specific compute-once mechanism is an architecture decision, not settled here.) |
| DT-05 | Per-order fees should be spread evenly across tickets so every line shows one blended per-ticket price | Display-simplicity argument — one number per ticket reads cleanly | ❌ REJECTED — amortising a per-order fee into per-ticket lines corrupts settlement (the fee belongs to the *order*, and its split recipient is owed once) and breaks refunds (refunding one ticket of three must not refund a third of the order fee unless policy says so). Per-order fees are disclosed at order level in the headline math but remain a distinct order-scoped line for settlement and refund correctness. |
| DT-06 | A companion ticket is "another ticket" in the order, so every fee an ordinary ticket carries — per-ticket **and** per-order — applies again on its line | Symmetry instinct | ❌ REJECTED **in its per-order half**, which is exactly where the surcharge hides: charging the order's handling/delivery fee a second time because a companion is present manufactures a de-facto accessibility surcharge and breaches statutory price parity (CX-05). The resolution is authored at `19.01.05` **D-05** — the accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are **one order carrying exactly one per-order fee**, while the companion's **per-ticket fees equal those on a comparable general ticket at the same face tier**. The per-ticket half of the hypothesis therefore survives: zero-rating the companion's booking fee is the mirror error, breaking the same accessible-equals-general test from the cheap side and leaving the two orders differently shaped, which is not what parity asks for. The check is enforced at save, not left to operator diligence. |
| DT-07 | Whether a mid-sale fee **increase** can ever be lawful is a behaviour this feature should decide | Completeness pressure — resolve every branch | ⏳ DEFERRED to Q-03 — this is a legal/product determination (does any jurisdiction permit a re-consented increase?), not an agent call. The *default behaviour is fully specified*: increases are hard-blocked (`FEE_INCREASE_AFTER_ONSALE`), so the safe path exists today; only the possibility of a future opt-in exception remains open, and it lives in Open Questions, not as a gap in the flow. |

> Log every Deep Think hypothesis — confirmed and rejected — as the audit trail that the agent explored beyond the obvious.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | All-in pricing is unconditional — no face-value-first display mode exists | A configurable option whose wrong setting is unlawful should not exist (DT-01) |
| D-02 | The all-in total shown at first impression is the exact total charged — captured once and carried unchanged through browse/cart/charge, never independently re-derived downstream (the compute-once mechanism is an architecture/BE-layer decision) | Two independent computations are two chances to diverge, and divergence is a compliance incident (DT-04) |
| D-03 | Fee configuration requires settlement-side rights, not box-office rights | Editing a fee split is editing who gets paid (DT-02) |
| D-04 | All money computed and stored in integer minor units; percentage fees rounded half-up at the individual fee line, never on the grand total | Makes the display/charge invariant exact and reproducible across surfaces (DT-03) |
| D-05 | Per-order fees remain order-scoped for settlement and refunds, never amortised into per-ticket lines | Amortising corrupts settlement attribution and refund math (DT-05) |
| D-06 | An accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are one order carrying exactly one per-order fee; the companion's per-ticket fees equal the comparable general tier; parity enforced at save — restating `19.01.05` D-05, which authors it | Prevents a de-facto accessibility surcharge without swinging into a mirror-image discount that breaks the same parity test from the cheap side (DT-06, CX-05) |
| D-07 | Mid-sale fee increases hard-blocked by default; decreases allowed and audited | Two cohorts at different totals for identical inventory is presumptively unlawful (Q-03 holds the may-it-ever-be-lawful question) |
| D-08 | Walk-up fee profile is optional; if absent, walk-up falls back to the online all-in structure | The buyer must never face an undisclosed door premium |
