# Feature: Accessible Seating & Companion Tickets

> **Number**: 19.01.05
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [SURFACE]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Overview

Wheelchair positions, viewing platforms, accessible seats and their companion tickets — inventory that is part of capacity but governed by statute rather than commercial preference. Accessible inventory must be held back, must be sold at a price equivalent to comparable general inventory, must be purchasable through the same channel as everything else, and may only be released to general sale under specific conditions.

This is a **Deep Think addition** — the 14-lens sweep did not surface it, which is itself notable: accessibility inventory is invisible to product thinking right up until it generates a complaint, a refund, or a regulator's letter.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees that accessible provision exists for their show and its status. Cannot configure it. Included as read-only deliberately: the reputational damage of an inaccessible show attaches to the artist's name in the fan's memory, not the venue's, so visibility is warranted even though authority is not. |
| Producer | None | Not visible. No box-office surface. |
| Operator | Full | Defines the accessible block per room configuration (positions, companion entitlement, access route), maintains its statutory release schedule, and evidences its handling. |
| Fan | Full | The only persona that transacts on it. Buys an accessible position and its companion ticket through the ordinary flow at the ordinary price, states access requirements, and receives the room's access information (step-free route, platform, facilities) without a phone call. |

## Behavior

The accessible block is instantiated from the room configuration (domain 16) — a set of positions with a type (wheelchair space, easy-access seat, viewing platform), a companion entitlement per position, and access-route metadata. It sits inside the manifest and counts against capacity, but is exempt from the ordinary hold lifecycle in 19.01.02: it is not released by deadline, and it is not killed for production without an equivalent replacement.

Companion tickets are entitlements attached to an accessible position, priced at the comparable general tier.

### Happy Path

1. Room configuration (domain 16) supplies the accessible block for the chosen configuration.
2. Operator confirms positions, companion entitlement per position, and the access information for this show (route, entry time, platform location).
3. Block is instantiated into the manifest at a price level equivalent to comparable general inventory.
4. On-sale opens; accessible positions are purchasable in the same flow, at the same time, at the same all-in price as everything else — not via a phone number or an enquiry form.
5. Fan selects an accessible position, declares whether a companion is needed, and states access requirements.
6. System issues the accessible ticket and its companion ticket, and sends the show's access information with them.
7. At the door (19.04), the accessible ticket routes to the accessible entrance and the companion scans alongside it.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Show sells out with accessible positions unsold | These may be releasable to general sale, but only under statutory conditions — the ordinary auto-release of 19.01.02 must NOT apply | Operator sees a distinct release flow with conditions stated; the generic hold auto-release is structurally excluded from this block |
| Accessible positions released early to general sale | Likely unlawful; a disabled fan then cannot buy at all | Blocked; release requires the conditional flow |
| Companion ticket carries a duplicate order fee | De-facto accessibility surcharge — parity breach | See `19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-cx.md#CX-05`; `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` |
| Accessible provision only reachable by phone/email enquiry | The status-quo failure this feature exists to remove; equal access to the *purchase channel* is part of the duty | Not offered — the enquiry-only path is deliberately absent |
| Production kills the viewing platform for a camera position | Capacity reduction that lands entirely on disabled fans | Blocked without an equivalent replacement position; escalates to the Operator |
| Fan's access requirement cannot be met by the room (no step-free route to the bar) | Must be disclosed before purchase, not discovered on the night | Access information shown at selection with known limitations stated plainly |
| GA standing room with no seat map | Accessible provision still required — viewing platform, wheelchair positions | Block exists independently of the seating model (`#R-03`) |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Empty | Room configuration has no accessible block defined | Operator sees a blocking prompt — a configuration with no accessible provision should not reach on-sale without an explicit, evidenced reason |
| Draft | Block instantiated, access info incomplete | Operator sees required-field prompts for route and facilities |
| Live | On sale | Fan sees accessible positions in the standard flow with access info |
| Sold out (accessible) | Accessible block exhausted while general inventory remains | Fan sees a plain statement and a route to the venue — not a dead end |
| Conditional release | Show near sell-out, accessible positions unsold | Operator sees the conditional release flow with the statutory test stated |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Accessible inventory can reuse the hold mechanism from 19.01.02 with a flag | Modelling economy — it looks like a held block | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection is the reason this feature exists separately. 19.01.02's flagship design call is *deadline-by-default with automatic release* (its DT-01), which is exactly the behaviour that must never apply here: auto-releasing accessible positions to general sale on a date is the precise unlawful act. Reusing the mechanism means the safety feature of one block is the compliance breach of another. |
| DT-02 | The duty is about *seats*, so GA rooms are out of scope | Naive reading of "accessible seating" | ❌ REJECTED — accessible provision in standing rooms (viewing platforms, wheelchair positions, companion access) is required and is the *harder* case, because there is no seat map to hang the inventory on. This is why accessible provision is modelled independently of the seating model (19.01.04) rather than as a subtype of reserved seating. |

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Accessible inventory is a distinct block, not a flagged hold | The hold mechanism's auto-release is the exact unlawful behaviour here (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Accessible provision is independent of the seating model | Required in GA standing rooms where no seat map exists (DT-02) |
| D-03 | Purchase happens through the ordinary channel at the ordinary time | Enquiry-only accessible booking is the status-quo failure being removed; the flow is deliberately absent |
