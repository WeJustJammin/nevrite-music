# Feature: Accessible Seating & Companion Tickets

> **Number**: 19.01.05
> **Parent**: [Ticket Configuration, Scaling & Allocations](./19.01-ticket-config-scaling-allocations-index.md)
> **Status**: [DEEP]
> **Last updated**: 2026-07-17

## Overview

Wheelchair positions, viewing platforms, easy-access seats and their companion tickets — inventory that is part of capacity but governed by statute rather than commercial preference. Accessible inventory must be held back, must be sold at a price equivalent to comparable general inventory, must be purchasable through the same channel, at the same time, with the same process friction as everything else, and may only be released to general sale under specific conditions.

This is a **Deep Think addition** — the 14-lens sweep did not surface it, which is itself notable: accessibility inventory is invisible to product thinking right up until it generates a complaint, a refund, or a regulator's letter. It is separated from the ordinary allocation feature (19.01.02) precisely because the safety behaviour of a general hold — deadline-by-default automatic release — is the exact unlawful act when applied to an accessible position.

## Role Lens

| Role | Access | What They See/Do |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Musician | Read-only | Sees that accessible provision exists for their show, its position count, and its status (`Live` / `Sold out (accessible)` / `Draft`). Cannot configure it, cannot price it, cannot release it. A config-write attempt returns `403 FORBIDDEN` with `code: ROLE_READ_ONLY`. Included as read-only deliberately: the reputational damage of an inaccessible show attaches to the artist's name in the fan's memory, not the venue's, so visibility is warranted even though authority is not. |
| Producer | None | Not visible — no box-office surface exists for this persona. Any direct route to this feature returns `404` (feature not exposed), not `403` (which would leak its existence). |
| Operator | Full | Defines the accessible block per room configuration (positions, type, companion entitlement, access route), maintains its statutory release schedule, evidences its handling, and completes the access-information record. Cannot delete a position that has been sold; cannot apply the generic auto-release of 19.01.02 to this block (the control is structurally absent, not merely disabled). |
| Fan | Full | The only persona that transacts on it. Buys an accessible position and its companion ticket through the ordinary flow, at the ordinary price, at the ordinary time, states access requirements in free text, and receives the room's access information (step-free route, platform location, accessible facilities) with the ticket — without a phone call, an enquiry form, or a proof-of-disability check. A Fan who also holds Operator rights on the same show (multi-hyphenate) transacts here strictly as a Fan: purchase decrements the manifest and charges money like any other buyer — Operator rights grant no self-comp path into accessible inventory (comps route through 19.02, never through this purchase flow). |

## Behavior

The accessible block is instantiated from the room configuration (domain 16) — a set of **positions**, each with a **type** (`wheelchair-space`, `easy-access-seat`, `viewing-platform`, `transfer-seat`), a **companion entitlement** (default 1 per position, see Q-03), and **access-route metadata** (step-free route, entry point, platform location, accessible-facility locations). It sits inside the manifest and counts against licensed capacity, but is **exempt from the ordinary hold lifecycle in 19.01.02**: it is never released by deadline, and it is never killed for a production requirement without an equivalent replacement position at the same or better sightline.

A **companion ticket** is an entitlement attached to an accessible position, priced at the comparable general tier (face value). The accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are grouped into **one order**, which carries **exactly one per-order fee** — the companion never adds a second per-order booking fee (this is the CX-05 parity resolution, below). Per-ticket fees on the companion equal those on a comparable general ticket at the same face tier — never a premium.

Purchase happens through the ordinary storefront flow. There is no enquiry-only path, no phone fallback, no separate accessible checkout, and no requirement to upload or declare proof of disability — requiring documentation is itself a barrier and, in key jurisdictions, unlawful (see DT-03). Abuse is handled downstream (quantity caps + resale monitoring in domain 24), not by gatekeeping at the point of purchase.

The **access-requirements free-text field** the Fan submits is health-adjacent personal data: it is encrypted at rest, never written to logs, never included in any AI/model payload, and visible only to the Operator's door/access staff for this show (see Cross-Cut Notes → domain 24 / security-first).

### Happy Path

1. Room configuration (domain 16) supplies the accessible block for the chosen configuration: positions, types, companion entitlements, and access-route metadata.
2. Operator confirms positions and companion entitlement per position, and completes the access information for this show (step-free route, entry time, platform location, accessible-facility locations).
3. Block is instantiated into the manifest at a price level **equivalent to comparable general inventory** — the system copies the comparable general tier's face value and rejects any attempt to set it higher (`422 ACCESSIBLE_PRICE_ABOVE_PARITY`).
4. On-sale opens; accessible positions are purchasable in the same flow, at the same time, at the same all-in price, with the same checkout reservation TTL as general inventory — not via a phone number or an enquiry form.
5. Fan selects an accessible position, declares whether a companion is needed (up to the position's entitlement), and states access requirements in free text.
6. System reserves the position and its companion(s) as one order (same TTL as general checkout), computes the all-in total once (face + per-ticket fees + single per-order fee + tax), and takes payment.
7. System issues the accessible ticket and its companion ticket(s), attaches the show's access information to the confirmation, and delivers it in the same channels as any other ticket.
8. At the door (19.04), the accessible ticket routes to the accessible entrance and the companion(s) scan alongside it — the companion cannot scan independently at a different lane or a different time.

### Edge Cases / Failure Modes

| Scenario | What Happens | User Sees |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Two Fans attempt the last accessible position simultaneously (concurrency) | Exactly one purchase succeeds; the position is never oversold — no oversell of a statutorily-scarce unit is tolerable, so first commit wins and the other is rejected before any charge | Loser sees `409 CONFLICT` with `code: POSITION_TAKEN` and a message routing them to the venue and to any waitlist; their card is not charged |
| Fan loses connection after reserving but before paying (network failure) | Reservation holds for the same TTL as general checkout (19.01.02), then the position returns to the accessible pool automatically — the TTL is **no shorter** than general (a shorter timer is itself a barrier, DT-05) | On reconnect within TTL: cart intact. After TTL: `410 RESERVATION_EXPIRED`, position offered again if still free |
| Room configuration has no accessible block defined and on-sale is attempted (empty) | On-sale is blocked; a configuration with no accessible provision must not reach sale without an explicit, evidenced exemption reason (values decision, Q-04) | Operator sees `422 ACCESSIBLE_BLOCK_MISSING` with a blocking prompt requiring either an accessible block or a written, logged exemption |
| Musician attempts to edit/release the accessible block (permission) | Write rejected; read-only is enforced server-side, not merely hidden in UI | `403 FORBIDDEN`, `code: ROLE_READ_ONLY` |
| Companion ticket would carry a second per-order fee (parity breach, CX-05) | The companion shares the order's single per-order fee; if a fee configuration would compute a second per-order fee for the companion line, it is rejected at fee-config save — a fan never reaches this at checkout | Operator sees `422 ACCESSIBLE_FEE_PARITY_BREACH` with `{ code, tier, feeName, delta }` naming the duplicated fee and the surcharge amount |
| Fan submits a script/markup payload in the access-requirements free-text field (malicious input) | Field is length-capped at 1,000 characters (input beyond the cap is rejected at submit), stored as plain text, escaped on every render, never interpolated into a query or an email template; no HTML/JS executes | Purchase proceeds normally; door staff see the sanitised text |
| Non-disabled buyer bulk-buys accessible positions to resell (malicious intent) | No proof is demanded (DT-03); instead a per-account quantity cap (1 accessible position + its entitled companions per order, configurable) applies, and any resale of an accessible ticket is flagged to domain 24 | Buyer exceeding the cap sees `409 QUANTITY_CAP_EXCEEDED`; resale flagging is invisible to the buyer |
| Access info incomplete at on-sale — route known, accessible-facilities unknown (partial data) | Block instantiates but sits in `Draft`; on-sale is permitted only once **route + entry point** are present, with facilities allowed to fill in later; known limitations are disclosed at selection | Operator sees required-field prompts; Fan sees the access info that exists plus an explicit "facilities information to follow" note — never a silent gap |
| Show near sell-out, general sold, accessible positions unsold, and a disabled Fan is mid-checkout on the last accessible position (race vs conditional release) | The mid-checkout reservation wins; the conditional-release count = unsold **and** unreserved positions only. Release never revokes a live reservation | Operator's release flow shows the releasable count net of active reservations; the Fan's purchase completes uninterrupted |
| Accessible positions released early to general sale before the statutory condition is met | Blocked — likely unlawful; a disabled fan would then be unable to buy at all. The generic auto-release of 19.01.02 is structurally excluded from this block | Operator cannot reach this action; only the conditional-release flow (with the jurisdiction test stated) is offered |
| Production kills the viewing platform for a camera position | Capacity reduction that would land entirely on disabled fans; blocked without an equivalent replacement position at the same or better sightline | Operator sees a hard block escalating the conflict to production (18); the kill cannot complete against an accessible position unilaterally |
| Fan's access requirement cannot be met by the room (no step-free route to the bar) | Must be disclosed before purchase, not discovered on the night | Access information shows known limitations plainly at the selection step, above the buy button |
| GA standing room with no seat map | Accessible provision still required — viewing platform, wheelchair positions, companion access. The block exists independently of the seating model (R-03) | Fan sees accessible positions in the GA quantity-flow; the block is not a subtype of reserved seating |
| Operator who is also a Fan buys an accessible ticket to their own show (multi-hyphenate, two roles) | Transacts as Fan: manifest decrements, payment is taken, no Operator self-comp path exists here (comps are 19.02) | Ordinary purchase confirmation; the Operator dashboard reflects one sold accessible position |
| Musician who also operates the venue (Musician + Operator on one show) | Grants are unioned — Full (Operator) supersedes Read-only (Musician); they configure and release as Operator, and the read-only artist view is subsumed | Full Operator controls; no contradictory dual view |
| Fan requests more companions than the position's entitlement | Rejected at selection; entitlement default is 1 per position (Q-03); additional attendees buy general adjacent inventory | `422 COMPANION_ENTITLEMENT_EXCEEDED` naming the entitlement; a link to comparable adjacent general seats |

### States

| State | Trigger | What User Sees |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Loading | Fan opens the accessible section / availability is being fetched | Skeleton of the position list; no spinner-only dead time — position count and types render before live availability resolves |
| Empty | Room configuration has no accessible block defined | Operator sees a blocking `ACCESSIBLE_BLOCK_MISSING` prompt (a config with no provision cannot reach on-sale without a logged exemption); Fan never reaches an empty accessible section because on-sale is gated |
| Draft | Block instantiated, access info incomplete | Operator sees required-field prompts for route, entry point and facilities; on-sale gated until route + entry point exist |
| Live | On sale, positions available | Fan sees accessible positions in the standard flow with access info and any known limitations at selection |
| Populated (Fan) | Fan has selected a position and companion(s) | One-order cart showing the accessible position + companion(s), single per-order fee, all-in total identical in basis to a comparable general order |
| Partial | Route present, facilities pending | Fan sees available access info plus an explicit "facilities information to follow" marker — not a silent omission |
| Sold out (accessible) | Accessible block exhausted while general inventory remains | Fan sees a plain statement, a waitlist option, and a route to the venue — never a dead end, and never a redirect into buying non-accessible inventory they cannot use |
| Conditional release | Show near sell-out, accessible positions unsold, jurisdiction test met | Operator sees the conditional-release flow with the statutory test stated and the releasable count shown net of active reservations |
| Error | Concurrency loss, expired reservation, permission or parity violation | The specific coded error from the Edge Cases table with a recovery route; card never charged on a failed reservation |

## Deep Think Annotations

| # | Hypothesis | Source | Outcome |
|---|-----------|--------|---------|
| DT-01 | Accessible inventory can reuse the hold mechanism from 19.01.02 with a flag | Modelling economy — it looks like a held block | ❌ REJECTED — and the rejection is the reason this feature exists separately. 19.01.02's flagship design call is *deadline-by-default with automatic release* (its DT-01), which is exactly the behaviour that must never apply here: auto-releasing accessible positions to general sale on a date is the precise unlawful act. Reusing the mechanism means the safety feature of one block is the compliance breach of another. The auto-release control is therefore structurally absent from this block, not merely disabled. |
| DT-02 | The duty is about *seats*, so GA rooms are out of scope | Naive reading of "accessible seating" | ❌ REJECTED — accessible provision in standing rooms (viewing platforms, wheelchair positions, companion access) is required and is the *harder* case, because there is no seat map to hang the inventory on. This is why accessible provision is modelled independently of the seating model (19.01.04) rather than as a subtype of reserved seating (R-03). |
| DT-03 | Requiring proof of disability at purchase prevents non-disabled buyers from taking scarce accessible inventory | Fraud-prevention instinct — the inventory is scarce and statutorily protected | ❌ REJECTED — demanding documentation at the point of sale is itself an access barrier and is unlawful in key jurisdictions (UK Equality Act guidance and multiple ADA-covered venues prohibit proof-of-disability gates). It also fails: it deters legitimate disabled buyers far more than determined resellers. Abuse is instead handled *downstream* — a per-account quantity cap at purchase (1 position + entitled companions per order, configurable) plus resale monitoring in domain 24 — never by gatekeeping the purchase itself. This is textually distinct from DT-01/DT-02: it concerns the *access-to-purchase* process, not the inventory lifecycle. |
| DT-04 | A companion ticket is simply a second ticket in the order, so ordinary per-ticket and per-order fees apply to it | Fee-engine symmetry — every ticket line normally carries the standard fees | ❌ REJECTED — a per-order fee charged a second time for the companion is a de-facto accessibility surcharge and a parity breach (CX-05). Resolution: the accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are one order carrying exactly one per-order fee; per-ticket fees on the companion equal those on a comparable general ticket. The invariant is enforced at fee-config save (`422 ACCESSIBLE_FEE_PARITY_BREACH`), so it is structurally impossible for a fan to meet it at checkout. This resolves the sub-domain's only outstanding CX-05 gap. |
| DT-05 | Small process differences for accessible purchase (a slightly shorter checkout timer, one extra confirmation step, a phone fallback) are acceptable so long as the price matches | Assumes parity is only about money | ❌ REJECTED — parity extends to the *process*, not merely the price. A shorter reservation TTL, an extra step, or a phone-only fallback each impose asymmetric friction on disabled buyers and reproduce the status-quo failure this feature exists to remove. The accessible checkout reservation TTL is therefore identical to general checkout, and the flow has the same number of steps. |
| DT-06 | On-sale should be hard-blocked whenever no accessible block is defined, with no override | Strict-compliance instinct — never let an inaccessible show sell | ❌ REJECTED in favour of block-with-evidenced-override — a genuine DIY basement venue with no step-free access must still be able to sell tickets, provided the limitation is disclosed and the absence is logged with a reason, rather than being locked out of the platform entirely. This is a values decision with a real cost to small venues; the *default* is a block, the *escape hatch* is an audited exemption. The values call routes to Q-04. |

## Cross-Cut Notes

- Touches **16 Venues, Studios & Spaces** ([path](../../16-venues-studios-spaces/)) — the accessible block, its position types, access routes and facility locations are room-configuration assets. **Requirement imposed on 16**: accessible provision must be a structured part of the room record (typed positions + route metadata), not free text in a venue description. Without this, this feature has no source data (Q-02). Feeds Step 6 as a hard upstream dependency.
- Touches **19.01.02 Capacity Manifest & Allocations** ([path](./19.01.02-capacity-manifest-allocations-holds.md)) — the accessible block lives inside the manifest and counts against capacity but is exempt from the hold/kill/auto-release lifecycle; the auto-release control is structurally excluded, not flagged off. See `#CX-04`.
- Touches **19.01.03 Fee Structure & All-In** ([path](./19.01.03-fee-structure-all-in-pricing.md)) — parity is broken in the fee layer; the resolution (single per-order fee per accessible order; companion per-ticket fees at general parity; `422 ACCESSIBLE_FEE_PARITY_BREACH` enforced at fee-config save) is the CX-05 close-out. 19.01.03's own edge-case row for this now resolves to the same rule.
- Touches **19.01.04 Seating Model** ([path](./19.01.04-seating-model-ga-reserved-seat-maps.md)) — independent, not hierarchical; accessible provision is required in GA rooms with no seat map at all (R-03). The block is not a subtype of reserved seating.
- Touches **19.04.01 Scan & Validation** ([path](../19.04-door-scanning-access-control/19.04.01-ticket-scan-validation.md)) — accessible tickets route to the accessible entrance; the companion must scan **with** the position holder at the same lane, not independently. Feeds Step 6: door logic needs the companion↔position link.
- Touches **19.02 Comps & Guest Lists** — the only path to a zero-cost accessible ticket is a comp issued in 19.02; the purchase flow here never self-comps, even for a multi-hyphenate Operator buying into their own show.
- Touches **24 Trust, Safety & Disputes** ([path](../../24-trust-safety-disputes/)) — access complaints have a statutory character and must not sit in a generic support queue; resale of accessible tickets is flagged here (the DT-03 abuse channel). Feeds Step 6.
- Touches **security-first / data-protection cross-cut** — the access-requirements free-text field is health-adjacent PII: encrypted at rest, excluded from logs and from any AI payload, escaped on render, visible only to this show's access staff. This is a concrete instance of the platform-wide PII-isolation duty.
- Cross-cut: **Accessibility as a platform duty** — the WCAG/product-accessibility mechanism is a global concern; what lives *here* is the *inventory* half (holding, pricing, releasing accessible positions), distinct from the interface-accessibility half.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Context |
|---|----------|---------|
| D-01 | Accessible inventory is a distinct block, not a flagged hold; the auto-release control is structurally absent | The hold mechanism's auto-release is the exact unlawful behaviour here (DT-01) |
| D-02 | Accessible provision is independent of the seating model | Required in GA standing rooms where no seat map exists (DT-02, R-03) |
| D-03 | Purchase happens through the ordinary channel, at the ordinary time, with identical process friction | Enquiry-only accessible booking is the status-quo failure being removed; the enquiry-only path is deliberately absent (DT-05) |
| D-04 | No proof of disability is demanded at purchase; abuse is handled by per-account quantity caps + downstream resale monitoring (24) | Documentation gates are themselves barriers and unlawful in key jurisdictions (DT-03) |
| D-05 | An accessible position and its entitled companion(s) are one order carrying exactly one per-order fee; companion per-ticket fees equal the comparable general tier; enforced at fee-config save | A duplicated per-order fee is a de-facto accessibility surcharge — parity breach (DT-04, CX-05 resolution) |
| D-06 | An accessible position cannot be killed for production without an equivalent replacement at the same or better sightline, and cannot be released early to general sale outside the conditional flow | A production kill or early release lands entirely on disabled fans |
