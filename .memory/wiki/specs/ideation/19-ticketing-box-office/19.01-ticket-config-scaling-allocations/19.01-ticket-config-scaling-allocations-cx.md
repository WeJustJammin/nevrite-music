## Cross-Cut Details

### CX-01: Types & Scaling ↔ Manifest & Allocations

**Relationship**: Price scaling defines *price levels* (e.g. Front Standing £35 / Balcony £28 / Restricted View £18). The manifest defines *inventory blocks*. The two are joined: every sellable unit carries exactly one price level at any moment. Moving inventory between blocks (releasing an artist hold to public sale) must resolve what price the released inventory sells at — the hold's price, or the current public tier if timed tiers have advanced.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: sets both; is the only role that can move inventory across price levels after on-sale.
- **Musician**: sees their allocation and its price level; can request a release but not re-price.
- **Producer**: not affected — no visibility.
- **Fan**: sees only the resulting price, never the block structure.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: The manifest owns the inventory unit; the price level is an attribute of it. Single owner (Operator), so no merge conflict — but concurrent edits by Operator staff during a live on-sale are a real race. `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`
2. **Trigger chain**: Releasing a hold triggers a re-price decision. If the re-price fails or is ambiguous, the release must not complete — inventory going on sale at an undefined price is a money incident, not a UI glitch.
3. **Permission intersection**: Holding inventory does not confer pricing rights. A Musician with a 20-ticket artist hold cannot change what those 20 cost.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A price-level change after on-sale must notify existing buyers only if it lowers the price and a price-guarantee policy exists. `[PENDING — policy is a product decision]`
5. **State transition conflict**: Timed tier advance (early-bird → standard) and a hold release can fire simultaneously. Ordering determines the released price. Must be deterministic.

### CX-02: Types & Scaling ↔ Fees & All-In Display

**Relationship**: The Fan-facing price is a derived value: face value from the price level, plus per-ticket fees, plus per-order fees, plus tax. Regulation now requires the total to be the *first* number shown, which inverts the traditional model where face value led and fees appeared at checkout. This means the fee structure is an input to the storefront, not a checkout-time concern.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: configures the fee structure and its split between venue, platform and (where applicable) the act.
- **Fan**: sees only the all-in number, with a breakdown available on demand.
- **Musician**: sees the fee structure on their own show because it determines their settlement; cannot change it unilaterally.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**:
1. **Shared state conflict**: Fee structure is owned by the Operator but its *split* is a term of the booking agreement (domain 17). Divergence between the two is a settlement dispute waiting to happen.
2. **Trigger chain**: Editing a fee after on-sale creates two cohorts of buyers at different totals for the same seat. Sync — must block, not queue.
3. **Permission intersection**: Yes — configuring fees requires settlement-side rights, not merely box-office rights.
4. **Notification fan-out**: A post-on-sale fee increase is likely to require re-consent or be prohibited outright. `[PENDING — legal review at /create-prd-security]`
5. **State transition conflict**: Not a race so much as an ordering guarantee — the displayed total and the charged total must be the same value computed once, not twice.

### CX-03: Seating Model ↔ Manifest & Allocations

**Relationship**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]`. Confirmed at breadth: the seating model changes what an inventory unit *is*. GA = a decrementing counter with a capacity ceiling. Reserved = a set of addressable seat identities with adjacency and sightline attributes. Mixed rooms (seated balcony, standing floor) carry both simultaneously, which is the common case in music venues and the case incumbent tooling handles worst.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: chooses the model per room per show; a room's model can change between shows (seats out for a rock show, seats in for a folk show) — the manifest is per-show, not per-room.
- **Fan**: experiences it as "pick a seat" vs "just buy" — a completely different purchase flow.
- **Musician**: cares because it changes capacity, and capacity drives the guarantee they can ask for.
- **Producer**: not affected.

### CX-04: Accessible Seating ↔ Manifest & Allocations

**Relationship**: Accessible positions are part of capacity and must be counted, but they may not be treated as ordinary held inventory. Holding them back is required; releasing them early to general sale may be unlawful; releasing them late (when the show sells out) is the standard failure that generates complaints. The manifest must model a block whose release rules are statutory rather than commercial.

**Role scoping**:
- **Operator**: must maintain the accessible block and evidence its handling.
- **Fan**: the only persona that transacts on it, and the only one who suffers when it is mishandled.
- **Musician**: read-only visibility — but the reputational damage of an inaccessible show lands on them, not the venue.
- **Producer**: not affected.

**Synthesis questions answered**: `[PENDING — /ideate-discover Step 5 deepening]` — jurisdiction mapping required first.

### CX-05: Accessible Seating ↔ Fees & All-In Display

**Relationship**: Price parity between accessible and comparable general inventory is a legal requirement, and the fee layer is where parity is quietly broken — e.g. a companion ticket carrying a second full booking fee. Recorded at breadth because it is a plausible defect that is invisible unless designed for.

**Role scoping**: **Operator** configures; **Fan** is harmed; **Musician** and **Producer** unaffected.
