## The Wedge

> THE SESSION RITUAL — every session, gig and collaboration you run through WeJammin writes two artifacts you cannot get anywhere else, as a byproduct of work you were doing anyway: a VERIFIED CREDIT, counter-attested in the room by the people who were actually there, and a SIGNED SPLIT SHEET, because the money does not move until it is signed. That coercion is the mechanism: the Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer cross-cut binds escrow release to rights execution, so paying the drummer IS executing the work-for-hire, and approving the mix both pays and vests. It works because it uses the platform's only real leverage (the money) at the only moment anyone is motivated (payday) — which is exactly why every rights-first attempt has failed and why Jaxsta, Muso.AI, Sound Credit and the MLC cannot fix this from the outside: they reconstruct after the fact from whoever remembers, and by then the room is cold and the friends are lawyers.\n\nWhy DAILY: this fires on the working musician's actual job — sessions, gigs, collabs — not on a separate habit the product has to manufacture. Domain 07 (Music Projects) and the DAW Bridge cross-cut mean the capture happens where the work happens, with no export-and-upload tax. Contrast the tempting alternatives: black-box royalty recovery ('we found you £800 you didn't know you had') is the most powerful ACTIVATION event available and should absolutely be the front door — but it is a once-event, not a habit. Gig alerts open the app daily but are trivially replaceable; Facebook groups do it today for free.\n\nWhy HARD TO LEAVE: the artifacts compound into an asset with no export equivalent. Verified credits become your résumé (02), your reputation (Reviews cross-cut), your dep-matching eligibility (04), your warm intros (03), your neighbouring-rights registrations (10), your gear's discography (15) and your A&R signal (22). Splits become your income (10) and your one-stop sync clearances (11) — a byproduct that makes domain 11 scale to the long tail where Musicbed and Songtradr must BUY catalog. Leaving means abandoning a verified professional history and a machine-readable ownership record that no competitor can reconstruct, because they were not in the room. And the honest test of the thesis is the Privacy & Data Portability cross-cut: WeJammin should promise frictionless export loudly, because if the compound value is real nobody leaves anyway — and saying so confidently is the proof. An industry carrying the scar tissue of MySpace, PledgeMusic and Splice Studio will not hand over its life's work on any other terms.\n\nDEPENDENCY (be clear-eyed): this wedge requires domains 02, 07 and 09 plus the Atomic Payment ↔ Rights Transfer and DAW Bridge cross-cuts. Owner decision #2 determines whether it survives. Option 2 there (rights as pure adjacency) forfeits it and leaves a good marketplace behind.

## Coverage Notes

WHAT THIS MAP IS. 24 domains, 32 cross-cuts, 25 not-product entries, ordered by the music-industry value chain: who you are (01-02) → who and what you find (03-04) → work you sell and teach (05-06) → work you make (07-08) → what you own and earn from it (09-12) → what you buy and sell (13-15) → where and when you play (16-19) → how you grow (20-22) → how you run it (23) → how it stays safe (24). Slugs are bare; folder names take the two-digit prefix.\n\nVERIFICATION OUTCOME — READ THIS FIRST. Zero candidates were majority-refuted; the REFUTED list came back empty. All 24 survivors are retained. Three domains drew a 1/3 minority objection (Identity, Real-Time Jamming, Fan & Audience) and all three objections identified REAL duplication that I acted on. Every entry in `demoted` is therefore a boundary strip or a dedup, NOT a domain kill. A later reader should not read `demoted` as 'these lost'.\n\nHOW I HANDLED THE MINORITY OBJECTIONS. I did not overrule any 2/3 majority. Where an objection found objective duplication (a capability claimed by both a domain and a declared cross-cut) I stripped it — that is not a judgment call, it is a contradiction. Where an objection drew a conclusion beyond its evidence I declined and surfaced the disagreement as an owner decision rather than resolving it silently (#4, jamming). The Identity objection produced the largest cleanup: four of six claimed capabilities collided with cross-cuts, and all four are now rehoused. The explicit seam that survives — Identity owns the ENTITY MODEL and membership graph; the Roles cross-cut owns ENFORCEMENT — was already drawn in the cross-cut's own rationale; I only made it load-bearing.\n\nDUPLICATIONS I FOUND THAT NO VERIFIER CAUGHT. (1) Band Name & Trademark sat in both Rights and Identity → resolved to Identity (a trademark is a different IP regime from copyright, and dissolution ownership is decided by the partnership agreement). (2) IPI was listed as a work identifier in Rights → it is Interested PARTY Information and moved to Identity; Rights now issues work/recording identifiers only. (3) Services Marketplace claimed buyer-initiated briefs and blind auditions, duplicating the entire Opportunities workflow → moved to Opportunities; the handoff is that a won Opportunity creates a Services engagement.\n\nTWO CROSS-CUTS EXTRACTED DURING SYNTHESIS, both to resolve duplication rather than to add scope. 'Real-Time Rooms, Presence & Audio Transport' — five domains must invoke the same synchronous pipe, so the pipe is a mechanism and domain 08 is the destination built on it. 'Shipping, Fulfilment & Logistics' — four domains need carrier labels (gear, D2F merch, two-way custodial repair intake, tour merch); extracting it is what lets the merch store stay in Fanbase instead of being wrong-cut into a gear catalog that has no concept of a t-shirt. One cross-cut merged: Privacy + Data Portability both claimed DSAR/erasure. One not-product merged: WAF/scraper into Enterprise Security.\n\nCALLS I MADE RATHER THAN ASKING (per decision-classification: structural, not product). Beat licensing stays in Digital Goods (bought in a store with a player and a cart) rather than Music Licensing (it is a licence with stream caps, exclusivity and retained publishing that lands in the split sheet). It is genuinely both; the line used is 'Licensing = a third party using finished music; Digital Goods = creative raw materials sold to makers'. BeatStars has paid out $200M+, so revisit if it underperforms. Digital music sales to fans stay in Fanbase and reuse Digital Goods' download/library delivery — split by destination per the map's own principle, with the shared delivery layer an acknowledged dependency, not a duplicated build. ### 01 — Identity, Profiles & Organizations

- **Slug**: `identity-profiles-organizations`
- **Novelty**: `industry-standard` | **Priority**: `core` | **Sub-domains**: 12
- **Personas**: working musician (multi-hyphenate), band, manager, agent, studio owner, venue operator, label, dealer/shop

**Summary**: Who exists on the platform: humans holding several simultaneous professional roles, and bands/labels/studios/venues/agencies as first-class entities that book, sign, own and get paid independently of their members — plus the membership and representation graph, the profile/EPK surface, and the claim-and-verify flow that turns a seeded record into an owned one.

**Why top-level**: Survives the skeptic on three grounds. (1) It is a destination, not a surface: the profile/EPK is the most-visited page type on any music platform and the artifact hiring decisions are made from; claiming a venue page is a funnel with its own conversion rate; band governance is somewhere you go. (2) It is product ontology, not mechanism. Music's defining structural fact is the multi-hyphenate (a drummer who mixes, sits in three bands, engineers at a studio and sells a cymbal) and the band-as-entity that signs and is paid independently of any member. Every platform that modelled 'user has a profile' — early SoundCloud, Bandcamp, every LinkedIn-for-music — could never represent a band or a manager acting for an artist. (3) Boundary narrowed in direct response to a verifier who argued this is a cross-cut. That objection correctly identified real duplication, and I acted on all of it: KYC/KYB moved to Payments, age assurance to Safeguarding, entity merge to Canonical Data, account security split to architecture and Trust. The seam that remains is explicit — this domain owns the ENTITY MODEL and the membership/representation graph (who is a member of what, with what mandate); the Roles & Permissions cross-cut owns ENFORCEMENT at every call site. That line was already drawn in the cross-cut's own rationale. What survives extraction is ontology, and ontology is the most expensive thing on this map to retrofit.

### 08 — Real-Time Jamming & Remote Sessions

- **Slug**: `realtime-jamming-remote-sessions`
- **Novelty**: `industry-standard` | **Priority**: `important` | **Sub-domains**: 6
- **Personas**: musician, band, remote session player, mix engineer, remote client/producer, teacher

**Summary**: Synchronous music over the network — low-latency jam rooms with geographic peer matching, high-fidelity remote session monitoring and talkback, and multitrack capture of what happened.

**Why top-level**: Renamed to drop the 'Collaboration' collision with domain 07, and narrowed hard in response to the strongest objection in the verification set. That objection's invocation test is correct — Education's lesson room, Services' remote session, Projects' live mix attendance and Community's jam spaces all call the same pipe — so I extracted the pipe as the 'Real-Time Rooms, Presence & Audio Transport' cross-cut and stripped the borrowed children (presence/multiplayer to the cross-cut; listening rooms split to Community and Fanbase). But the objection over-reached in concluding the destination does not exist, and it defeats itself with its own analogy: Stripe is standalone and Payments is a cross-cut here, yet Gear Marketplace is still a domain. A cross-cut pipe does not preclude a destination built on it. What survives extraction has interacting capabilities and is unowned by any sibling: the 25-30ms desync ceiling caps the playable radius at a few hundred miles, which turns latency budgeting into a matching feature ('show me who I can actually play with') that is a product decision, not infrastructure; and high-fidelity remote monitoring is a proven paid category with the inverse trade-off — loose latency, uncompromising audio quality — where Zoom's codec destroys exactly what is being judged (Audiomovers Listento, acquired by Abbey Road/Universal; Source-Connect is the broadcast standard). ## Owner Decisions — REQUIRED before seeding

### Decision 1: Physical gear, digital goods and human services — three top-level marketplace domains, or one 'Marketplace' domain with three heavy sub-domains?

**Options**:

- THREE DOMAINS (as mapped: 13 Gear, 14 Digital Goods, 05 Services). Each owns its own inventory model, fulfilment physics and returns policy. Shared cart/payments/search/reviews/tax/shipping stay as cross-cuts.
- ONE 'Marketplace' DOMAIN with Gear / Digital / Services as heavy sub-domains. Preserves the buyer's single 'shop' mental model and one vendor-onboarding surface.
- TWO DOMAINS — 'Goods' (gear + digital, sharing a cart and a store surface) and 'Services' (human work, sharing an engagement lifecycle).

**Recommendation**: Option 1 — three domains, as mapped.

**Why**: Everything genuinely shared is ALREADY a cross-cut: cart, payments, messaging, search, reviews, disputes, tax, and now shipping. So the merge would buy you nothing you do not already have. Everything that differs is irreconcilable at the schema level: gear is qty=1 non-fungible inventory with condition as ~40% of price plus freight/CITES/packing; digital has licence keys, machine seats, a format×OS×DAW×host-dependency matrix, piracy and refunds that cannot be un-given (colliding with EU withdrawal law); services are scoped human output with briefs, revision economics, taste disputes and the question of who owns the resulting master. Collapsing them yields a 'listing' entity with forty nullable fields, a 'fulfilment' sub-domain meaning three unrelated things, and a returns policy that cannot express any of the three. Reverb, Splice and SoundBetter are three separate businesses that share only a checkout button. The strongest counter is real and I want you to see it: the buyer's mental model IS 'shop', and three vendor-onboarding surfaces is friction. But that is a navigation and IA problem — solvable with a unified store front-end over three domains — whereas a merged schema is not solvable at all once built. Option 3 is the weakest: it merges the two that share the least below the cart while splitting nothing that needed splitting.

### Decision 2: Rights & Ownership, Royalties & Collections, Music Licensing and Release & Distribution are four domains and ~70 sub-domains that NOTHING you said asks for. Your directives (D-05..D-08) name a marketplace, digital goods, a directory and event management. Is the rights stack the thesis, or an adjacency?

**Options**:

- THESIS — all four are `core`. WeJammin's differentiator (split-at-creation, payment↔rights atomicity, black-box recovery) only exists if it holds the ownership registry. Accept a regulated, society-integrated, multi-year build with counterparties that move at their own speed.
- ADJACENCY — mark all four `important`. Ship the directed scope (marketplace + directory + events + services) first; add rights once liquidity exists.
- PARTIAL — keep Rights & Ownership `core` (split capture at creation is cheap and compounds), mark Royalties / Licensing / Distribution `important`. Capture the ownership data now; build or partner the collection machinery later.

**Recommendation**: Option 3 — partial. Capture rights now, collect later.

**Why**: This is the single largest inference on the map and I want it owned by you rather than smuggled in by me — I have currently marked Rights & Ownership `core` on inference alone. The asymmetry that decides it: split-at-creation is the map's only genuinely unrepeatable advantage. You can only capture the split sheet in the room, on the day, while everyone is friendly and the facts are fresh — that data cannot be reconstructed later at any price, which is precisely why Jaxsta, Muso.AI and the MLC all fail at it. It is also cheap: a signed document at a moment you already control. But society registration, CWR exchange, statement ingestion and DDEX conformance are a different company (Songtrust, Curve, the MLC) with regulated counterparties and multi-year integration timelines. Capturing ownership is cheap and appreciates; collecting on it is expensive and can be bought or partnered. Option 3 keeps the wedge and defers the machinery. ### Decision 3: Does WeJammin have a fan-facing consumer surface, or are fans objects in an artist's CRM rather than users of the platform?

**Options**:

- B2B ONLY — fans are records (email addresses, buyers, members). No fan accounts, no fan app. Fanbase & D2F is purely artist-facing tooling; the fan's experience is the artist's microsite and store.

- DEFERRED — build B2B-only now, but model the fan as a first-class entity from day one so the surface can be added later without a migration.
